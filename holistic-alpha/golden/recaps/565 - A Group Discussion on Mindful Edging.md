> **TL;DR** — Mindful edging is a deep dive within yourself that builds self-control, presence and the muscle of intention; shame is released by moving into the practice and stacking evidence, consistency is critical, and starting out takes acceptance, coconut oil and a softer, slower touch.

*From Holistic Alpha episode 565, a conversation between host Steven Mathis and Scott Palmer, Rick and Corey. Lines marked **Guest** are the guests', unmarked lines are the host's. Not fact-checked.*

- From a discussion among a few in the Brotherhood community: referred newcomers who have never listened to the podcast may arrive with no idea of anything practiced here, something already seen and sure to continue, probably a change going forward. A number of guys known personally have no real edging practice, or are early on and maybe hit a challenge or a false start, so each guest (all three with edging practices for a while now, hugely beneficial, "high vibrational, sacred") shares what edging means to him and how the transition went.
- **Guest (Corey):** Personally, edging was, and still is, more about self-control. Never a big fan of porn, it's about reaching deep into himself mentally to see how far he can go; with his life in a pattern, it had to fit in to stay productive. Focusing on not ejaculating instead of going into orgasm "set a whole nother bar", leading to more control elsewhere; the more on his plate, the more self-control and firm mindset he needs.
- Going deeper inside yourself seems a fundamental piece that a lot of guys new to these ideas maybe have no idea of, or find hard to appreciate until they experience it: "a deep dive within yourself in so many ways", physically, emotionally, discipline. Rephrasing something the guest touched on: it "increases your capability as a man", enabling you to be you and show up the way you do (true of that guest, personally, and probably of a number of guys on the call), a huge perspective shift from what guys are used to.
- **Guest story (Scott):** In a long-term marriage, edging within his wife's awareness was a huge hump. After very open discussions she was super cool: "I see you. I hear your heart, go. Like, chase you, go get you, develop you." But he had his own internal shame and conditioning: one morning, set up upstairs for an edging session, he heard her open the door and felt like he was 13, though she knew this is what he does: something to deprogram and unlearn. Now she's not around most of the time, but when she enters the room it's commonplace, like push-ups or yoga.
- **Guest (Scott):** Personally, edging has brought overall balance to all forms of his energy, emotional and physical, and the ability to take sexual energy where it normally feels like you'll lose it, and hold it. One thing he does often: rest back and let the erection soften without any touch. The presence that creates carries through the day: whatever stirs his emotion, "be it a meeting, be it an encounter, be it a text message", he can hold himself "in a strong, steady, enlivened place without losing control."
- That increased solidity, the ability to stand strong in any situation, is "monstrous", and personally it has changed over the years since the twenties. As our sexual energy goes (its control and connection, not just its intensity; as we get deeper, it's such a foundation), our strength of soul and presence basically goes, though not linearly; so when something emotional comes up in a meeting, you can move through it in a more powerful, positive, connected way.
- For guys with no edging practice, or only the old idea of masturbation, edging is a completely different world, but "absolutely every bit as real" as the other one, which every guy on the call has been in to some degree at some point, even if very briefly. "We get to choose the one that we want to live in."
- **Guest (Corey):** Mentally (he doesn't know about the other guys, but is pretty sure one of them goes in this way too), going in not going for ejaculation is a real help: without that mental block set up going in, the brain triggers the part of you that ejaculates [?], and it's gone; going in set on this being an edging practice carries pretty much the rest [?].
- The bigger picture is the ability to set an intention and follow through, personally one of the fundamental things edging brought. "It is a muscle": every edging session, even with a subtle, unspoken intention, practices it, and it "translates into every other intention in every other place of life."
- **Guest story (Rick):** Personally, edging healed past shame. In porn, he could ejaculate four or five times a day, draining himself, a slave to it, sneaking into the bathroom at work, driven by a force in his head, with incredible shame and knowing it wasn't good for him; without porn, he still ejaculated excessively, because he had trained himself: you can take porn out of the mix and still ejaculate multiple times a day. On finding the podcast, edging seemed foreign, even ridiculous, since getting there was the goal every time. Talking with the host, he saw how much shame he had internalized about sexuality and masturbation: learning to love himself and his cock was a foreign concept, something he enjoyed but wasn't supposed to.
- **Guest (Rick):** Edging let him feel through that, put aside the shame and the trauma, and accept that finding pleasure with himself was okay. Once you experience edging, or even masturbation, without shame, the shift in your mind "can start adding to you"; when it's done with shame, the shame takes away its power and "negates every beautiful thing", and "the shame takes it all away when you're done."
- **Guest story (Rick):** He started on the host's advice, "Get a five-minute timer, do that, be done", since that was what he had to start with, and worked his way up. Now edging is part of his routine and of who he is ("I don't wanna say it's brushing teeth"), a way to stoke that fire, be okay and happy with it, and still enjoy himself without constantly, excessively ejaculating; it still happens from time to time, but not in the old quantity.
- **Guest (Rick):** A lot of guys in semen retention are confused into thinking holding on as long as possible is the goal, so ejaculating means failing. Personally, the philosophy shifted to "hold on and if it happens, it does"; if it doesn't, you're moving forward, you've gained a lot, you're expanding yourself. The transition from shame is a major one, among other dynamics.
- Shame has come up a lot, with at least one, if not a couple, recent posts. An important part of what the guest said is that truly moving through it is "an active process": we let it go by taking actions that rewire and reprogram the emotional response. Hearing that edging is sacred might release a little, but ultimately we release shame by diving into the practice, grabbing on to something else: the more we move into the sacred, the more we let go.
- We also need evidence, experienced personally early on and heard from a lot of clients: the brain really benefits from documented, experiential evidence in your own life of why you shouldn't be ashamed, like seeing, at the end of a day that started with edging, that you were a better dad, business owner, employee or spouse, physically better and more effective. Stack it, and shame is no longer the truth: "this is not making me worse. This is actually making me much better as a man."
- A number of guys have mentioned, and a few recent posts show, that starting out they go three, four or five days without anything, to retain, edge a day or two, release, and repeat. Personally, it seems really important to speak to how critical consistency is: it is what allows us to develop discipline and deepen connection; a couple of times a week won't do it. [speaker unclear] A favorite word: momentum, which you have to build in your edging practice to push through the transitional states.
- **Guest:** [speaker unclear] A player who misses a shot, skips practice all week and expects to make it [?] at next week's game misses what practice does: it brings hits and misses, but more hits. Starting out takes a while and some discipline.
- Some experiences discussed here are generally not available in the first week, so we need appropriate expectations and acceptance: early on you might bump up against shame more, have a really hard time controlling it, or be disappointed at losing an intention to maintain discipline, and it's okay. A favorite thing to tell the kids: "we've got to be willing to suck at stuff", the only way to get good; not that everybody will suck per se, but we need to be willing to endure some initial discomfort to reach the beautiful part.
- **Guest advice (Rick):** Basics a lot of guys don't think about: first, lube, like coconut oil; second, get away from the desperate "monkey death grip". When starting an edging practice, it needs to be slow and much lighter; so many guys are used to getting it done fast with a death grip that retraining takes time and attention to some of the nuances.
- Softer, slower: two good words to keep in mind. It seems all on the call agree "coconut oil is the gold standard", though some guys, a couple already seen, say they don't need lube. **Host story:** after years of edging (not as long as one of the guests), control with no lube would not be nearly the same: better than 20 years ago, but not what it is with coconut oil. Don't underestimate the importance of some of the nuances of setting yourself up for success.
