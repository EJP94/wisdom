# Topic instructions v2 (draft, not adopted)

A **topic note** gathers what Holistic Alpha says about one subject, across every episode. It is built only from Recaps. It merges what episodes repeat. **It never drops an idea.** Reading a complete note, nobody should think the podcast said something about its subject that the note leaves out.

These instructions are the only rule for topic notes. A Recap still follows `recap-instructions.md`.

This is a draft for the two-step pilot of 2026-10-30. Version 1 stays the rule on `main` until Esteban adopts this one.

## Changes in v2

v1 on 24 Recaps gave 0 merges in 419 bullets, and its cost per Recap grew with the notes (1.5, 1.9, 2.3 cu). v2 works in two steps:
- **Step A, per Recap:** split every bullet into claims and save them in a registry, one row per claim. A never reads a note, so its cost stays flat
- **Step B, per topic:** build the note from all the topic's claims at once. **B writes no line text.** It writes a layout: sections, the In short and, per line, the ids of its claims. A script renders the note, where each line's text is word for word one of its claims, and checks it
- The registry replaces `applied.csv`, the per-bullet logs and the parked lists
- A proposes subjects that `topics.csv` lacks. The script counts them, and they are born at 3 episodes
- Each claim carries its explicitness, so a piece can pick lines by level

## 1. Files

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `holistic-alpha/topics.csv` | The closed list of topics: `slug`, `area`, `name`, `status` (`topic` or `parked`), `explicitness`, `min_hits`, `keywords`. Step A never edits it |
| `holistic-alpha/claims/<id>.jsonl` | **The registry.** One file per Recap, one row per claim, in Recap order. `<id>` is the episode id of `episodes.csv` |
| `holistic-alpha/claims/_proposed.csv` | Subjects proposed by step A and not yet in `topics.csv`, with their episodes. Generated by `tools/claims.py`. Never edit it by hand |
| `holistic-alpha/topics/<area>/<slug>.layout` | Step B's layout of a note |
| `holistic-alpha/topics/<area>/<slug>.md` | The note, rendered by `tools/topic_note.py` from the layout and the registry. Never edit it by hand |
| `holistic-alpha/logs/claims/findings/<run>.jsonl` | Review findings of step A |
| `holistic-alpha/logs/topics/findings/<run>.jsonl` | Review findings of step B |
| `holistic-alpha/logs/runs/<date>-<run>.md` | The report of each run: cost, findings, results |

The six areas are `mind`, `sex-energy`, `hormones`, `training`, `fuel` and `relationships`. Only Esteban adds, removes or renames an area.

## 2. Three levels

- **Area**: six, fixed. They only group topics. An area has no note of its own
- **Topic**: one note per subject
- **Claim**: one statement, one row in the registry, one line in its topic's note, with the episodes where it appears. A claim never gets a note of its own

## 3. Step A: a Recap becomes claims

### 3.1 What gets split

Every bullet line of the Recap, nested ones included: a line that starts with `- ` after any indent. The TL;DR and the italic line under it are not split: they repeat the bullets.

Every bullet line gives at least one claim. Nothing is dropped: a bullet about the podcast itself (why it exists, what a future episode covers, a plug) gives a claim with topic `none`.

### 3.2 What a claim is

- **One statement**: one fact, view, instruction, reason, number with its subject, or example. A bullet often holds two or three
- **It reads alone.** Step B never adds words, so a claim carries the few words it needs from its bullet, its parent bullet or the bullet before: the subject ("For testosterone, …"), what "it" is. No "this", "it" or "that" pointing outside the claim
- **The Recap's words**: every hedge, number, specific, cited source and quote, exactly. Never stronger, never wider. A claim keeps its reason when the reason makes no sense alone
- **These stay whole, as one claim:** a story (**Story:**, **Host story:**, **Guest story:**, **Listener story:**) told in one bullet, a **Quote:**, a **Listener question:**, a **Guest question:**. A story told over several bullet lines gives one claim per line, with the same topic and marker. A **Guided practice:** gives one claim per step
- **English**, like the Recap

### 3.3 The row

Step A writes one JSON object per line, in Recap order, with these fields:

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| `line` | The number of the Recap line the claim comes from, counted from 1 in the file, frontmatter included |
| `topic` | The slug of the topic the claim is mainly about, from `topics.csv` (`topic` or `parked`). `+<slug>` for a proposed subject (3.4). `none` for the podcast itself |
| `also` | Other slugs the claim is also about, often `[]`. The script lists the claim in those notes as a pointer |
| `section` | A suggested section for the note, as a short question or label: "How to practice", "Why it matters", "What goes wrong" |
| `marker` | The Recap marker that governs the claim's sentence, without bold and colon: `Story`, `Host story`, `Guest`, `Guest (Rick)`, `Guest story`, `Guest advice`, `Guest question`, `Listener question`, `Listener story`, `Quote`, `Guided practice`. Empty for the host's view. A marker can open a sentence inside a bullet: each claim takes the one that governs its own sentence |
| `level` | `general`, `sexual-health` or `explicit` (section 9) |
| `claim` | The text |
| `name`, `area` | Only on rows with a `+<slug>` topic: a short English name and one of the six areas |

Then `python3 -I tools/claims.py fill <id>` adds `id` (`<episode>-<n>`, such as `018-07`), `ep` and `date`. `fill` never changes an id it already gave. A row added later gets the next free number.

```json
{"line": 23, "topic": "kegels", "also": ["edging"], "section": "How to practice", "marker": "", "level": "sexual-health", "claim": "Do at least as many reverse Kegels as Kegels", "id": "018-07", "ep": "018", "date": "2019-10-29"}
```

### 3.4 A subject not in topics.csv

1. Use a topic of `topics.csv` whenever one fits, active or parked
2. If none fits, propose one: topic `+<slug>`, with `name` (short, English) and `area` (one of the six). Step A never edits `topics.csv`
3. Before writing, read `claims/_proposed.csv`. If the subject is already proposed, use that slug, name and area. This is why blocks of step A run one after another
4. With fewer than 3 episodes, a proposed subject stays parked in the registry. It has no file of its own
5. When the script counts 3 episodes, the run report lists it, and it enters `topics.csv` as a `topic` in that run's commit, with keywords guessed from its claims
6. A subject that fits no area takes area `?`. The run report asks Esteban

### 3.5 The registry is never rewritten

Once a Recap's claims pass review, its file is final. Step B only reads it. One exception: if the Recap is corrected later, step A runs again for that Recap alone and its file is replaced. Git keeps the old one.

## 4. Step B: a topic becomes a note

### 4.1 Input

`python3 -I tools/topic_note.py claims <slug>` prints every claim of the topic, oldest first: id, date, marker, level, suggested section, text. Step B reads only that, never the Recaps or other notes.

Claims of other topics that name this one in `also` are not B's work. The script lists them at the end of the note as pointers.

### 4.2 The layout

B writes `holistic-alpha/topics/<area>/<slug>.layout`:

```
in short: Kegels squeeze the root muscle and reverse Kegels push it out. Do both, seemingly in equal measure, a few minutes a day.
## How to find them
- 006-04 014-11 018-03
## How to practice
- 006-05 014-12 018-06 027-02 034-09
  - 018-07
  - 014-13
  - later 721-02
- 014-14
misplaced: 027-05
```

- `in short:` is one line of at most 60 words, composed from the claims, keeping their hedges. With `## ` section titles, it is the only text B writes
- `## <title>` opens a section. Sections group lines by question: what it is, why it matters, how to practice, what goes wrong. Choose them from the claims. The suggested sections are hints
- `- <ids>` is one line. The first id gives the line's text. Pick as first the claim whose words read best alone. Every other id on the line makes the same claim, with the same strength and scope (4.3)
- An indent of 2 spaces nests a line under the one above: the same claim with another strength or scope, or a detail of it (a number, a dose, a step, an example)
- `later` before the ids marks a change of view (4.4)
- `misplaced:` lists claims that are not about this topic. The script leaves them out of the note and lists them in its report. They are fixed in the registry, never moved by B
- Every claim of the topic appears exactly once: on a line or in `misplaced`

### 4.3 Merging: the riskiest step

Put two ids on one line only when they make **the same claim, with the same strength and the same scope**.
- Same claim, different strength or scope: a nested line. ✗ merging "Kegels seem to help erections" with "Kegels bring stronger erections"
- A story never merges. It stays its own line, and a story told over several claims stays together, in id order
- Claims with different markers never merge: a guest's or a listener's claim never shares a line with the host's. The script checks it
- Never strengthen a line because more episodes repeat it. Five episodes saying "probably" still say "probably"

### 4.4 Changes of view

When a claim contradicts or changes an older one, nest it under the older line with `later`. The script opens it with **Later view (YEAR):**, the year of its episode. Never drop the older line.

### 4.5 What the script renders

`python3 -I tools/topic_note.py render <slug>` writes the note:
- Frontmatter: `topic`, `title`, `area`, `claims`, `episodes`, `explicitness` (the highest `level` of its claims), `instructions: v2-draft`, `section: topic`
- The In short, the italic line, then the sections
- Each line: the marker in bold when the first claim has one, the first claim's text, the episode ids oldest first, and the claim ids in a comment: `- **Guest:** text (537, 565) <!-- 537-12 565-04 -->`
- `## In other notes` at the end: each claim whose `also` names this topic, with a link to its home note

Then `python3 -I tools/topic_note.py verify <slug>` checks the rendered note against the registry:
- The text of each line, without its marker, ids and comment, equals the text of one of the line's claims. The In short and the section titles are the only exceptions
- Every claim of the topic is on a line or in `misplaced`, once
- Every id on a line exists and belongs to the topic
- The episode ids shown are those of the line's claims
- All claims on a line share one marker
- It counts lines, merges (lines with claims from 2 or more episodes), nested lines and later views

## 5. Order: one topic at a time

1. Find the topic's episodes by keyword: `python3 -I tools/topic_episodes.py --topics holistic-alpha/topics.csv --transcripts holistic-alpha/transcripts/ --episodes holistic-alpha/episodes.csv --recaps holistic-alpha/recaps/ --only <slug> --out holistic-alpha/topic-episodes.csv`
2. Write the Recaps they still lack, with the `/recaps` pipeline. Esteban approves the list and its cost first
3. Run step A on every Recap that has no claims file yet, in blocks of about 8, one block after another
4. Run step B on the topic once all its episodes have claims. Rebuild a note only when new claims arrive or the rules change

Wiring `topic_episodes.py` to the registry (coverage and status from the claims files) comes after the pilot.

## 6. Splitting a note

When a note passes about 150 lines, propose a split in the run report: which sections go to which new topic. Esteban decides.

## 7. Explicitness

Each claim has a `level`. A topic's `explicitness` in `topics.csv` is the highest level of its claims. When a topic's claims reach a higher level, the run report says so and `topics.csv` is raised in that run's commit. Each piece then picks its own level, line by line.

| Level | Claims that |
|---|---|
| general | have no sexual content |
| sexual-health | speak of sexual function, anatomy, sex drive, porn or its effects, in plain words |
| explicit | describe masturbation, edging or sex acts, step by step |

## 8. Hard rules

- Only what the Recaps say. No transcripts, no outside knowledge, no fact-checking. Esteban checks the facts of a piece before he publishes it
- The message, not the messenger: no "Steven says". Markers carry who said what
- Step B never writes a line's text. Nobody edits a note by hand: fix the registry or the rules, then render again
- English
- Wikilinks only to other topic notes

## 9. Process

Opus writes, Sonnet reviews, Opus fixes, all at xhigh effort, as in the Recap pipeline. The pilot also tries Sonnet as the writer of step A.

### 9.1 Step A

**Writer.** For each Recap of the block: read it, write `claims/<id>.jsonl`, run `fill`. Then run `python3 -I tools/claims.py check <ids>` and fix what it reports.

**Reviewer.** Reads each Recap and its claims file. Doesn't edit. Returns findings:

```json
{"id": "014", "ok": false, "findings": [
  {"severity": "S2", "kind": "hedge lost", "line": 23, "claim": "014-07",
   "recap": "Kegels seem to help erections",
   "found": "Kegels help erections",
   "fix": "Kegels seem to help erections"}]}
```

Severity:
- **S1**: a statement of the Recap in no claim. A claim changed or reversed. The wrong speaker (marker). A claim the Recap doesn't hold
- **S2**: a hedge, number, specific or source lost. A claim stronger or wider than the Recap. A claim that doesn't read alone
- **S3**: two claims in one row. The wrong topic or a missed `also`. The wrong level. A proposal that duplicates a topic or another proposal. Format

`kind` is one of: idea lost, claim changed, speaker wrong, line invented, hedge lost, number lost, source lost, claim strengthened, not standalone, not split, wrong topic, wrong level, duplicate proposal, format.

**Fixer.** Same rules as section 15 of the Recap instructions. Edits the claims files, logs every finding with its verdict in `holistic-alpha/logs/claims/findings/<run>.jsonl`, runs `fill` and `check` again.

### 9.2 Step B

**Writer.** Reads the topic's claims, writes the layout, runs `render` and `verify`, and fixes the layout until `verify` passes.

**Reviewer.** Reads the claims and the rendered note. Doesn't edit. Reports:
- **S1**: two different claims merged on one line. A change of view dropped
- **S2**: a merge across different strength or scope. A change of view not marked `later`. An In short stronger than its claims
- **S3**: a missed merge. A wrong section or nesting. A claim that belongs in `misplaced`, or one put there wrongly

`kind` is one of: merge wrong, merge widened, view change hidden, summary strengthened, merge missed, wrong section, wrong nesting, misplaced.

**Fixer.** Edits the layout, runs `render` and `verify`, logs findings in `holistic-alpha/logs/topics/findings/<run>.jsonl`.
