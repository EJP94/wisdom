# Topic instructions v1.1

A **topic note** gathers what Holistic Alpha says about one subject, across every episode. It is built only from Recaps. It merges what episodes repeat. **It never drops an idea.** Reading a complete note, nobody should think the podcast said something about its subject that the note leaves out.

These instructions are the only rule for topic notes. A Recap still follows `recap-instructions.md`.

## Changes in v1.1 (2026-10-30)

A test on 24 Recaps gave 0 merges in 419 bullets. Each line copied a whole bullet with several claims, so a later episode that repeated one of them could not add its id without being credited with the rest.
- A line holds one claim (sections 2 and 6)
- New path **nested** for the same claim with another strength or scope (sections 6 and 7)
- The reviewer reports missed merges and unsplit lines (section 14)
- Written down from the test: a pointer doesn't count toward a birth (section 3), the parked-list format (section 1), the placeholder summary (section 10)

## 1. Files

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `holistic-alpha/topics.csv` | The closed list of topics: `slug`, `area`, `name`, `status` (`topic` or `parked`), `explicitness`, `min_hits`, `keywords` |
| `holistic-alpha/topics/<area>/<slug>.md` | One note per topic |
| `holistic-alpha/topics/<area>/_parked-<area>.md` | The parked subjects of an area: frontmatter (`title`, `area`, `instructions`, `section: parked`), then one `##` heading per subject, with its `name` from `topics.csv` |
| `holistic-alpha/topics/applied.csv` | One row per Recap already applied: `id`, `date`, `instructions` |
| `holistic-alpha/topic-episodes.csv` | Each topic's episodes, found by keyword. Generated by `tools/topic_episodes.py`. Never edit it by hand |
| `holistic-alpha/logs/topics/<run>.jsonl` | Where every Recap bullet went, one line per bullet. The script reads them too |

The six areas are `mind`, `sex-energy`, `hormones`, `training`, `fuel` and `relationships`. Only Esteban adds, removes or renames an area.

## 2. Three levels

- **Area**: six, fixed. They only group topics. An area has no note of its own
- **Topic**: one note per subject
- **Idea**: one claim, written as one line in its topic's note, with the episodes where it appears. An idea never gets a note of its own

Stories, quotes, guest lines and listener questions are not levels. They keep their Recap marker and travel with their line.

## 3. When a topic is born

- A subject becomes a topic when its ideas appear in **3 or more episodes**. Only lines homed under the subject count, not pointers to it. Until then its lines wait under a heading with its name in the area's `_parked-<area>.md`.
- When a parked subject reaches its third episode:
  1. Create its note and move its lines there, unchanged
  2. Delete its heading from the parked list
  3. Change its row in `topics.csv` from `parked` to `topic`
  4. Turn every pointer to it into `[[<slug>]]`
  5. Log the birth and name it in the run report
- The list is closed. Use only the topics in `topics.csv`. A subject not on the list is parked first, with a new `parked` row: its name, its area, keywords guessed from its lines, `min_hits` 3.
- Never create an area. A subject that fits no area is parked in the closest one and named in the run report.

## 4. Order: one topic at a time

1. Run the script for the topic: `python3 -I tools/topic_episodes.py --topics holistic-alpha/topics.csv --transcripts holistic-alpha/transcripts/ --episodes holistic-alpha/episodes.csv --recaps holistic-alpha/recaps/ --applied holistic-alpha/topics/applied.csv --logs holistic-alpha/logs/topics/ --only <slug> --out holistic-alpha/topic-episodes.csv`
2. Write the Recaps its episodes still lack, with the `/recaps` pipeline. Esteban approves the list and its cost first.
3. Apply every Recap not yet in `applied.csv`, oldest first (section 6). A Recap is applied to every note it touches, not only to the run's topic.
4. Stamp the notes: the same command with `--stamp holistic-alpha/topics/` and without `--only`.

A note is `complete` when every episode the script matched for it is applied, and `partial` before that. **Pieces are made only from complete notes.** The script writes `coverage` and `status`.

A topic's episodes come from two sources:
- **The run logs**: every applied episode whose bullets went to the topic. This part is exact
- **Keywords**: a cheap guess for the episodes not applied yet. On 2026-10-29, tested on episodes 001–034, they found 72% of the episodes a full reading found, and 87% of what they found was right. Most misses are episodes where the topic is one passing line. Those lines still arrive when their episode is applied for another topic, and the logs then add the episode to the topic's list

When a run shows misses or false finds, tune the topic's `keywords` or `min_hits` and say so in the run report. A false find costs nothing in the end, since every episode gets its Recap. It only comes earlier.

## 5. Note format

```
---
topic: kegels
title: "Kegels and reverse Kegels"
area: sex-energy
status: partial
coverage: "6/64"
explicitness: sexual-health
summary_at: 6
instructions: v1.1
section: topic
---

> **In short** — <at most 60 words, composed from the note's lines, with their hedges>

*Ideas from Holistic Alpha, as told by host Steven Mathis and, where marked, his guests and listeners. Numbers in parentheses are episodes. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

## How to find them
- Stopping the flow mid-pee is a Kegel. Peeing harder is a reverse Kegel (006, 014, 018, 034)

## How to practice
- Do both Kegels and reverse Kegels (006, 014, 018, 027, 034)
  - Do at least as many reverse Kegels as Kegels (018)
  - Balance both directions, seemingly equally (014)
- Three timings, on both sides: holds of maybe 5–10 seconds, 1-second squeezes, holds of 20–30 seconds or longer (014)
```

The nested lines show section 7 at work: 014 and 018 say how much, each with its own strength, so they don't merge into the parent line.

- `status` and `coverage` come from the script. `summary_at` is the number of applied episodes when the **In short** was last written. `explicitness` copies `topics.csv`.
- Episode ids are the `id` of `episodes.csv`: `"006"`, `"404b1"`, `"especial-2024-12-25"`. Ids in a line go oldest first.
- Sections are `##` headings that group lines by question: what it is, why it matters, how to practice, what goes wrong, and so on. Choose them from the content. Add a section when lines arrive that fit none. Reorganizing existing sections is a split (section 11).
- Inside a section, a new line goes at the end.

## 6. Applying a Recap

First split each bullet, and each nested bullet with its own idea, into its claims. A Recap bullet often holds two or three: a claim, a second claim, a number, an example. Each claim becomes one line, in the Recap's words, with its own hedges, numbers and sources. When a claim leaves its bullet, add only the few words it needs to keep its scope ("For testosterone, …"). A claim keeps its reason when the reason makes no sense alone. These stay whole, as one line: a **Story:**, a **Quote:**, a **Listener question:**, a **Guided practice:** with its nested steps.

Before writing a new line, look for a line with the same claim in the note. When claim, strength and scope match, merge.

Each claim takes one of these paths:

1. **merged**: the same claim is already a line (section 7). Add the episode id to that line
2. **detail**: the same claim, plus a specific the line lacks (a number, a dose, a step, an example). Add the id to the line and the specific as a nested line with its id
3. **nested**: the same claim with another strength or scope (section 7). A nested line under it, with its own words and id
4. **new**: a new claim of an existing topic. A new line in the right section
5. **parked**: no topic fits. A line under its subject in the area's parked list
6. **born**: the line that gives a parked subject its third episode (section 3)
7. **skipped**: only bullets about the podcast itself, such as why it exists or what a future episode will cover

A story told over several bullets stays together, in one home, in its order. Its other topics get pointers.

Log every claim, in order. `bullet` is the bullet's position among all the Recap's bullet lines, nested ones included, from 1. `claim` numbers the claims of that bullet from 1. `pointers` lists the topics that got a pointer to it (section 9):
```json
{"id": "018", "bullet": 2, "claim": 1, "path": "detail", "topic": "kegels", "pointers": [], "line": "Do at least as many reverse Kegels as Kegels"}
```
Then append the Recap's row to `applied.csv`.

If a Recap changes after it was applied, apply only its changed bullets, with path **reapplied**: fix the lines that came from them, or place them as new ideas. Never apply a whole Recap twice.

## 7. Merging: the riskiest step

Merge two lines only when they make **the same claim, with the same strength and the same scope**. A line with two claims can't take a merge, which is why a line holds one claim (section 6).

- Same claim, same strength: add the id
- Same claim, different strength or scope: a nested line with its own words and id, path **nested**. ✗ merging "Kegels seem to help erections" into "Kegels bring stronger erections"
- A **Story:** never merges with advice. It stays its own line, with its episode
- A guest's or a listener's line never merges with the host's
- Keep every hedge of the four kinds in section 4.4 of the Recap instructions
- Never strengthen a line because more episodes repeat it. Five episodes saying "probably" still say "probably"

## 8. Changes of view

When a Recap contradicts or changes a line from another episode, never edit or delete the older line. The newer view goes as a nested line under it, opening with **Later view (YEAR):**, with its id. If the older episode is applied after the newer one, its line goes on top and the newer one becomes the later view. Dates come from `episodes.csv`.

```
- Do Kegels every day (014, 018, 032, 034)
  - **Later view (2024):** <what the later episode says> (721)
```

## 9. One home per idea

An idea lives in one note: the topic it is mainly about. Another note that needs it gets a **pointer**: a short line with the link and the ids, never the content.

```
- Porn lowers dopamine: see [[_parked-sex-energy#Porn]] (006, 016)
```

## 10. The summary

The **In short** is at most 60 words, composed from the note's lines, keeping their hedges. It is the only part of a note that is ever rewritten. Rewrite it when the note has 10 more applied episodes than `summary_at`, or when its status turns `complete`. Then update `summary_at`. A note that starts with only a pointer gets a placeholder ("No lines of its own yet"), rewritten when the note gets its first line of its own.

## 11. Splitting a note

When a note passes about 150 lines, propose a split in the run report: which sections go to which new topic. Esteban decides. A split moves lines and never rewrites them. The 150 is a first guess, to calibrate on the first runs.

## 12. Explicitness

A topic's `explicitness` in `topics.csv` is the most explicit level any of its lines reaches. Each piece then picks its own level.

| Level | Lines that |
|---|---|
| general | have no sexual content |
| sexual-health | speak of sexual function, anatomy, sex drive, porn or its effects, in plain words |
| explicit | describe masturbation, edging or sex acts, step by step |

When a new line needs a higher level, raise it in `topics.csv` and say so in the run report.

## 13. Hard rules

- Only what the Recaps say. No transcripts, no outside knowledge, no fact-checking. Esteban checks the facts of a piece before he publishes it
- Keep every hedge, number, specific, cited source and quote of the Recap bullet. A quote stays exactly as in the Recap
- The message, not the messenger: no "Steven says". His stories keep **Story:**, guests keep **Guest:**, listeners keep **Listener question:** or **Listener story:**
- Never delete a line. Lines move only in a birth, a split or a change of view (section 8). The one deletion: a fixer removes a line the Recaps don't hold
- English
- Wikilinks only to other topic notes and parked lists

## 14. Process

The writer is Opus, the reviewer Sonnet, the fixer Opus, all at xhigh effort, as in the Recap pipeline. The first run measures the cost.

A run goes in blocks of about 8 Recaps, as the Recap pipeline does, so the review stays thorough. Blocks run one after another, never in parallel, because they edit the same notes.

**Writer.** Applies the run's Recaps (section 6), logs every bullet, writes or rewrites the **In short** when due (section 10), runs the script with `--stamp`.

**Reviewer.** Reviews the diff of the notes against the applied Recaps, not against the transcripts. Doesn't edit. Returns findings:
1. Recall: every bullet of each Recap has a log line, and its content is in a note or a parked list
2. Merges: every merged line makes the same claim, with the same strength and scope, as its Recap bullet. And the other way: a new line whose claim is already a line of the note is a missed merge, and a line with two claims was not split
3. Home: each idea is in the topic it is mainly about. Pointers carry no content
4. Changes of view: marked, in date order, nothing deleted
5. Format: frontmatter, ids, markers, the In short

```json
{"id": "014", "ok": false, "findings": [
  {"severity": "S2", "kind": "merge strengthened", "topic": "kegels",
   "recap": "Kegels seem to help erections",
   "note": "Kegels bring stronger erections (006, 014)",
   "fix": "nested line: Kegels seem to help erections (014)"}]}
```

Severity:
- **S1**: an idea lost. A claim changed or reversed. A guest's or listener's line given to the host. A line the Recaps don't hold
- **S2**: a merge that made a line stronger or wider. A hedge, number or source lost. A change of view hidden or deleted
- **S3**: the wrong section or topic. A pointer with content. A missed merge or an unsplit line. Format

`kind` is one of: idea lost, claim changed, speaker wrong, line invented, merge strengthened, merge widened, hedge lost, number lost, source lost, view change hidden, wrong home, pointer content, merge missed, line not split, format.

**Fixer.** Same rules as section 15 of the Recap instructions. Logs every finding in `holistic-alpha/logs/topics/findings/<run>.jsonl`.
