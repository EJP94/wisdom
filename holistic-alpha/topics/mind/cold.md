## Physical effects

- The first way cold showers help is physical: cold exposure is "absolutely proven now" to up your metabolism (090) <!-- 090-06 -->
  - If you're looking to drop weight or get leaner, cold showers will lead to an increase in metabolism for sure (090) <!-- 090-11 -->
  - Cold showers would probably be worth it just for the increase in metabolism (090) <!-- 090-12 -->
  - The result of cold kicking thermogenesis into gear is a big metabolic boost that lasts a significant period of time, so you burn more fat throughout the day (090) <!-- 090-10 -->
  - One study of cold exposure used longer exposure at not as cold temperatures, an hour (you don't need to do an hour): 68 degree Fahrenheit water, just barely cool really, gave a 93% increase in metabolic rate; 57 degree water, a 350% increase (090) <!-- 090-13 -->
- The body basically produces heat through thermogenesis, shivering and non-shivering (090) <!-- 090-07 -->
  - Cold kicks both shivering and non-shivering thermogenesis into gear, shivering only initially: once you adapt a little, shivering goes away and non-shivering thermogenesis takes over (090) <!-- 090-08 -->
    - Non-shivering thermogenesis is basically the process of brown adipose tissue, brown fat, a special tissue really, really good at turning food into energy, specifically heat (090) <!-- 090-09 -->
- Every time you expose yourself to cold, your body responds by building brown fat (140 · also 090, 289) <!-- 140-36 -->
  - Every time you take a cold shower or expose yourself to cold for any decent period, you build brown fat (090) <!-- 090-49 -->
  - Cold exposure, a cold shower or going out in the cold, makes your body respond in a number of ways, one being that it builds your brown fat (140) <!-- 140-31 -->
  - The norepinephrine cold triggers also triggers an increase in brown fat, the tissue behind non-shivering thermogenesis that keeps us warmer (090) <!-- 090-46 -->
  - In one study, people exposed to cold air at 60 degrees, not that cold, six hours a day for 10 days, had on average a 37% increase in brown fat (090) <!-- 090-50 -->
  - Every little bit of cold trains the cold engine, brown fat: a 30-second or 10-minute cold shower, an ice bath, shoveling your driveway in a t-shirt, walking from the car to work in a short-sleeve shirt (289) <!-- 289-35 -->
