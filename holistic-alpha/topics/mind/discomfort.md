> **In short** — All growth lies on the other side of hard things; a major update to the you that you are only comes through difficulty. One of the best ways through is hard situations in physical training, which transfers to the rest of your life. The suggestion: get uncomfortable, decide it's okay and keep going, without ignoring your body's signs.

*Ideas from Holistic Alpha, as told by host Steven Mathis and, where marked, his guests and listeners. Numbers in parentheses are episodes. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

## Hard is how you grow
- There is no easy button for life: so much of it boils down to our willingness and ability to do hard shit, the only way to grow and progress meaningfully. "Hard is just another word for new", and all growth lies on the other side of hard things, some more challenging than others (010)
- If we're willing, the most power can be found through choosing intentional discomfort: decide it's okay, your friend, your power, because it is. All our power lies in the hard shit in our life. It's cliche, but a lot of cliches are true: all our strength lies in our hardest times (010)
- **Story:** one of the greatest periods of growth came when his son, about a week and a half old, had open heart surgery, then three months in the ICU and a year or so of recovery. Watching him fight and almost lose, as a kid really, was by a wide margin the hardest experience to that point, still top two or three, and the time of the very most growth (010)
- Life as software versions: like iOS or Android, the dot versions, one point two, one point six, 13.2, 13.4, are usually minor security fixes; the big changes come with the next whole version, six to seven. Habits and intention make incremental gains, but a major update to the you that you are only comes through difficulty, "the reality of life". Recognizing that difficulty is our friend, you can make those things happen and push the minor updates along faster. Do hard stuff, your own way (010)

## Practice it in physical training
- One of the best things we can do is intentionally practice hard things, not just where we kind of have to: a business, job or managing-people challenge probably takes hard stuff to grow through, and one of the best ways through is hard situations in physical training (010)
- Physical training transfers to the rest of your life: train to discomfort, readjust and keep going until it sinks in that you can exist there; practiced regularly, it translates. It's facing resistance "in an arena of your choosing at a time of your choosing", to show him who's the boss, so tomorrow, at your job or business, resistance is still there, but you're a little more powerful against him, and he comes in feeling a little worse about himself because you kicked his ass in the physical arena (010)
- For men, physical training is about so much more than looks: mind, emotions, success, drive, hormones, relationships, everything is affected, negatively by inactivity or extremely positively. It helps the best man we all want to be bring what lives inside into the physical world, body change or not: the harder the thing, the more you can benefit and the stronger you can be when you face it (010)
- Fun in your workout may mean you're not on the right path, until, the awesome thing, you have fun being uncomfortable, once you really do that a lot and get into that mindset. **Story:** some climbing workouts were just climbing to tunes. That, or a run where you feel awesome, differs a lot from pushing until really uncomfortable, then deciding to keep going (010)
- Try to break yourself: if an extreme enough physical effort has never kind of broken you down mentally and emotionally, you haven't pushed yourself. Push hard enough and emotional stuff will all of a sudden come up, a part of you that you're not facing (010)
- Strong advice: decide before some workouts that your only goal is to push until discomfort. That's when the workout starts, everything before is warming up, and every minute there pays huge dividends, in body and life. A promise: spend time there and you'll feel the benefits, if you're willing to put in the work (010)
- Just for today, forget the future: you can only live today. Find discomfort in the physical arena today, maybe bodyweight squats, a plank as long as you can and then a whole lot longer, push-ups, climbing or running: it will pay off (010)

## Push hard, not into injury
- Listen to your body and know pushing really hard from injury: you can push way harder than you think without injury, or too far, into injury. The suggestion is not to ignore your body's signs but to get uncomfortable, decide it's okay and keep going, in push-ups, pull-ups, climbing, running, lifting, whatever, chosen as your intention beforehand (010)
- You can't necessarily go this hard every day forever: you need active recovery days, and our bodies kind of go in cycles a little bit. But as often as you can, go in with the one goal to "push until I get as uncomfortable as I can get" (010)

## Are you willing?
- Life comes down to: are you willing? **Story:** plenty of life was spent unwilling, and the question applies to sharing this message too. Are you willing to put in the work, physical too, for the change and growth you want? We either are or aren't (010)

## Your pain is your power
- Your pain is your power: a fundamental truth that personally took a long time to learn, with a dramatic impact on our life. Wherever your greatest pain points are now, or have been in the past, that's the source of your strength: your challenges and pain make you stronger and make you who you are. In the midst of the pain it's not always easy to see: it's real easy to get overwhelmed and bummed out, and not see it for what it truly is, your strength (024)
- **Story:** why helping men feel great and kick ass is such a passion: a pretty typical childhood; never an athlete, just kind of a skinny little kid. After high school, a job, marrying pretty young, and filling what seems to have been a lack of true purpose with negative things on the outside. The diet got worse and worse, with no idea what was being done, and no care either: more and more weight, weaker and weaker, more and more hurting, more miserable mentally and emotionally (024)
- **Story:** there was a wife at the time, loved, and a daughter had pretty young (three amazing kids now), and parts of life were awesome, but being in such a shitty place internally and physically made it miserable. You've probably been there, "we've all been there", in some way, with sort of health, wellness and habits, not feeling good because of them (024)
- **Story:** by 25 it felt like twice that age: the heaviest and most out of shape ever, unable to run a quarter mile to save one's life, literally, falling apart, mind and body a mess. Some half-hearted attempts at change, but nothing ever really stuck (024)
- **Story:** then came one of the major events of a lifetime, which shifted things. Of the three kids, two girls and a boy, the son, Andrew, is in the middle. At nine days old he wasn't really seeming to do that great, wasn't eating a lot, and by the end of the day his lips were a shade blue, terrifying. At the Instacare his pulse oxygen was low; then the ER, and a couple of hours later the news: a couple of holes in his heart and what's called a narrowed aortic arch. He needed open heart surgery, and had it two days later (024)
- **Story:** the job then was stock trader at a financial services company, trading for clients, a typical corporate gig: pretty good money, but absolutely miserable, probably part of what fed a negative life experience in a lot of ways. The wife and kids were awesome, but the job was hated, the body in terrible shape, life physically miserable (024)
- **Story:** about the next three months were spent by his side in the ICU at the children's hospital, on a leave of absence from the stock trading job. So thankful, and so lucky to be near such amazing care: Andrew's surgeon, one of the top surgeons in the country, absolutely saved his life, and without him Andrew probably wouldn't be alive today (024)
- **Story:** without question the hardest time of life to that point, and it changed something (024)
- Galvanization: going through hard things makes us stronger, applying a force to produce a stronger outcome. It doesn't always feel like that, but it's true (024)
- **Story:** it unleashed a new determination to live the life wanted. Until then there was seemingly the idea a lot of us have kind of as kids: not really seeing the finite nature of life, seeming to feel we'll live forever. Now came the realization: you're going to die. Do you want to die like this, in this condition, living life this way? The answer was no. What had been stop-and-start, half-hearted efforts completely transformed, during that experience and the months after (024)
- **Story:** all that pain, so hard to deal with and like nothing dealt with before, needed somewhere to go: all the emotion, energy, sadness and heartache were poured into workouts, hill sprints at the time, literally crying while running them. It took years to really recognize what had been happening: all that pain and difficulty was coming on board as strength, a transformation into a stronger, better, more capable person who could attack and deal with life in a new way (024)
- Hard, difficult things not only give us the experience of having gone through them: they shift our perspective. Getting through them, you realize you're stronger than you think you are, and sometimes it takes those kinds of events to make you really realize it. **Story:** a divorce not that long ago, for a long time a worst-case scenario in the mind that couldn't even be imagined; it was hard, but going through the time with Andrew, and years and years later the divorce, brought that realization (024)
- Whatever pain you're experiencing, now or maybe in the past, serves a purpose: it builds you into the person you are (024)
- **Story:** probably one of the reasons for the passion for helping men be manly, physical, strong and healthy is having been the opposite and felt the pain of that place: an alcoholic, fat and out of shape, completely emotionally and mentally lost. Now there's amazing gratitude for a completely different place: almost 40, 40 this year, for sure stronger and healthier than ever, and feeling younger really than ever as an adult (024)
- Being the strongest, best version of you at 30 or 40 or 50 or 60: see [[never-too-late]] (024)
- **Story:** a lot of years went into living for nothing other than money in a job, miserable; now it's the opposite, nothing other than helping people. If only one person is helped to avoid or get out of that pain, that's enough. For men feeling the pain of being out of shape, hormones out of whack, without the life force and energy they want, the aim is to help them get out of that, to physically and energetically kicking ass: that's why the podcast and the work with clients (024)
- The challenge: look at whatever challenges and pain you've gone through, or are going through now, the same way as the pain of Andrew's surgery. They're there for a reason, to create you into the person you are (024)
- It seems everything is meant to be for a reason in life for us. With some things, like a family member getting cancer, it's real hard to see the reason why, and sometimes we won't ever really see it, but it seems everything does happen for a reason. Whatever challenges you've had in your life, body, health, success or living the life you want, that pain was there for a reason: shift your perspective, look at it as the power it is, and everything changes (024)
- Today is the only day you have: you can't live tomorrow and you can't relive yesterday. Whatever pain is eating at you, transform it into a power by shifting your perspective and your energy, today. That pain is your power, even if you don't feel it yet (024)
