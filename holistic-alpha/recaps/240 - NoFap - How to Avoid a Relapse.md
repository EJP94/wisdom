> **TL;DR** — Avoiding a NoFap relapse comes down to five-second choices of discomfort: daily cold showers, meditation and physical training build that ability; when the urge comes, use those, understand its circumstances, and redirect the energy into something you'll do instead, knowing why you care.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 240, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- A relapse seems to apply here to porn, definitely, since moving away from porn is, of course, the basis of NoFap, and it also may apply to unwanted masturbation habits outside of porn. Whether you intend to break away from any activity for a while or, as in the last episode on edging, from porn while continuing to edge in a mindful way, this applies either way
- Not an exhaustive list, and some of it depends on your unique circumstances: a few principles and practices that prove very helpful with the majority of guys, in what kind of breaks down into mostly two categories: primarily daily practices that lead to better control over the urge, and what to do when it actually comes up
- At bottom it's your willingness and ability to choose discomfort. Behind the urge are a lot of emotions, varying with the circumstances, or maybe a purely kind of sexual urge with less of an emotional link. Either way, if you intend not to do it, you need to choose discomfort over the comfortable, easy path of pulling up porn or masturbating to get that feeling
- **Five-second decisions**, talked about before: our life is made up of comparatively basically a handful of them in 24 hours, and we live with their results the rest of the time. Home from work: water or a beer from the fridge? At the store: the produce, or the candy and cookie aisle? Porn: pull it up or not? Two minutes or four hours of it doesn't matter: what matters is the five-second choice to walk down that road
- The daily habits build the ability and willingness to make a five-second choice that is not the most comfortable, easy one in the moment and leads where we want to go, which applies to lots of things, and certainly to avoiding a relapse
- **Cold showers**, habit number one. So many people, men and women in all walks of life, use them for a variety of reasons that don't include this, and a lot of men use them for this reason:
  - Physically very, very good: they lower inflammation and boost your immune system (a fair amount of science, and a boatload of anecdotal evidence in the millions and millions of people who rely on them), and lead to positive hormonal and neurotransmitter changes, boosting things like norepinephrine, like one of your feel-good hormones
  - A huge mental and physical reset button. When the cold hits you (also a cold bath, or cold outside or in the mountains in winter), you won't continue down sort of mental loops and negative emotional states: it snaps you out of your patterns, forcing one of two choices, "holy shit, get out of here, it's cold", or settle in, dial in, calm down and find that inner space. The bill due, the conversation you're dreading, or porn here, won't stay in your mind
  - Probably their biggest benefit: they strengthen your ability to just do an uncomfortable thing. What leads to feeling really good we can kind of learn to look forward to and enjoy, but even enjoyed, the cold wouldn't necessarily be called comfortable: it's always kind of uncomfortable, and that can be a really good thing. Every cold shower strengthens your ability and willingness to say "this thing that I'm about to do is gonna be uncomfortable, but I'm gonna do it anyway because I like where it goes"
  - So later that day or night, when the thought of porn, or of ejaculating so you can fall asleep, pops up, you're stronger facing that five-second choice. Every day that starts with a cold shower starts with being willing to do uncomfortable things that lead to good places: not looking at porn, eating in a disciplined way instead of giving in to cravings, working out, since any progress in training is uncomfortable, of course: if it's not, you're probably not getting anywhere
  - A habit that seems so simple, but the right simple things can really add up in a powerful way
- **Meditation**, habit number two, is really important and powerful here. We walk down roads a big part of us doesn't want to because of mental patterns: responding a certain way to internal or external triggers, an emotion or an event. So much of how we live is patterns, daily habits and responses
- To avoid a relapse, understand and release some of those patterns, to live less on patterns and more with intention, keep positive ones and let go of the old. Your habits, thoughts and emotions all happen between your ears. Not to make it sound too light, what happens in our head can be intense, but we'd better address it to change external habits, and meditation is the most powerful and fundamental way to do that, period
- If you have a human body and a mind, you should be meditating: "that's all there is to it". It builds and improves our relationship with our own mind, and relating to ourselves more effectively, we experience more joy and peace, make the changes we want in life, and relate to other people more effectively
- It's like exercise, which every moderately healthy person, and even a lot of relatively ill people to some degree, should be doing, with no excuse of being a different kind of human who doesn't need it. You can write meditation off all you want, but with a brain, a mind, a heart and a spirit, we should take time every day to take care of that mind. Vegging out with TV after work is the opposite; caring for your mind is sitting down, just being, and gaining some awareness
- The goal: people often sit down and think they're not doing it right or not feeling what they should. The more we talk about it, the more we kind of move away from it: you really have to experience it. The best description found: the majority of the time, all the time really, until we start meditating, we're swept up in our thoughts instead of seeing them, palpably in intense anger or sadness, but always, basically becoming them. Meditation at its roots is stepping back, getting a little distance, becoming an observer of what's happening inside
  - Like an airplane taking off on a cloudy, stormy day: climb high enough and there's clear blue sky, and we always have kind of that place. Climbing didn't make the clouds go away: you recognize they're there, and a place beyond them that's clear and still
  - Further along, your mind gets a little quieter on its own and you can kind of quiet it when you choose, but you're never going to just stop thoughts: that's not how it works, and not the point. The point is a little separation and awareness
  - Then habits, thoughts you're not even really aware of and strong emotions (really angry, sad, maybe even really happy) lose much of the power they get from us being swept up in them, with no distance. If you have felt like some of those challenging, "quote, negative" emotions or thoughts have had a lot of control over you, becoming the observer through meditation is one of the best ways to change that feeling
- It doesn't have to take long: 10 minutes a day, done consistently over time, will change your mind and your life, and you can even start with less. It's not instant, like the gym: you train every single day, better yet outside, as a lifestyle, and two weeks, a month, three or six months later you see changes. With meditation as a habit, after a few weeks or months you relate to yourself a little better, find peace a little easier, and let go of some of these patterns
- A relapse, the term in and of itself, is falling back into an old pattern. Meditation helps release patterns and also deal with a lot of what drives the habit: for most guys, an unwanted masturbation and porn habit is due to inner emotional stuff. They get bored, anxious, stressed, maybe sad, maybe love [?], and respond by doing that. Those triggers, which usually send you down the path (unique to you, but those are some common ones), are rooted in your mind, so meditation helps you address the root cause: the loneliness, anxiety, stress, boredom
- **Physical training** every day, again mainly to choose discomfort: unwilling to do uncomfortable things, you get sucked into comfort-fueled behaviors, watching TV, eating shit, jerking off to the point of ejaculation, being addicted to porn. Breaking them means choosing uncomfortable but productive things, as in a lot of life, business, relationships, health: doing uncomfortable things in the moment because they lead where you want to go

One of its very best things is building the muscle to say "that's uncomfortable, but it leads to a good place and I'm gonna do it right now", which makes you stronger in the five-second choice on porn, or on not masturbating if you're avoiding that
- Three fundamental habits (cold showers and meditation every single day already make a big difference in the five-second choice) that seemingly all of us should be doing all the time; not doing all three every single day puts you at a major disadvantage with the NoFap thing, wherever you are: further along, practicing semen retention past a week or two at a time, building it with a partner, or in the very early stages, after watching porn basically every day of your life. - The urge is still going to come up: say you work from home, it's three o'clock in the afternoon, a break between meetings, and it tries to pull you toward porn. What will you do in that moment?
- First, any of those three works fantastic, and it doesn't have to be big: a set of push-ups or burpees, a two-minute walk or run, literally a 15-second cold rinse, or one, two or five minutes of meditation
- **Understand the urge.** Especially early in the journey, gain awareness of what tries to send you down that road: what was I doing, where was I, what was I feeling? You might recognize that it usually comes after 45 minutes on social media at home in the afternoon, then you find yourself moving to porn. Knowing the circumstances, change them: get off social media, use it at a different time, or limit it to a set amount and then move to another predetermined activity. It could be anything

Part of the moment is the action items, part is being willing to look at the urge: where am I, what am I feeling, doing, experiencing, what led to this? Stepping back when you feel the urge to ask what's happening and how to address it differently is really fundamental
- **Channel the energy.** We are energetic beings: everything in the world is energy, we are too, everything we feel is. - So many guys talked to use phrasing like "I need to avoid this", "I'm not gonna look at porn", part of the reason for this episode's title. It's really, really hard to not do something, much easier and more effective to do something else instead. Say don't think about a pink elephant and you'll think about one: a core primal part of our brain literally does not understand the "don't". Repeat "don't look at porn" and part of your brain hears "look at porn, look at porn". Move from not doing this thing to doing this other thing instead

If part of your life has been draining a significant amount of energy, ask what you want to do with it instead: maybe building that business, changing your body, building a nonprofit. Everybody has their own dreams: tap into yours

If it's because somebody else thinks you should, or some moral thing (there are morals to it too), really think about why. Probably you maybe want better choices, more confidence, more energy, a different charge and charisma with people, or the discipline to create the life you want, seeing it slip away for lack of the choices you need to make. Whatever it is, ask what you really want to use that energy and life force for instead of this significant drain
