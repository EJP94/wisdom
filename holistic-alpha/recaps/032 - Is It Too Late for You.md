> **TL;DR** — It's probably too late for the NBA, but never for a dramatically different life: many successes came late, we're not stuck, major change might take a year or two of daily effort, and it seems growth means becoming more of who you truly are.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 32, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- Too often we let time go by without making the changes we want. This weekend, take even a single minute of action: any step toward where you want to get is better than no step at all
- Is it too late for us to do the things we want to do? More than likely, unless you're really young (some young guys listen: want to be an NBA player, do that), you've probably passed the window to play in the NBA or NFL, or probably fly a fighter jet. But it's never too late to live a dramatically different life and be a dramatically different person
- The saying "what have you done for me lately" is really true of our life: after major changes, you won't dwell on how things used to be. No matter how old you are, it's never too late to change: the health and fitness you want, that relationship change, that business, your career
- So many of the most successful people we have ever had in this world didn't achieve their noteworthy success until much later in life: their forties, fifties, sixties, seventies. Warren Buffett made more of his money in his sixties and seventies than in all the rest of his life combined
- A reminder seen the other day: Samuel L. Jackson, who seemingly has been around forever, a star for something like the last 15–20 years, didn't get his first real acting break until he was 47
- Study every field, for the most part, and it's like that. A couple are young kids' games: dudes don't decide in their mid-30s to become an NBA player, work at it for seven years and get in the league at 42. We have to be a little realistic, but way too often we shortchange ourselves on the changes we believe we can make
- We are not stuck with who we are, the way things are or the way we experience life, none of it. Whether 25, 30, 50 or 65, we have so much time, so who cares how things have been? At 35 to 50, you're maybe halfway through this game: taking care of yourself, you potentially have another 30, 40, 50 years or more, with medical understanding and technology improving. Even at 60 or 65, starting to take care of yourself now, you might have 30, 40, 50 years
- Major changes might take a year or two of consistent daily effort. Then one day you look back on how you used to feel, your old fitness, that job you hated. At 55, 60 or 65, you can make the changes you want over the next six months, year or two years: building a business, leaving a relationship that's not serving you and finding somebody new
- You can do so much more than you think in a couple year period, and really even less. They say we often underestimate what we can do in five years and overestimate what we can do in a year. Sometimes we tend to be impatient and want things overnight, yet we don't let our imagination run enough to see how different things could be
- You're not stuck with the body you have right now: change things, and you'll have an entirely different body two or three years from now. You are what you eat, you are your habits. Change what you do every day for work, and two years down the line things will be dramatically different
- Be brave and go for it. We're going to die, a year from now, tomorrow or 55 years from now: don't die with the regret of not doing the things you want to do
- The main first step is just thinking it's possible. It might be too late to be an astronaut, but too late to create the life you want, destroy the regret you would have, pursue your real passion? Hell no. When the clock hits triple zero on your life, that's when it's too late
- At 20, have some patience: you've got your whole life ahead of you. At 65, you've got decades ahead of you
- It seems we don't really change through self-improvement: we're becoming more of who we truly are, letting more of it out into action and life, and letting go of the crap we're not. It's never too late to let go of that crap, or to pursue the dreams you know are inside you. They won't go away: left where they are, over time, they turn into regret
- **Story:** in the 20s, a belief that it was too late, that it was getting old and all this stuff was done, without even getting started
- Do your Kegels and reverse Kegels every day (at least a couple of earlier episodes cover them). Besides improving your sexual life, performance and enjoyment, which feeds into so much of your life, they help light that fire, your internal drive to make changes
- We need drive and energy to start taking action. Part of it comes from just doing it: once you take a little action, life feeds more drive and energy back to you so you can keep going
- Make the change: one minute, five minutes, twenty minutes toward where and who you want to be. Be whoever you want to be that you're not being right now, for one minute, for five minutes. That's where it starts
