> **TL;DR** — Oxytocin, a peptide hormone known as kind of the love hormone, has documented social effects and, in mice studies, a link to youth through muscle-regenerating stem cells; vitamin D, vitamin C, magnesium, caffeine, especially interpersonal touch, singing, yoga and fenugreek boost it.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 26, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- Oxytocin is a little-known hormone, yet some modern science is showing just how important it really is
- A hormone is a signaling molecule, produced by the glands of our endocrine system and carried by our circulatory system to target distant organs, regulating physiology and behavior: basically messengers giving the body orders on what to do and how to behave. Personally, a lot more passion for hormones in recent years, given their powerful effects on our lives
- Three main kind of types of hormones:
  - Eicosanoids, kind of lesser known, probably not talked about anytime soon
  - Steroid hormones, including androgens like testosterone, and estrogens, familiar ones
  - Amino acid or protein derivatives, including peptide hormones. Oxytocin is a peptide hormone, based on an amino acid, and a neurotransmitter. Other peptide hormones include growth hormone, insulin and leptin, really powerful and important ones
- It's produced mainly in the hypothalamus in the brain, but also elsewhere: there's conclusive evidence it's synthesized in the testes and the prostate, and oxytocin receptors are present throughout the male reproductive tract, and the female one. It definitely acts on the brain, but does a lot more
- For years it has been documented to affect social feelings and interaction. Released when you hug someone, have sex or bond socially, it's often been called kind of the love hormone. But a lot of recent science shows it's involved in more social aspects than just love, and has far more impactful effects on our bodies
- Its social effects are pretty well researched, a lot of the studies giving people an inhaled mist of oxytocin, essentially a boost. Documented effects:
  - It increases couples' desire to kind of be with and gaze at each other, strengthening that bond
  - It increases sexual arousal and helps maintain erections
  - It reduces social fears, "a really big one", and has been shown to induce feelings of optimism and increase self-esteem
  - People become more trusting, "in very proven ways"
  - It increases generosity, and reduces cortisol and stress
  - Low levels have been associated with depression, and a number of studies now show pretty powerful therapeutic potential for treating clinical depression
- Bottom line, those effects, known kind of a little longer, are incredibly beneficial, but really just the start: oxytocin has really powerful physical effects. Previous studies have shown it's beneficial in healing wounds, relieves pain via anti-inflammatory action, and reduces osteoporosis and obesity: it physically impacts us to a great degree
- A group of studies with mice from the last several years shows how it affects our health on a fundamental level. Keep in mind some are very new and only on mice, but they clue us into what could very likely be happening in our human bodies
  - A little creepy but amazing: connect an old mouse and a young mouse so the young one's blood flows through the old one's body, and the old mouse becomes dramatically rejuvenated, its heart, skeletal muscle, even its brain. Scientists are figuring out why, since that could delay aging and the onset of aging symptoms, potentially in humans
  - A team published a study, probably in 2014, "so it's been a few years". They found a protein called GDF11 partially responsible for the rejuvenation, then, knowing some of oxytocin's effects, investigated it as potentially responsible too
  - Removing the ovaries from female mice dropped oxytocin levels dramatically, and importantly, the mice also started aging dramatically
  - Bodies, mouse bodies at least, have oxytocin receptors on places that include the stem cells that generate new muscle. Those receptors would not be there without a reason: oxytocin is sending those stem cells a signal
  - Old mice had a third of young mice's oxytocin levels, and their stem cells had fewer oxytocin receptors
  - Given oxytocin, old mice regenerated far more new muscle fibers. Blocking it in young mice left them unable to regenerate new muscle effectively, and they essentially grew old really quickly
  - Muscle stem cells in a dish with oxytocin added multiplied quickly once they grabbed onto it: they discovered definitively that oxytocin directly alters the behavior of muscle-regenerating stem cells
  - The effect is so proven that oxytocin is already used in modern stem cell therapy, often applied to what they call precondition stem cells before they go into a person, so they're more effective once the treatment is administered
- The bottom line, though it's still being understood even more: oxytocin is "a pretty damn good thing". We feel the effects of not having enough, and can probably feel for sure the benefits of potentially having more
- How to get it, a few kind of categories:
  - Nutrients to be sure to get enough of, a few of the basics we want anyway: vitamin D, vitamin C and magnesium. Magnesium and vitamin D especially are two of the supplements recommended most personally, and that doctors around the world recommend most, super important for so many bodily functions, including oxytocin production
  - Caffeine, interestingly, has been shown to increase oxytocin production: another reason coffee is pretty much the bomb
  - **Interpersonal touch**, number one among the major lifestyle things: sex, cuddling, kissing, and even non-sexual touch, including hugging and even shaking hands. Other things may be having an effect, but it's very likely that this aspect, positively or negatively, is having a big impact on your oxytocin levels. One of the reasons breakups, separating from a partner or divorce can be so hard: you've been sort of at an oxytocin level, getting that interpersonal touch, and when it ends your levels tank, a really powerful impact
  - Music, and especially singing, especially loud singing, which has amazing physiological benefits that translate into a lot of aspects of our body and life. Sing in the car, in the shower, wherever
  - Yoga has also been shown to boost oxytocin levels, among the many other benefits [?]
  - A supplement: **fenugreek**, personally one of the favorites, found incredibly beneficial. A seed, and a spice often used in curry, interestingly, it contains oxytocin among its many other complementary compounds and tends to boost oxytocin production
    - Some studies on fenugreek specifically show it boosts libido, or sex drive, raises testosterone levels, balances cholesterol, reduces fat mass and increases strength, and soothes muscle pain
    - Connect that to how powerful oxytocin is on a fundamental level, literally signaling our stem cells to regenerate muscle, with effects throughout our body, and that's just one of its effects
    - Highly recommended to give it a shot if you haven't, and see how it makes you feel. It's really inexpensive and you can get it anywhere; get organic fenugreek if you can
- A big fan of just trying a supplement as long as it's safe: in kind of the natural arena, most things are pretty safe at reasonable dosages. Fenugreek certainly is completely safe: a seed, you could go crazy with it and not hurt yourself, and certainly not end up in the ER no matter how much you swallow. Try it for a week or two and look back at how you've felt or what result you got. Some you'll like, some you won't, but you'll discover a few probably right for you, your body and your unique physical and mental situation, that can add huge benefits
- For oxytocin, do the things you can, especially interpersonal touch: it really matters for a lot of physical reasons in our body, in addition to the mental and emotional ones
