> **TL;DR** — Two counterintuitive ways to clear pain and tightness: roll your whole body, ideally with the Gaiam Pressure Point Massager, to uncover tightness before it hurts, and look next door, since the root cause of pain is often a connected tight muscle, not the sore spot.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 30, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- Two really effective ways to get rid of pain and tightness, kind of counterintuitive and a little unknown: not really tricks, but not a lot of people seem to think about them or use them
- We all have pain and tightness in various places, from physical training or from a lack of it
- **Story:** in the least pain ever, and clearly the oldest ever. We don't have to live in pain, even training hard
- **Story:** a few days ago, training really hard a couple days in a row built up tightness and sore spots, so the last couple days went to a lot more recovery, a reminder of how useful these two things are
- Number one: **use rollers**. The various muscle and fascia release rollers are all really useful, one by far the most. Probably their most useful side is not working on what you already know about, which you can do, but uncovering tightness before it gets really painful
- The favorite: the Pressure Point Massager by Gaiam (G-A-I-A-M), a company making kind of yoga stuff and a lot of recovery tools. Three kind of balls covered in spikes, basically a combination of acupuncture and fascia release by rolling
- **Story:** making passes with it over literally the whole body, neck, arms, the core around the back, chest and sides, hips, glutes, all over the legs. It took a while to figure out what was so useful: you roll over a spot and it kind of hurts, though you weren't really aware the tightness was there
- As with a lot of things in our body, the earlier we catch it, the more we can tweak things and get rid of it: with tightness, the less it affects us and the less likely it becomes some kind of injury
- Probably on Amazon, last checked for 7 bucks, ridiculous. **Story:** paid probably 14 bucks at a Whole Foods or a Natural Grocers, where it was first discovered. It's crazy the places you'll find things when you open your mind to wanting solutions; really into recovery, knowing the difference it makes personally. Lots of other rollers used, but this insanely cheap one is absolutely the favorite
- Secret number two: often, the thing hurting is not the root cause, especially with muscles, training and joints. So often it's not what hurts that is really tight, with the knots to work out, but what's connected to it. Looking next door uncovers the root causes of a lot of pain:
  - Oftentimes with a sore knee, very likely your quads are very, very tight, or it could be your hamstrings, shins or calves, or a combination
  - Low back pain, which we probably all have had: oftentimes it's your glutes and hamstrings, kind of along that chain
  - Hip pain: often the quads, the hamstrings, maybe the pelvic muscles
  - A hurting back could come all the way up from the neck, whose muscles feed down into it: you feel it in your back, but need to work up into the neck
- That happens a lot after standing a really long time. One of the reasons: the glutes, hamstrings and whole backside chain are engaged the whole time to support you, get worked and get tight. Tight muscles, like the hamstring and glute, which a lot of times get tight together, shorten up and pull on what they're connected to, in a lot of cases the lower back
- **Story:** knee pain almost never happens, but one of the most surefire ways to get a little is tight quads: without fail, that's the cause. Rolling out, without having really felt how tight they were: "wow, holy shit, there's a lot of tightness there"
- Get a roller, highly suggested the Gaiam one, the best 7 or 14 bucks or whatever you could spend on muscle recovery. Be diligent: it only takes 5 or 10 minutes of quick passes over really the majority of your body, then maybe another 5 or 10 minutes to dig into the areas that hurt
- Rolling out tightness means being okay with a little intentional pain: the spots you find are going to hurt, a little uncomfortable. The point is a little short-term discomfort for a lot more long-term health, comfort, power and wellness. Recovery is so crucial
- When you get pain, don't limit yourself to that area: more often than not, probably, something next door or connected to it in your body's chain is the root cause
