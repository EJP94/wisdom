> **TL;DR** — We are the sum of our habits, and tallies are one of the best ways to upgrade them: a habit goes from absent, to tracked with effort, to automatic, part of your identity; you own that process; start with one to three habits.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 212, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Story:** day 20 of the 75 Hard Plus, Holistic Alpha Style challenge (explained in the episode of May 4th), all numbers hit: awesome so far, and definitely a challenge, as expected
- One of the most effective ways found to change habits, personally and in coaching guys, is tracking them with **tallies**, on paper or, for most guys, in a phone app. Needless to say, it works, extremely well
- We truly are the sum of our habits: who we are, what we experience, our life situation, in a lot of ways our identity, what we become and the life we build are largely determined by them. One of the best ways to upgrade our life, maybe one of the only ways, is to upgrade our habits, and one of the best ways to do that is tallies
- **Story:** a client that day mentioned briefly something actually pretty powerful: a cold shower felt like just part of who he is now, and he didn't really feel he needed to track it with a tally anymore. That is the process we want
- To truly change our lives, change our habits so they become who we are. At first, a daily tally takes discipline and the system of tracking, which is highly effective: the system you use is everything here. It takes thinking about it, intention, and setting up the situation to be easy and rewarding, your environment triggering the habit. Executed long enough, it becomes automatic, part of who you are
- **Story:** that happened with a lot of things by now, certainly cold showers: once intentional and tracked, now automatic and untracked, one every day; never using a tally app again wouldn't matter
- It seems so often we think a change (working out, cold showers, eating better, a sexual health thing) will always take a massive, constant effort. It doesn't take the same effort forever: what takes effort is changing the habit, and once it's ingrained deeply enough it becomes automatic
- **Story:** automatic now: cold showers, meditating, some workout every day, Kegels, a lot of things. Some stay tracked to be absolutely certain they get done, though even untracked, some things would still just get done, being a part of identity. Others in the current challenge aren't that automatic and mean pushing edges to stay on track, a great thing too: being willing and able to do that is an important part of growing
- Reaching automatic is beautiful, because then you've truly changed who you are. A cold shower might not seem exciting, but the process that made it automatic is: if you can do that for one habit, you can do it for any other
- Guaranteed, you have done it for some habit, recently or further back: it became automatic, and even if it takes some effort it doesn't feel like a lot. So, guaranteed, you can do it again for any habit you choose: you own the process to make a habit part of your identity. Put it to use where you want
- The process: not happening at all, then happening with a system, tracking and intention, then automatic, who you are. The more we move positive things through it, the more we change our life, period
- It does not need to be 18 things at a time, as mentioned before. **Story:** after a long time with this system, about 20 things are tracked in the 75 Hard Plus challenge, a few already kind of automatic, some part of the actual rules, a lot not, partially to kind of prove how much the system works
- Don't track 20 things. Start with one, every single day, making sure it gets done every single day, or maybe two or three. **Story:** his son tracks three right now: water, push-ups and squats
- Once used to tallies, expand: a habit goes from not existing, to your tally, to automatic, then off the tally to make room for another. Over time which one, two or three change, and maybe you add some
- With a system to track habits day to day, a tally app or not, you have the power to take anything from absent, to present with effort and tracking, to automatic, a good place for habits to be. Your habits determine who you become and your life path: pay attention to them and upgrade them
