> **TL;DR** — Day one of the 21-day habit challenge: 500 daily burpees broken into sessions, an hour of meditation at night, and a long cold shower that turned meditative, since the cold takes you inside; rough days ahead are some of the most important to debrief.

*From Holistic Alpha episode 550, a conversation between host Steven Mathis and Charles Martin. Lines marked **Guest** are Charles's, unmarked lines are the host's. Not fact-checked.*

- **Host story:** for listeners this is the next episode, but in real life about six days had passed since the first conversation. The habits probably started about three days earlier (it would take a look in HabitShare [?]), but today counts as day one, together with the listeners
- **Host story:** getting back to 500 burpees a day has been rough, "a freaking wake up call": once, from habit, it didn't seem that big a deal, and training has continued, in good shape, but not the same. Hard and pretty miserable, yet it brings back the fired-up feeling after burpees, like an animal, a big fire. For now it's a couple of sessions a day, two 250s or so; over the coming three to five days, a session or two in one go, then more regularly
- **Host story:** the other habit, an hour of meditation, is really hard time- and schedule-wise. The last few days it got done at the end of the night, potentially staying up a little later, not ideal; mornings already start with meditation, so the hour is catch-up, but it's getting done
- One benefit of working on habits intentionally, it seems, is figuring out how to fit them into your schedule, which can make a big difference in whether they get done
- **Guest story:** day one beat the last six days and took a lot of willpower, with cold showers again. Getting in felt like "that resistance. Well, that's exactly why I need to go forward". Of the past two days of cold showers, day one was the best he felt from them, because he held it longer: normally about five minutes, this time no quick in and out, no running from it, no warm shower after, "the real deal, the whole duration". He aimed for 15 minutes and made it to like 11 or 12
- **Guest story:** his body got used to it and stopped feeling the freezing water, so he slowed everything down into some form of meditation, mind and body connected (no meditation guru, still learning). He had energy for the day without even needing coffee, did his workouts and burpees, shaking off rust, and fit in another round of meditation at night: after a while without it, quieting your mind takes a while
- Meditating in the cold doesn't seem weird at all. A favorite thing about the cold in general (a cold shower, a plunge, the mountains) is that it takes you inside. The initial response is a freak-out, "holy shit, I gotta get out of this cold", but if we can move past that, it gradually takes us deeper and deeper
- The more we do a sitting meditation, as the guest has for a while, for the most part, the more it shows up elsewhere: in a cold shower, in edging or at some other time, you get into a really meditative place
- Not perfect, but going forward a day at a time. There will be some ugly days: inevitably, even in only 21 days, some will be rough, and the longer the things we do in life, the more ugly days appear. Those are some of the most important days to talk and debrief
