> **TL;DR** — Conflict is an opportunity to deepen trust if you stay calm, listen and make your partner feel safe; glycine, a hard-to-get amino acid shown to support glutathione, creatine, collagen, sleep, liver, heart and metabolism, is probably worth trying, while listening to your body.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 303, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- From single to married, from early dating to a long-term relationship, one of the most important things to keep in mind seems to be that **conflict is an opportunity**, a very big one. True in any relationship, especially romantic ones
- A lot of guys seem to see conflict as a negative thing, something going wrong, even the first one after dating has been awesome and fun. Repeating patterns over and over can of course become negative, but in a lot of ways conflict is an amazing opportunity to see how you move through things together, deepen the relationship and each person's trust, and help each person feel supported, safe and heard, in longer relationships too
- It depends on how we move through it. So often we tend to get very defensive and reactionary, stop listening and focus on saying what we think. Not that we can't and shouldn't share our thoughts and feelings, but it's so important to step back, listen and give your partner a space to safely express herself and feel heard. When your wife or girlfriend says something that triggers you, reacting emotionally reduces trust and safety. Truly listening to understand, not filtering through what you think the situation is, with calm, deepens the relationship
- Especially in the earlier stages, how you deal with conflicts is a very important factor in how the relationship progresses, and in how safe facing conflict with you is for your partner. On the deepest levels, one of our most primal drives is to feel safe, especially true with women in a relationship: she wants to know you won't fly off the handle and freak out, that you'll listen and take time to understand what she is feeling
- In the short term, after a disagreement with your wife, do you come back together closer at the end of that day, or feel more distant for days or longer because you let it separate you? Usually that comes from being reactive, emotional, not truly listening
- **You have a responsibility.** Regardless of what your partner feels and of any idea of right or wrong, you have a responsibility and an opportunity to be a place of safety, calm and reason: stay calm, listen, understand, and when the time is appropriate make your feelings heard, calmly, not in an accusatory way. Something very positive can come of it, but only if you see it that way and stay grounded
- Setting the intention is important, and meditation comes into play. Your ability to find a quieter, deeper place of inner calm when shit goes crazy outside, an argument, something in business, a health issue with your family or kids, is dramatically impacted by a regular meditation practice. It helps you stay grounded through the brief moments when part of you wants to be triggered and react
- Develop that ability and willingness and you will feel the difference in trust and connection. She wants physical safety from danger, physical safety in terms of sexuality, and emotional safety: can she talk to you, can you have conflict without you freaking out? The more safe your partner is in all the ways, the closer you get, and "she's gonna be a hell of a lot more attracted to you". Safety is attractive, especially for women with their partner
- Use conflict as an opportunity and find that calmness. It will pay dividends in your relationships: "I guarantee that it will, but only if you're willing to put in the work and do it"
- The last episode covered shilajit, a favorite supplement, "super powerful, amazing stuff". **Glycine** wasn't really on the radar until maybe the last few months, and very quickly became a favorite too. It seems appropriate for nearly everybody, something you may want to try
- **Story:** over the last couple of years, more experimenting with supplementing various amino acids. They can be incredibly powerful: the body uses them so fundamentally that lacking important ones, or raising certain ones, can have powerfully beneficial effects, as was definitely found with glycine
- Glycine is one of the most important amino acids, and rather hard to get from food. Amino acids are sort of the building blocks of protein, but the body uses them in very complex ways, beyond getting enough grams of protein. Some are relatively abundant from the right foods, some much harder to get even on, quote, sort of an ideal diet, glycine among them
- A main source is animal products, but most glycine in animals is in connective tissue, since glycine is a building block of collagen. Meat gives you some, for sure, but not large amounts, so even people on protein-rich diets are often not getting enough
- **Glutathione.** The body uses glycine to make glutathione, the most powerful antioxidant in your body, or one of the most powerful, and definitely the most abundant. Antioxidants essentially protect cells against oxidative damage, which cascades through body systems and leads to various diseases. Low glycine limits glutathione and so the handling of oxidative stress over time. Glutathione naturally decreases with age, so for older or somewhat older listeners this matters even more: maintaining glutathione levels through glycine supplementation can be a very powerful and important way to keep oxidative stress under control
- **Creatine.** Glycine is one of the three building blocks the body uses to make creatine, which the vast majority of listeners have probably heard of, very commonly recommended as a supplement in the fitness world. Given the building blocks, the body makes more. Creatine gives muscles basically the energy for quick, intense bursts of activity, and there are tons of studies showing it increases muscle size, strength and power
- **Collagen.** Glycine is the main amino acid in collagen, probably a buzzword you've heard because it is one of the most abundant proteins in the body, giving strength to muscles, skin, cartilage, bones, ligaments and even blood. The body can't make enough without building blocks, and glycine is one often lacking in people. Supplementing supports collagen production, with lots of physical benefits
- **Sleep.** Glycine has been shown to quite significantly improve sleep quality, and a lot of people have trouble sleeping at times. It is not habit forming and won't knock you out and leave you groggy. People report, and some studies show, that glycine before bed has a bit of a calming effect on the brain, can help you fall and stay asleep, and can help lower core body temperature, very important for deep sleep. Too high a temperature late in the evening hurts sleep quality, how rested you wake and hormone production, since a lot of testosterone production happens at night. It has also been shown to lessen daytime sleepiness and improve cognition
- As with shilajit on the last episode, all these things mentioned about glycine have been shown in studies: the data backs up these claims, as it were
- **Liver.** Glycine can protect your liver from alcohol-induced damage, and has been shown, in some animal studies specifically, to help reverse liver damage from excessive alcohol. A lot of listeners have had their share or more of alcohol in the past, and a lot may be drinking somewhat more than they want now
- **Story:** "I've had a problem": many beers every day, secretly drinking at 9 a.m. before running hill sprints. No drinking at all now
- People often think of the liver as a filter for toxins, and a lot of people think it stores toxins. It's the complete opposite: it helps detoxify but doesn't store toxins, and maybe even more important, it stores nutrients until they are released, since the body can't use them all at once. A liver not functioning ideally hurts detoxification and nutrient release, with downstream negative effects. The more we support our liver, the better
- **Story:** eating organs, including plenty of beef liver. Eating animal livers is very powerful, because liver stores nutrients
- **Heart and erections.** There's quite a bit of evidence that glycine protects your heart and positively affects blood flow, one of the most important factors in erections. "No question, glycine has the potential to positively affect your erections and your erection quality". It is very likely to improve erections, erection quality and morning wood, and very possibly to increase flaccid size. No studies show that last one, but personally there was a bit of a bump in flaccid size, seemingly almost immediately, within two or three days maybe, which can matter from a confidence standpoint: "what guy wouldn't" want it bigger
- Glycine has been linked to benefits on the hardening and narrowing of arteries. One of the very first things that happens when that starts affecting blood flow is that it affects your erections, which is why erection quality and morning wood are kind of the canary in the coal mine
- **Nitric oxide.** One way glycine does this is that it can improve your body's ability to use nitric oxide, a very critical gas that dilates blood vessels and lets blood flow. If nitric oxide gets very low, blood can't reach the tissues it needs to. Boosting it can have a dramatic effect on erections and in the gym: more strength, better muscle recovery, better athletic performance
- A study around chest pains related to hardening and narrowing of the arteries: across 4,100 people, higher glycine levels were associated with a lower risk of heart disease and heart attacks at a seven-and-a-half-year follow-up. Whatever naturally supports heart health supports erectile health too
- **Metabolic health.** A lot of listeners may have some impairment there: any extra weight means it could for sure improve, and even a lot of guys at a pretty good weight can have sort of less than ideal markers, depending on what they eat. Paying attention to metabolic health and avoiding things that lead to diabetes is very, very important. It has been suggested that glycine supplements can improve impaired insulin response in people with some level of diabetes and help people not move down that path. Most diabetes is not actually caused by high blood sugar, as commonly thought, but usually by your body's response to insulin, a separate but related thing
- **Muscle wasting.** Glycine has been shown to reduce muscle wasting, which can come with age, with the stress of being sick or a longer-term illness, or with poor fueling and nutrition. It has been shown to be quite effective in inhibiting muscle breakdown and enhancing muscle building, so if you train or want to keep muscle as you age, it can very likely help. All of these benefits come from the studies, with data to sort of back them up
- **Story:** on glycine, the mind felt sharper, with more mental clarity; it has been shown to be specifically beneficial to the brain through blood flow. Dreams became more frequent and vivid, without question, taken as a sign that it was improving blood flow to the brain
- The body has sort of gauges, if we listen. Morning wood is a very important one. Another is dreaming: whether you dream, recall your dreams and how vivid they are can be a very important indicator of your brain's health and whether it gets the nutrients it needs
- **Story:** it probably helped erections, though it's hard to tell when things are already pretty good. Felt very good from a muscle-building standpoint: being kind of sick the last couple of weeks threw things off a little, but overall, since starting glycine, strength felt high and recovery very quick
- Glycine is probably appropriate for just about anybody to try. It has been shown to be extremely safe: any amino acid already part of food is something our body handles quite well. It is quite cheap and easy to find
- **Dosing.** You can kind of play around with the dosing and feel pretty safe doing so. A lot of the studies use somewhere around maybe five grams, and some safety studies used as much as 90 grams per day for weeks with no serious side effects. Not a suggestion to start at 90 grams a day, but it is quite safe to play with dosages. Suggested start: a few times per day, maybe morning, midday to afternoon, and a round at night before bed, which can be really beneficial; maybe two or three grams, two or three times per day
- Personally, very good things heard from people it was recommended to, and very good results. It seems essentially free of meaningful side effects: none of the studies really show any. Look for a quality supplier: always do your due diligence, and make sure it is a trustworthy brand
- **Don't just take everything all the time.** Hearing about a supplement doesn't mean starting every one that might even potentially be beneficial. Cycle off things and watch how your body responds: maybe it does what you read or heard, maybe it won't. You might find a sort of secret sauce that hardly anybody talks about, or something lots of people talk about might do very little for you. Bodies are unique, and responses differ at different points in life. Listen to your body, test, cycle on and off, and supplements can be a big benefit
- It seems very important to get the majority of our nutrients from food. The idea that you could eat crappy food and take a multivitamin seems "one of the ultimate stupidities". Nutrient-dense food seems very, very critical, but supplements sometimes let us go a little beyond what food alone gives, and glycine is probably one worth trying
