> **TL;DR** — Cold showers, which you can start with 10 or 20 seconds of cold, help reset the mind and train your willingness to get uncomfortable; recognizing your wins and progress, alone or in a relationship, expands them and reminds you of your power.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 350, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Cold showers** are one of the most simple yet incredibly powerful habits, long recommended, and more guys find them on their own. Most people absolutely hate them at first, but they help hit the mental reset button, energize and invigorate you for the day, clear out your emotional space and benefit the physical body
- Personally, over the last several years, it's one of the habits with among the greatest impact: it gets a day on track and puts you in a different mental and emotional state for the rest of it, which is hard to describe until you experience it
- A lot of you are already on the cold shower train: stay on it. If not, you can start at the end of a warm shower with a little cold, 10 or 20 seconds, then ramp up; a suggestion: fairly quickly go straight cold
- Maybe among the most important benefits: it strengthens your willingness to make a choice that is uncomfortable in the moment but benefits you in the long run. That willingness is "basically a muscle" that grows with practice, and a cold shower is excellent daily practice for it
- **Recognizing wins**: almost everyone met on clarity calls, in coaching or in real life tends to focus on where they're coming up short, the current challenging step, their mistakes, and doesn't take enough time and intention to appreciate the good they've done. It seems common to most humans, but certainly to these men. For example, someone getting porn out of their life fixes on the current struggle and completely overlooks all the progress made
- This drags down how we feel and actually slows our results: what we give focus to, we give energy to, and what we give energy to expands. To expand the positive things, give them focus by recognizing them
- This weekend, today, think about the good things you've done, today, this last week, or over six months, a year or two years: your accomplishments, your progress, the hard things you've come through, which we so often overlook. Recognize yourself, celebrate yourself, be proud. The more you focus on the good things, the more you draw them into your life
- Both are simple but very powerful: acknowledging wins shifts your state of mind and what you give energy and focus to, and reminds you of the power you do have
- Focused on the current challenge or frustration, we often make our problems out to be sort of bigger than they are, and ourselves kind of smaller and less powerful. Remembering your progress and wins makes you stronger and more confident facing the current challenge
- Maybe discuss it in a relationship: married, it's very easy to get lost in the current problem. Recognize the good and the progress in your relationship; appreciate the other person for it, and yourself. The good things are "absolutely there"; you just have to recognize them to get the benefit
