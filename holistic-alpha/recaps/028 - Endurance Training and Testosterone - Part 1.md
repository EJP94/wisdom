> **TL;DR** — Training beyond about 45 minutes raises stress hormones, which inhibit testosterone, and runs you out of good hormones: outside endurance sports, keep workouts to 45 minutes or less; sleep and nutrition (no sugar or toxic fats, little processed food, enough good fats) are the basics.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 28, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Story:** the 20s were pretty much the opposite of healthy and strong: fat, weak, really no discipline, a horrible diet, erectile dysfunction, an alcoholic. Since then, a journey to maximize personal health and wellness, and the dramatic difference made helping other guys the biggest passion. At almost 40, in every way healthier, stronger and more physically well than at 25
- It doesn't matter how old you are; what matters is how you live your life, and you can change things to experience a dramatically different body
- Endurance training can have a very significant impact on your hormones and your testosterone
- There's a time frame around 45 minutes or so. Any physical training at sort of a moderate intensity or higher for more than 45 minutes starts to have negative effects on our hormones
- If you're not specifically into an endurance sport, it's absolutely advisable to keep all workouts, at least the vast majority, to 45 minutes or less and focus on increasing intensity. A big fan also of even just a 15-minute workout: set a timer and go as hard as you can for those 15 minutes
- An intense or even moderate-intensity workout up to 45 minutes generally gives a hormone boost, especially with strength training. Hopefully you're including some; if not, it definitely makes a big difference in supporting your hormones. Beyond 45 minutes, the impact shifts from positive to really negative
- If you're for sure going to train multiple-hour runs or rides, or any longer distance training, on a very regular basis, it will have a negative impact on your hormones. That doesn't necessarily mean you can't deal with it, but it seems even more important to do everything you can to minimize that effect and support your hormones
- The impact grows the more you train: training 2–3 hours a day, day after day, you'll feel it more than training an hour and a half every other day. There are really two major impacts:
  - Number one, it raises your stress hormones, including cortisol and adrenaline. That hurts in two ways:
    - Their direct effects impact our mental and physical health, our cardiovascular health, in really significant ways. That can be true whether you're an endurance athlete or not: a lot of us run in way too high a stress hormone state just from letting stress from the job or whatever pile on without dealing with it effectively. We need to minimize stress regardless of the training, and with longer endurance training it becomes even more important to lower cortisol and adrenaline
    - They also inhibit testosterone. A lot of studies show cortisol has a direct inhibitory action on testosterone, so stress hormones block your body from having as much quote-unquote good hormones: kind of a two-fisted blow, with cascading effects throughout your health
  - Number two, it directly reduces your testosterone. To simplify: no hormone can really be classified as good or bad, each has an important function. But stress hormones can essentially be called bad, since too little cortisol is possible but very rare, and most people in today's world, especially athletes, probably run too high a cortisol state, so we want to minimize it as much as we can. The androgens, including testosterone, are essentially the good ones. Hormones are messengers that send signals, and when you require your body to use them for hour upon hour, in a simple way, since hormones are very complicated, you basically run out of enough good hormones
- Key takeaway: not an endurance athlete, keep workouts to 45 minutes or less and focus on intensity. An endurance athlete, do everything you can to optimize your hormones, because you're taxing them in pretty powerful ways and have less margin before you start feeling the impacts of low testosterone and too high cortisol in your body
- Two really critical basics for hormone production, whether you're an endurance athlete or not, and extra important if you are:
  - **Sleep** is your superpower in a number of ways, one being that the majority of your hormone production happens during it. Not enough quality sleep hits you in two ways: it raises stress hormones like cortisol and adrenaline, and it hurts the production of the good hormones, which happens at night while you sleep. Everything else works better with enough sleep. Stay off the devices so there's no blue light in your eyes late at night, because it does affect sleep quality, and don't eat too late, because that absolutely hurts your sleep quality and your hormone production
  - Fuel and nutrition, its own complicated in-depth topic, but a couple of absolute basics:
    - Major thing number one, cut out three things: sugar; highly processed carbohydrates, and highly processed food of any kind, ideally; and toxic fats, including polyunsaturated fats. The main sources are kind of the nastier oils: canola, vegetable, corn, peanut, safflower and sunflower oil, most of them negative because they're packed with polyunsaturated fats. So stay away from sugar, stay away from processed carbs as much as possible, and cut out the toxic fats, especially things like fried food
    - Major thing number two, get enough good fats, the building blocks your body uses to make hormones: healthy sources of saturated and monounsaturated fat. Favorite sources personally: coconut oil for saturated fat, organic red palm oil for both, and olive oil for monounsaturated fat, three that really stand alone as great sources of healthy fats for your hormones. Other good sources include pasteurized [?] dairy and eggs
    - Sugar has a very significant direct impact on testosterone: if you make only one change and just cut out sugar, you'll be doing better than you have been. Doing these things pretty well hits the major areas from a hormonal standpoint
