> **TL;DR** — Winter can beat you down or be a tool for growth: focus extra on sleep and hydration, for most people about a gallon a day, then attack it by going out into the cold with little clothing, building brown fat and lifting your mental state.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 140, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- Mondays are a great chance to get after it in a new way. If you dread them and going to work, find something you don't hate going to, in a whole world of opportunities: maybe a new career, maybe just a new company or mindset. Life is too short for that kind of bullshit. Even if it's not instant, start the process, so Mondays become a new chance to kick ass, grow and do something you enjoy
- **Story:** in Salt Lake City, the first valley snow came yesterday, and about 14 degrees on waking this morning: a little taste of winter
- Winter can really drag on us, but like other challenges in life, we can let it beat us down or take it as a tool for growth, the preferred way to see it. Some people just dread it, and it has its challenges, but it's such an awesome time to grow, attacking it instead of going into hibernation mode
- A few tips that seem kind of minor but make a big difference in how winter impacts us:
  - **Sleep.** Not enough of it, which is not news, affects body and mind in profound ways. It makes it harder for your body to maintain its temperature, so you feel colder and let winter kick your ass: plenty of sleep is one of the ways to literally stay warmer and physically not feel the cold as much
  - Personally, winter can be hard on the mental and emotional state. In summer, feeling down, you can walk out into the warm sun; in winter, that state seemingly can be much more challenged. One of the most surefire ways to feel more down, stressed and less happy is not getting enough sleep, so short sleep in winter compounds the two, which can lead to really not good things for our mental state
  - So, for literal warmth and mentally, enough high quality sleep becomes extra important in winter. Good sleep hygiene: shut down screens a couple hours before bed, and eat early rather than right before bed, which will disrupt your sleep
  - **Hydration** really can impact your core temperature too. In summer, dehydration in the heat puts you at a greater risk of heat exhaustion and heat stroke. In winter it makes you feel colder: reduced blood flow and reduced vasodilation, so your blood vessels aren't as expanded, especially in your extremities. Feeling colder tends to sometimes drain our energy and drag on our mental state and physical performance
  - Give these two extra focus as winter comes. In summer we're more proactive about seeking a drink; in winter, a lot of times people drink less, not feeling that urge as much. So be extra diligent: use logical thought to stay on top of it
  - For most people, shoot for a gallon a day, depending on how much you weigh. Significantly less than 200 pounds, like 120, you could probably get away with less, say three quarters of a gallon; significantly more than 200, you might want a little more than a gallon
- Another way to attack winter: literally **attacking it**
  - Cold exposure, a cold shower or going out in the cold, makes your body respond in a number of ways, one being that it builds your brown fat. White fat is the typical fat stores we have in most places, not necessarily something we want more of; brown fat, we definitely want more of
  - We have much, much less of it, in little stores in a few areas: where a warm towel wrapped around your neck would sit, where you'd place things to keep warm. Babies have more brown fat, and then we kind of lose it
  - Brown fat is basically our body's warmth engine: the more we have, the more easily we stay warm, literally warmer in a colder temperature. Every time you expose yourself to cold, your body responds by building it. It's basically a warmth muscle
- **Story:** getting out in the cold by wearing a tank top or a t-shirt all winter long, shoveling snow or going to the store. That brings up conversations where people say they just don't deal with the cold well. That's bullshit: we're not born with a predetermined level of how we adapt to the cold. Your thyroid can affect it, of course, but one factor is just adaptability: you are far more adaptable to the cold than you think
- For most people with that "I just get cold" mindset, one of the big ways to change it is to literally expose your body to cold, not go out all bundled up: it builds your cold muscle. Cold showers, cold baths or ice baths, and the favorite, one of the best and most simple ways to attack winter, which almost nobody does: going out in the cold with very little clothes on
  - Of course, don't be insane: no 10-mile hike in the mountains in just shorts your first time ever, dying of hypothermia. Be smart, be safe
  - When it's cold enough, give it a shot: a walk, a hike, even around the neighborhood, in a t-shirt or no shirt. Your body will adapt more than you think, short and long term
- Like cold showers, cold exposure is so powerful for raising your mental and emotional state, your sort of vibration level. Walk, hike or work out in the cold with less clothes: long term, your body adapts and builds brown fat; today, you feel that so powerful mental and emotional boost
- It seems so simple, but when it gets cold, one of the best things we can do is go out into the teeth of it: lean into it, as with other life challenges. When this cold front reaches you, don't bundle up extra and stay inside: go out into it. It sets a whole different tone for the winter and your response. Winter asks us to either shy away, hibernate and get smaller, or lean in and get strong, and then you feel the physical and mental benefits
- Beyond hydration and sleep, the big thing above all is to lean into it: get out in the cold and practice exposing yourself to it. **Story:** continually amazed, getting older, in life and in working with other people, at how adaptable we are as creatures
- We can adapt to really shitty circumstances, which kind of leaves people stuck sometimes: a lot of times people stay in shitty physical or mental conditions because they've adapted to "this is the way it is". We gotta be careful of that, but more importantly we're adaptable in a positive way. When we ask our body and being to adapt, it will, as with working out or improving your diet: we gotta have faith that it will, and do the work that leads to it
- As winter comes, have a determination to attack it. Winter will make you stronger, an opportunity to lean into the challenge and grow: "I can promise you", attacking it instead of hibernating, you'll get so much more out of it. With all the bullshit happening right now, the "scandemic nonsense", who knows what this winter will bring; it's likely to bring a more challenging mental and physical health situation, with all the lockdowns. Don't let it push you down

The suggestion is to do the same: you'll get so much more out of this winter and be in a better mental and emotional state

A lot of guys take cold showers on the regular now after hearing about them here or elsewhere: one of the best ways to upgrade your mental state and fight depression and anxiety. Winter takes cold up another level: cold showers and ice baths are great, but an hour or two working out, hiking or walking in the cold is next level
