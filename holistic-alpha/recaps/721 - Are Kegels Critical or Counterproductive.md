> **TL;DR** — Kegels are both critical and counterproductive: the pelvic floor needs to be strong and relaxed, so balance Kegels with relaxing it through the day and holding ancestral squats, and get out into nature.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 721, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- Searches, experts and social media ads, a ton since they can so easily target men now, often say one of two things. Some teach Kegels to get stronger and improve stamina. Other experts say don't do Kegels: they increase tension and make everything worse, and tension is the enemy. Lots of grays lie in between
- Are Kegels, or root squeezes, critical or counterproductive? Both: "both and neither, I guess, depending on how you want to look at that"
- The root muscle is a number of muscles we can kind of think of as a group, like overall leg strength. Muscles work well when both strong and flexible, and not well when weak or tense. The root muscle too
- **Strong and relaxed.** "It really actually bugs me" how many places talk about only one or the other, and how few people seem to understand that the reality is in the middle: strong, flexible, with little or no tension
- Only ever doing Kegels, with nothing that intentionally and effectively relaxes the pelvic floor, you're gonna run into problems: maybe discomfort or pain, and very likely worse stamina challenges
- Conversely, if relaxing is the only thing you ever do, you're also gonna kind of run into problems. Being really relaxed is great, but as with any muscles, relaxed and not very strong won't serve you very well
- To get everything you'd like out of your sexuality (strong erections, great sex, stamina and lasting, transmuting lots of energy into success in life and confidence), the pelvic floor needs to be both strong and relaxed. So Kegels are indeed critical, but only critical and positive if and when balanced with relaxing and relieving tension
- One of a number of ways to relax it: tune in throughout the day, the way you might notice you're slouching and sit up a little. Relaxing and letting go a little, repeatedly, can be a really effective balance
- **Ancestral squats**: just holding a deep squat, how we would be chilling around a fire. The term was probably first heard from a friend, Scott ("I believe so")
  - Holding and relaxing into it is one of the best things personally found to powerfully release the pelvic floor, so important to balance the strength side and for stamina and control: "one of the most important practices we can possibly have"
  - It is not squeezing the pelvic floor or a strong push: just letting go and relaxing
  - Maybe one, two or three sets or whatever through the day, shooting for 5–10 minutes total, something like that. You can gradually work it up
- Then absolutely do Kegels too: incorporate pelvic floor strength into your workouts, and you can incorporate it into meditation breathing, "cock breathing", essentially incorporating the root muscle into breathing that draws it up into the rest of your body
- Don't settle for someone who says only one side is important. Told that legs only need to be as tight, tense and strong as possible, or only relaxed, you would see the folly. But a lot of guys, because of the kind of mystery around sexuality, don't often see it there. Be strong, be relaxed: nowhere is it more important than the pelvic floor. Work on being very, very strong and very, very relaxed
- Nature, whatever it looks like, will serve you well. **Story:** the last episode was recorded in Red Rocks, this one next to a river. Get out into nature and keep stacking bricks in your life
