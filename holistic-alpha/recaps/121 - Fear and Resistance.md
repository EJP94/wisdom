> **TL;DR** — At its root resistance is fear, mostly of the unknown, and the world now says be afraid and stay where you are, so fight it with daily work, cold showers, grinding and the little moments, starting with willingness, the only thing you need.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 121, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Resistance** holds us back from acting, from being the man we want to be and know we can be. It shows up in lots of forms, but at the real root of it, resistance is fear: we fear, so we don't act
- In a lot of cases we fear the unknown. Changing our diet or body, building that business or relationship: mostly we fear change because it's unknown, and that stops us from taking action so often
- So much of what is coached and suggested here is about moving past that fear to take action: not the individual technique, but fundamentally shifting your relationship with fear and resistance inside you
- Everything in our lives is an inside game. Being the man you want to be has nothing to do with the outside world and everything to do with what's inside you, which determines your actions, how your relationship goes, how you take care of your body. The number one thing that keeps you from going where you want and living the day-to-day you want is fear and resistance
- One huge concern right now: so many people are getting in a state of fear, and their resistance is getting so much stronger. The world situation has maybe accelerated it, made it more pronounced: the world wants you held back, literally saying stay at home, and wants you afraid. We've gotta fight that, and it's about the day-to-day actions
- Doing the work every single day is not about looks: no measuring biceps on social media. It's about being strong, having the ability and willingness to do what you want in your life: that's why eating well and training your body matter. Being in good shape is nice, but a huge part of training daily is battling resistance daily, saying "Yes, I'm gonna fight today"
- **Cold showers**: a lot of guys already take them, a lot started recently after hearing them mentioned on this podcast or somewhere else. If you haven't, please do: they're about fear and resistance. Starting your day with one is literally saying "Yes, I will fight. No, I will not be afraid". There are anti-inflammatory effects and awesome things in your body, but probably the most important part is standing up to fear and resistance in that moment
- One action gets you on the path: one push-up gets you moving, one meal of eating better puts you on the path of eating better, one minute of a cold shower on the path of beating fear. Once on that path, it's a whole different ball game. Guys say over and over again how life-changing this one simple habit can be
- Get your daily training in, work hard. Being willing to physically grind, get uncomfortable and keep going is a powerful, powerful way to shift the fear inside you and stand up taller against it. Training every day also seems the best way to get the best physical results, but that's another topic. Against fear and resistance, daily physical work has a ripple effect throughout you as a being, your actions and how you feel
- Nobody knows but you, especially now with gyms closed: nobody's gonna push you along or be disappointed in you moment to moment, and you don't have the energy of other people at a gym. It's gotta be up to you. Do the work physically and you'll feel such a difference in how willing you are to step into the unknown
- The unknown is discomfort, and we fear both. A lot of times that's why we don't push harder when we train: mostly not because we can't, but because we fear the discomfort, not knowing what we can or can't do. The more often we push into that unknown space, the more comfortable we get with it, and it carries through to everything. The more often you step into a cold shower, the more you convince yourself: "I can step into the unknown. I can step into discomfort"
- Do the daily work not for your looks or weight, but because the fear and resistance inside you is there and is not going anywhere. The best way to kick its ass is the little things every single day, each a little jab right to its face. When you don't, you leave yourself open: you get smaller, resistance gets bigger, things seem harder, you're less strong and confident
- For so many people, life is going one way or the other, significantly, as a video posted the day before also said. Mental health issues are skyrocketing: "I heard calls to suicide hotlines are up 900% here in Utah". Obesity is going up: how many people do you know who gained weight over the last four, five months, are eating worse, drinking more? But a lot of people are going the other direction
- As individuals we often face moments where the path shifts one way or the other. A thought, also talked over with some people: this pandemic has been basically that point for everybody at the same time. So make sure you're getting stronger, healthier, more willing to step into the unknown
- If you've been going the other way, that's okay: start now. For those struggling, it's fear and resistance: in so many ways we don't act because we don't know what will happen when we act. We've got to be willing to step into that unknown
- The situation, governments and pressure from various people are basically saying two things: be afraid and stay where you are. "Fuck that": move, face fear and battle it. Be a person who leads, showing people they don't have to be afraid, that they can step into discomfort, go out, live their life and not be stuck
- If you've been struggling, gaining weight, getting out of shape, stuck, two things:
  - It's okay. Be gentle on yourself, don't beat yourself up: regret about how much weight you've gained or how miserable you are doesn't help. Forgive yourself, forget the past, be in this moment
  - Make a decision today and do one thing. Once on the path, it's so much easier to stay on it, "I promise": one step, then the habit of one step after another. You can only do one thing
- Willingness is the only thing you need: no special knowledge, skills or equipment. If you truly have the willingness to be strong, healthy and the best you can be, it leads you to the solutions. Willing to do the work, you can dramatically change your body, your mental well-being, whatever is dragging you down: "I know you can"
- With willingness you're gonna get there no matter what, but the more you figure out on your own, the longer it takes. **Story:** 15 years figuring stuff out, step by step, grinding, learning and studying. The less you learn on your own, the more you can dial into the critical knowledge and habits
- Across the five pillars of power, fitness, nutrition, hormones, sex and mind, plugging where we leak power and building it adds up to completely shifting our relationship with fear and resistance. Besides better shape and feeling better, most importantly you're willing to step into discomfort and the unknown: you fear less, act more, are more confident, and when those change, everything changes
  - There's a compound effect: every day in that new position you feel the change more. You'll see some changes in 30 days, and can see dramatic changes in a short period of time, but applied for six months, a year, two years, "What I can tell you happens is dramatic, complete changes" toward the real, powerful version of you. That's why we do the work every day: it adds up
- A lot of guys are doing the work, seen in Bootcamp and among guys talked to. But it's not just physical training: it also means being willing to face and process your emotions, to meditate and find the quiet space within you, to have the conversations in your relationship, to eat clean. Food is fuel, it is not entertainment
- Do the work in the little moments: the choice of what to eat or buy, the moment you decide to get moving, to start the conversation, to sit down to meditate
  - Whether you meditate 10 minutes, 20 or five, the part that matters by far the most, "like a thousand times more than all the rest of it", is the two or five seconds when you sit down and hit play: with Headspace, for example, pull it up, hit play, set your phone aside. Those five seconds are a huge determining factor in your life
  - The choice at the store is very, very brief, but it ripples out to all the rest of the moments in your life. You know the ones: do the work in them, and it adds up
