> **TL;DR** — Tomorrow starts tonight, so debrief your nights for the little levers, close the day's open loops with a judgment-free snapshot recap, and, maybe even more importantly, acknowledge that today's over, it was enough and you're enough.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 720, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- How we end our night has a huge impact on the next day, undeniably seen personally and "over and over and over and over again with nearly every coaching client", in some way or another. The next day actually starts the night before: tonight's wrap-up is going to actually determine how tomorrow starts, which might be emotionally, energetically, when you get up, what you do or don't do. "It is a little lever" that opens or closes big doors.
- Seen with so many guys: an early evening going a certain way gets them off track and they use porn later at night, or maybe get distracted by the internet or otherwise off track and stay up super late, later than they should, want or intend, affecting the next day. Examples are limitless, but "truly tomorrow starts tonight".
- A lot of this is very unique to you, so with intention and awareness, **debrief your nights**: what at night often leads to what the next day. When a morning goes really well (feeling great, maybe hormones in a great place, good morning wood, strong, disciplined, up early, workout done), ask how last night led to that; when you get off track, debrief that too.
- Pay attention to the little levers in your night; they vary a ton. One example seen: taking five minutes between work and home to be really intentional, maybe meditating five minutes or setting intentions for the night, can have a huge impact, one way or the other.
- Come up often personally, and heard from clients: an underlying angst, as the day wears on, that not enough happened or got done today, or maybe about big-picture things you want to change that haven't changed yet.
- **Open loops**: our mind does not like open loops and wants to close them. Promising to tell someone something later opens a loop in their mind; never addressed, it creates a little uneasiness, depending on interest. Your mind does this with your own life: planning tomorrow opens a bunch of loops, and the day probably opens a bunch more.
- It seems this often affects us at the end of the day as uneasiness, one way being trouble getting to sleep, kept up by something underlying you're not conscious of: the angst over open loops keeps us from being willing to actually end the day.
- Tool one, a **snapshot recap**, usable anytime: at the end of a workday before home life, or in bed at night, "a great time to do it". For 30 seconds to a couple of minutes, close your eyes and let snapshots of the day flow through your mind from start to finish, up to that moment, without thinking about it, judging it or feeling bad. Maybe just five, 10, 15 snapshots: little video clips or, if you're not visual, a sort of awareness.
- Even if things you wanted didn't get done, recalling how the day went "really tends to close a lot of those open loops": you may fall asleep easier or, done earlier in the evening, move on more easily with your night. Experiment with when; at a minimum, a suggestion: try it when you get in bed. No judgment, neither bad about what got done nor really great about what went great: just pass through as an observer.
- Tool two, very simple but "very, very powerful": be willing to acknowledge "today's over, today was enough, I'm enough", let go of the day and start again tomorrow. **Story:** for a very long time, this was not a tool in the mental toolbox.
- Often, in an underlying way, we don't do that: a background program says I didn't get this or that done, I'm not enough because this thing hasn't changed. Take 10 seconds, at some point or maybe multiple points in the evening, maybe even verbally, at least in your mind and heart: today's done, it was enough, I'm enough, letting it go, moving on.
- That acknowledgment "very well may make the difference" in going to bed on time, falling asleep, doing a meditation at the end of the night [?], or ending the night more disciplined, grounded and centered. Heard from a lot of clients, and definitely felt personally: not making that statement can often lead to staying up much later than we should, from an unconscious hesitation to let go of the day while other things might still get done.
- Of the two, the acknowledgment is used more regularly personally and probably depended on more, maybe even more important. The "I'm enough" part matters: probably no one ever met personally has the knowing, "a hundred percent in every cell of their body", that they're enough; we have little layers of thinking we're not good enough. Those 10 seconds are huge, "a game changer", so simple; it seems you might be surprised at how impactful that can be.
