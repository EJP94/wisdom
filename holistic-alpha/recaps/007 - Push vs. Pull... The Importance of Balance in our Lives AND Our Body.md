> **TL;DR** — When something in the body hurts, the root cause is almost never that spot but a tight or weak connected or opposite muscle, as with low back pain or climbing without push-ups, so consider balance in your body as in your life.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 7, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Balance** between the parts of life is really critical, and it matters in your body specifically too
- Over the years, one finding has been kind of counterintuitive but almost universally true: when something starts hurting, it's almost never that thing that's the actual root cause. **Story:** thankfully no serious injuries, just little things here and there, some taking a few weeks to recover from
- When your low back hurts, so often it's not your back at all that's weak or tight, but usually your glutes and hamstrings: tight, they pull on your low back
- So often you feel the pain but aren't conscious of the tightness: unless you're closely paying attention, it's easy to miss that the area next to it, or balancing it, is kind of the root cause
- Things that can make your low back hurt: standing or being on your feet a really long time, and things that really engage your glutes and hamstrings, especially if you're not used to it
- Often, sitting too much tightens the front of the hips, the psoas muscles, throwing your body kind of out of balance front to back and causing back pain often enough. When the psoas, a really deep abdominal muscle, is tight, people probably extremely rarely feel pain there: what hurts is what it throws out of balance
- **Story:** climbing almost daily, a lot at the gym, worked the upper-body pull muscles a lot while the push muscles didn't carry their weight, which overstressed the pull muscles and made them sore. Adding push-ups consistently, now alternated with climbing on a lot of solo days, made the climbing better, harder, longer and less painful, with a stronger feeling, because of the balance. Not surprising: too much pulling makes push muscles weak comparatively
- Consider balance in your life, too: between individual, family and work time, leisure and activities. Getting out of balance causes issues: working nonstop maybe with your spouse or kids, playing nonstop maybe with your work
- Balance between life's parts seems a little more obvious, maybe because we hear and think about it more, though a reminder still helps. Balance within the body, which affects us a lot, we really don't seem to hear a lot about
- The encouragement: when something is tweaked, sore or hurting, consider that it may be a balance thing, the opposite or connected muscle, maybe the next muscles in the chain. With your back, it's not a front-to-back thing: a lot of times it's above or below in the chain, usually below, like your glutes and hamstrings, and even your calves and feet, causing low back pain through tightness in that chain
- You can try this: different rollers work well, but some kind of massage roller works great (personally, a lot of different ones get used). Rolling out your body reveals tight spots you weren't conscious of, and it's like, "whoa. That's really tight"
- Think about connected things and the opposite side, balance-wise: push versus pull, front versus back, left versus right. So often the root cause is not the one you think
- **Story:** realizing this helped with pain and soreness: overall health and wellness got tremendously better, feeling strong without tight, sore, painful areas, as it helped figure out what was going on and deal with it more long term
