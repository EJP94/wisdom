> **TL;DR** — The 80/20 of testosterone is four areas, three keys each: sleep, low stress, removing toxins like fragrance; good fats, no toxic fats, no sugar; less ejaculation, rarely if ever alone, an active sex life; daily activity, intensity and compound resistance work.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 2, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- The **80/20 principle** basically means that 80% of the benefit or results come from 20% of the activity, in everything from personal life to business to milk production in Wisconsin. The book *80/20 Sales and Marketing* by Perry Marshall teaches a lot more about it
- For testosterone, the 20% that gives 80%, the "most bang for your hormonal buck", is four areas with three keys each, in no particular order, since where you get the most depends on your particular circumstance
- Lifestyle:
  - Sleep is absolutely crucial. Most hormone production happens during sleep, and too little sleep raises cortisol and stress hormones, which really don't coexist with testosterone: more stress hormones generally means lower testosterone. Enough sleep gives double the benefit, too little hurts both ways
  - Keep stress down, since high cortisol impacts testosterone. Sleep, meditation, exercise and a few supplements help. Personally, the favorite is ashwagandha
  - Remove toxins. The most impactful one you're probably being impacted by is fragrance, in body wash, soap, shampoo, deodorant, cologne, anything listing it: it's highly, highly estrogenic. Keep away from plastic and its chemicals too. There are other areas, but keeping fragrance and plastic out of your life as much as possible gives the most bang for your buck
- Nutrition: magnesium matters, with pumpkin seeds a great source, and cruciferous vegetables like broccoli and cabbage remove excess estrogens, but it seems to boil down to three keys:
  - Enough good fats, saturated and monounsaturated, from clean sources: coconut oil, olive oil, organic and sustainably produced palm oil, pasture-raised butter, ghee. They're incredibly important for your brain, and what your body produces hormones from. One big mistake a lot of people make is avoiding fat, and so not getting enough good fats. Of the healthy nuts, macadamia nuts and pumpkin seeds are, personally, two of the only ones really liked, for their fat profile and some other things
  - Avoid toxic fats "like the plague": basically the polyunsaturated fats, very unstable, which can very easily and will harm your body and hormones. Fried and fast food are absolutely chock full of them, as are a lot of processed and packaged foods. Peanut, canola, vegetable and sunflower oil, among a lot of these oils, are a really potent source
  - Sugar is absolutely huge, with a direct, significant negative impact on testosterone. In one study, men given sugary syrup saw their testosterone drop 25% and stay lower for hours after. Daily sugar is absolutely going to impact your hormones, and already does every time you consume meaningful amounts. Avoid it like the plague
- Sex absolutely impacts your testosterone and hormones in major ways:
  - Avoid excess ejaculation: too often, no matter how, it drains your hormones, your life energy, your "qi, as some people call it", in major ways
  - Stop ejaculating alone, which has the worst impact on your hormones: avoid it ever if at all possible, or do it very, very infrequently
  - Be active and fuel your sexual energy, with one partner, yourself or multiple partners. It doesn't mean not having sex. Far enough along, it means separating orgasm and ejaculation, so you can be highly sexually active (in general, not with multiple people, though that's your choice). You can absolutely work towards it, and the more you conserve semen, the more you feel it in your hormones, life force energy and mind. The encouragement: learn to ride the edge of not ejaculating while getting the benefits of sexual activity, and eventually to separate ejaculation and orgasm
- Fitness:
  - Daily activity: you've got to move every day. Hormones are your body's response to its environment: run into a bear on a trail and it spikes adrenaline and cortisol, both hormones. That's largely what hormones really are, in a lot of ways your body's signal and response mechanism. Just sitting signals that you don't really need testosterone. Daily activity signals that you need to be strong and active, so you need testosterone and not too much estrogen
  - Intensity probably makes a bigger difference in your hormonal response than anything else, and you can feel it. Rather than an hour or an hour and a half of slow, low cardio, you'd be much better off going really hard for 15 minutes, like sprints with short breaks if you want to run. **Story:** going back and forth between climbing and push-ups, really hard, really fast. The harder you go in a short time, the more hormonal benefit
  - Resistance: you've got to get some weight, which doesn't mean lifting heavy all the time, every day, for hours. A couple of sets of some reps with the right activity can have amazing benefits. Use compound exercises with most of your body, especially your lower body: squats, a clean (like a clean and jerk), kettlebell swings, the kettlebell snatch. Whole body, some explosiveness and some weight give the most bang for your buck
- Each area holds a lot more detail, and where you get the very most bang for your buck may depend on your specific circumstances
