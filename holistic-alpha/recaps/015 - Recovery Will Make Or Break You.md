> **TL;DR** — Sustainable training maybe hinges most on an active role in recovery: roll out proactively, before tightness becomes soreness or injury, with a favorite tool, the cheap Gaiam Restore Pressure Point Massager, before and after workouts, since a relaxed body feeds a relaxed mind.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 15, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- Releasing pressure points and tightness in your muscles is so crucial for feeling good, performing and, more importantly, avoiding injury. Injury sucks: training that leads to injury leaves you worse off than not training
- One of the most important personal keys to fitness: training in a **sustainable, long-term way**, whose maybe most important part is listening to your body: getting rid of tightness and pressure points, taking an active role in recovery instead of waiting while things build up
- Rolling out, the favorite term: the foam roll has its place, but a couple of other tools seem a lot better and more versatile
- Staying on top of tightness and sore spots, you feel better and stronger and have longer, harder workouts. **Story:** tight muscles after a workout tend to mean worse sleep, maybe for you too. Tightness, a lot of times the kind we can't even really notice until we roll out, builds up until all of a sudden something gets really sore or injured. **Story:** the aim is to roll out at least a little every day
- **Story:** probably 15 different foam rolls, massage rollers, sticks and recovery tools were tried over the years, plus plenty of professional massages, and some have their place. The Roll Recovery, spring loaded with lacrosse wheels on both sides, is really awesome, but about 140 bucks and really only has sort of applications to kind of the big parts of your body. The favorite is the most basic and the very cheapest: the Gaiam Restore Pressure Point Massager, from the yoga wellness company, seven bucks on Amazon, probably 15 when bought, "the best seven bucks you've ever spent", used until a couple wore out. Keeping only it, without even professional massage, would be alright. Highly recommended, though any roller you have is a start
- Its three spiky balls make it kind of a combination of acupuncture and the release of rolling, and, a personal favorite, it really helps you notice what is tight
- **Be proactive**, the most important thing found with rollers, stretching and any recovery: wait until something's sore and it's too late. As they say, if you wait until you're thirsty to drink, you're already dehydrated; a pain signal means the area has gone beyond tight to really inflamed and hurting. Rolling over your main areas lets you find tight spots and address them before they get painful during activity
- **Story:** quick passes over the entire body, neck down through arms and legs, then working a little more into a sore, really tight spot with a pressure point, or using one of a couple of other favorite rollers
- Otherwise, or in addition, use a foam roll, another roller or a professional massage. Recovery is so critical for keeping your body in the game long term, even if you're not training hard. So often, as people age, areas start to hurt, which seemingly largely doesn't have to happen: it's largely built-up tension and tightness, worse over time unaddressed
- **Story:** before climbing that day, a good 20 minutes of rolling out everywhere loosened things up for a much, much better workout; some versions take two minutes or less of quick passes. The spiky balls' acupuncture kind of action really increases blood flow wherever you use it: perfect as a pre-workout warm-up, and great after, for loosening up and digging into sore spots
- A promise: you will feel the benefits in your body, and really in your mind too. **Story:** tight hamstrings, quads and legs especially lead to sort of an uneasiness, and uneasiness in the body translates to the mind. The more relaxed our body, the more relaxed our mind, and the other way too. We hopefully relax the mind with meditation, exercise and enough sleep; releasing tightness feeds the relaxation loop back from the body, for feeling better physically and mentally
