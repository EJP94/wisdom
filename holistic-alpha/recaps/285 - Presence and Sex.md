> **TL;DR** — Presence, being fully right now in mind, body, spirit and energy, is maybe the most important factor in sex; meditation, mindful edging, eyes-closed movement and grounding together build it, and presence eases anxiety, deepens connection and rapport, and grows with cold showers too.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 285, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Quote:** dictionary definitions of presence: current existence or occurrence; the area around a great personage; a person present in an impressive way; "a person's bearing, especially when it commands respectful attention"; "the quality of self-assurance and effectiveness that permits a performer to achieve a rapport with the audience"; "the state of being in a certain place, and not in some other place", as a planet in its orbit, or near the speaker, or where his thought is directed
- A few of these seem to touch on really important things. As defined here, presence is being fully right now in mind, body, spirit and energy together. Part of it is the mind on the present moment, not lost in past or future thoughts, worries or daydreams; but, as some definitions kind of allude to, presence is really kind of more than now-ness, and there's something energetic about it: around somebody with that quality you feel a different connection, rapport and energetic charge
- For most of us, most of our lives, we're very much not present a lot of the time, the mind on the past, the future, later today or an earlier conversation, and our energy split among those places. That impacts every moment of every day, hugely
- The more present, the more peace and power we feel, the better we connect, and the freer we are from the controlling, overwhelming feeling of negative or challenging emotions: they don't necessarily go away, but feel much less powerful
- Until we start a journey of meditation and other practices and develop this with intention, most of us have very little presence most of the time. Part of that is kind of modern society, our attention always drawn to 18 different places, messages and everything in our digital and physical world. So recognize presence very intentionally and come back to it: we don't create it, it's always with us, and other stuff gets in the way. It matters for living life the way we want, being effective, and connecting with people as deeply as possible
- In sex, without question, presence is maybe the most important factor for experiences that are not only pleasurable but deeply connecting, alone (masturbating, edging) or with a partner: the number one factor, more important than erection quality, your weight or your looks. Present, we connect with ourselves, our body and its feelings, energy and sensations, and with a partner's. It is only through being present that we make those connections
- You may have had the experience, alone masturbating or having sex with a partner, where you or your partner wasn't present, and with a partner sometimes it can be quite obvious: your wife mentally moved on to the next thing in your day, or you have, or you're freaking out about the last time you couldn't get an erection. So rarely are we fully present, and it makes all the difference in the world: fully connecting with the energies, sensations, bodies, emotions and spirits, yours and your partner's, is when sex becomes magic
- Unfortunately, a lot of how modern society portrays sex is the opposite, a very disconnected version: porn takes a big chunk of your being, mentally and energetically, off to another image, person or imagined scene
- Fantasy does too, one reason to move away from it, ideally as much as possible, when masturbating or edging: imagining somebody, specific or generic, necessarily, by definition, takes some of the awareness away from right now. You can't think about a person who isn't here if your full presence is here. Less of it means better sexual experiences: certainly important in edging, so critical with a partner
- With a partner, presence matters more than anything else. Fully present, lots of things can, "quote, go wrong" (awkwardness, something funky and laughing, not quite the erection you want, losing it) and it will almost always still be a wonderful, connecting experience. Conversely, everything can "quote go right", a great erection, porn-performance kind of sex that looks "quote, right", and feel very disconnected and disempowering, a lot of times, if either of you isn't present
- How to develop more presence:
  - Meditation, talked about over and over because it's absolutely critical: all of us should be meditating. Its benefits stretch into every aspect of life, without question into sex, with more presence in daily life and during sex
  - **Mindful edging**: no porn, ideally no fantasy, and ideally full awareness on what's happening in your body right now, the feelings, sensations, energy. Get away from the external and from wanting another scenario, and be with what is. Edging truly present versus not is light years different, with tremendous benefits even if you're single and expect a while before a partner, because of your life or your age. It translates to sex: "practice like you want to play", talked about before
  - Moving with your eyes closed, talked about before: physical training from yoga to bodyweight to kettlebells. Closing your eyes to the outer world brings you into right now, into your body, its energy and sensations. A still meditation is a great and important foundation, but doing this in movement translates presence into movement, and sex involves movement
  - Maybe the last one for now: very intentionally bring presence into sex with a partner. Married and about to have sex with your wife, take a little time at the start, even very, very brief, to find stillness and grounding in the present moment together: before you're out of your clothes, maybe a hug, face to face, forehead resting against hers, just breathing and being for a second; or lying in bed in that stillness
  - Grounding into your body (a recent episode covers returning to your body, a really powerful way to presence) is great alone; together, it brings both of you into presence. Its look may differ, but the idea is a little moment of stillness, connection and breathing before moving and doing all the things, which can really make a big impact on how it goes and how deeply you connect
- A lot of guys struggle at times, some of you surely, with some form of kind of sexual anxiety: how you'll "quote perform", erection quality, how long you'll last. All of it takes you away from presence: fully present, you're not worried about anything, and all of those things improve with presence. For erection struggles, lots of practices address it overall, but the very best thing in the moment is to let go of the worry and just be present
- Found over and over, personally and working with clients: present with the sensations and energy, everything gets better, feeling, erections, arousal management, lasting longer with better control, connection with yourself and your partner. Frankly, the same goes for life. **Story:** being more present is one of the fundamental things that has really changed the experience and the course of life
- We're never going to be 100% present all of the time; we all get pulled out occasionally. Most of us start from almost never really being present and gradually bring it into more of our day, life and sexual experiences. The more we tap into it, the more we stay and the easier it is to come back, and everything gets better, without question sex too
- Before sex, presence is what helps your partner feel connected with you, safe, secure and attracted, and you with them. Being fully present, not lost in some thought, worry or unprocessed emotion, is the greatest gift you can give somebody in that moment, a friend, a date, your wife, and the most powerful way to connect: on a date, with someone at the gym or the store you want to ask out, with your wife
- As in the definitions of bearing and of a performer's rapport: connecting with a date, a wife or a friend isn't a performance, but fully present, you establish rapport, with greater charisma and connection
- Being aware of your presence, practicing it and making it a priority has such a tremendous return on investment: it changes how life feels, how you function, connect and have sex, no question. We all have to do the work: sometimes the gym, mental work like writing or a project, the conversation you need to have, being vulnerable, or inner work on your relationship with yourself and how you are being in the world. It changes everything
- "Be present" can sound like trite or hollow words to some of you. Feel it as an experience and you know it can make life feel very different: sometimes after a couple, a few weeks or a month of meditation, plus regular mindful edging, eyes-closed movement and grounding into your body. Then you also notice times you're very much not present, caught up in some other place or time
- As soon as you realize you're not present, in a lot of ways you're present again, in the observer spot. That's the journey: pulled out, realizing it, coming back, again and again, and over time you spend more and more time present
- There is probably nothing with a greater impact on your relationships, and on how fulfilling and deeply connecting sex is, than building your presence: you connect with people, your partner and yourself differently. Work absolutely worth doing, with habits: meditation, and another really good one not mentioned in a while, cold showers
- Of the cold shower's lots of benefits, one is this sort of quiet place of presence: it's nearly impossible to stand in one thinking about bills, a project, a house chore or yesterday's screw-up. You're just right now, with that sensation, and each time helps carry that quality into the day, encounters with people, and certainly sex
- Do the inner work, the outer work, all the work: sit down, be still, and get after it for your mental health, finding that place of presence
