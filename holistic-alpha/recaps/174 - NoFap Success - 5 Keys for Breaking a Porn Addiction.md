> **TL;DR** — Porn is a short-term tactic but a terrible long-term strategy; to break it, find a strong why, deal with the triggers behind five-second decisions, do something else instead, practice mindfulness, and build willpower with daily habits like cold showers.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 174, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- Mondays are an awesome day for a fresh start. A suggestion if you've been kind of off track, or want to add to an already good routine: make it small, one or two things, but make sure they happen every day this week
  - If you're not already taking a cold shower every day, "I would highly suggest" starting: maybe that's this week's one goal. Keep doing your other things, possibly a little more flexibly, but focus on the one thing. Or, if you're not already meditating, maybe commit to downloading Headspace and doing at least a five minute meditation every day this week
  - Small changes made every single day are huge, because once you get the habit, you can grow it. One set of push-ups every day, and your body and mind kind of get the signal that this is the way things are. Then one set becomes two, becomes five; the cold shower becomes cold shower plus training plus good nutrition, one little piece at a time. Make each new piece small and doable, and do it every single day without fail
- Porn sucks: it always drags on our life long term, "100% of the time. Period." It's a short-term tactic to satisfy certain physical, emotional and mental things, to get that dopamine response, but a terrible long-term strategy: over time it drags on you in powerful ways, sometimes sooner than others, but it always happens. In talks with guys from 18-year-olds to guys in their 50s, inevitably at some point porn rears its ugly head and they feel the negative effects. **NoFap** is about getting off that habit
- Five overall keys, not really a comprehensive step-by-step guide, that seem likely to really help:
  - **Key one, a strong why.**
    - A lot of guys talked to become unable to get hard when trying to have sex with their girlfriend or wife. Depending on how intense and frequent the porn is, it's inevitable: it might happen after a year, five years, 10 or 15, but at some point, because of what porn does to us, it happens. Maybe it's already happening, a huge motivation to change, as guys report. Or you see it down the road: married 10 years, into porn since you were a kid, still kind of looking fairly regularly and still able to have sex with your wife, then a year from now you can't get an erection. Nothing else changed, but the porn added up
    - All these whys kind of apply, but identify what really motivates you in particular, and let it carry you through
    - Another why: the power and drive to get stuff done and create the life you want. A combined porn, masturbation and ejaculation habit drains your life force, your punching power, in every area: relationships, interacting with women or anybody, your confidence, your drive, your ability to turn on that fire, and so your finances and every aspect of life. It happens to guys in different degrees, but with that habit you feel weaker and softer, give up easier, aren't getting after it in life. **Story:** been there, years ago, in all the things talked about in this podcast, and grown through it
    - Of the lots of other whys, find what scares you the most or what you identify with the most, and focus on it
  - **Key two, identify and deal with triggers.** While full on in the habit and feeling fine about it, triggers may not be as easy to identify. Once you start to move away from it, or want to, start identifying them: if after a week effectively staying away you get that urge and fall back, understand what triggered it
    - A lot of times it's an emotional trigger for guys: stress, used as a release, or feeling down about a part of life where you're not doing what you should. A lot of times we use it as an escape from feeling things we don't want to feel: stress, guilt, whatever
    - Other triggers: scrolling Instagram where you follow a bunch of bikini girl accounts, maybe just that visual triggers you
    - Make no mistake, you are growing through this. Everything happens in life for a reason: if you have faced or are facing porn addiction, it happened to give you the opportunity to learn what you needed to learn and grow through it
    - Then deal with the triggers themselves. Bikini accounts: unfollow them, or don't get on Instagram. Emotions: implement strategies to face them. Say it's Saturday afternoon, you're alone, stressed about school projects, a trigger you've identified, and the urge comes up: get the project done, or meditate, maybe a three-minute, five-minute SOS kind of meditation to get through the urge, redirect that energy and maybe calm the stress. For guilt or shame about part of your life or the past, meditation can be a great strategy to step back from that thought pattern and come back to the present moment
    - Mental or physical environment triggers: understanding them will go a long way to help you change, because those are the moments
    - We live our lives with the result of **five second decisions**. Five seconds at the grocery store choosing what to buy, and we live with the results the rest of the 24 hours. Choosing a water or a beer: the time spent drinking the beer doesn't even matter, the five seconds of the choice do. You might spend an hour on porn, and that matters of course, but it's not the hour that makes the difference: it's the five seconds of the decision. Knowing your triggers helps you get control over them
    - Some five seconds are much more important than others: not remotely tempted, hours or days go by without one. Then that Saturday afternoon anxious feelings come up, or some visual on Instagram triggers you, and you either redirect, maybe work out, meditate, get the project done, or lose that five-second battle and go down the porn path
  - **Key three, do something else instead.** It's very hard to just stop doing something. Say "don't think about a pink elephant", and it pops into your mind to some degree: a part of your mind literally doesn't hear the "don't", just the rest. So instead of telling yourself you won't look at porn when those triggers come up, which keeps you in "don't do it, don't do it", get into an instead kind of thought: how will you deal with that trigger instead, how will you spend that time instead?
    - It could be anything, very dependent on you. Maybe you wish you were writing or practicing more music, and could take porn's time for it, maybe with more motivation if you weren't ejaculating to porn all the time. Dive into a project, into growing yourself: business, a relationship, a hobby. And even in the moment: what will you spend those 20 minutes doing instead?
  - **Key four, mindfulness.** Its impact over the years of your life is impossible to overstate. When you get, not really control, but an understanding of your mind and a practice to manage and use it, everything changes
    - That critical five-second decision to turn porn on comes from within, not from something outside you. So much of it is patterns: habitual thought and emotional patterns, needing that dopamine boost from porn on a regular basis. The more you practice mindfulness, the more effectively you break old patterns and peel away those layers
    - One of the most important things for NoFap is daily meditation, so critical to get in touch with your mind, and more importantly with the quiet space inside you. No matter what thoughts run through your head, a quieter, deeper space is always there. Meditation isn't about stopping thoughts, which come and go, or about controlling every thought: it's practicing making your way to that place underneath, always peaceful, always powerful. It's not instant, but with practice you find your way there, let go of old habits, patterns and thoughts that take you down roads you don't want, and make way for peace in the present moment and for new ways of being
    - Every single guy who has talked on the phone has heard about meditation, and the show has covered it a number of times before. Breaking a porn addiction, it is absolutely critical to start, and there's no excuse not to: three minutes, five, ten. Personally, the favorite way to start a meditation practice is an app called Headspace, really awesome and recommended; you can also just close your eyes and follow your breath
    - Another trigger, if we really think about it: not being in the moment. In the past we feel bad about something we did; in the future we worry about a project, a report, a bill. So often that creates emotional responses that will often be the triggers that turn you to porn. The more you can be now, the more you release old patterns and manage your response to what's happening inside you
    - A great way to be more now is the book The Power of Now, recommended before and still: one of a lot of ways to practice presence, a really good place to start, helping you understand and experience truly being now, in the moment, in a much more powerful way, and "I know that'll be beneficial in your journey to kick porn"
  - **Key five, habits that build your willpower.** Some of this comes down to willpower. We don't need it forever, just long enough to make things a habit, truly ingrained, with the porn habit replaced. To carry you through that period, you need habits that support your overall willpower stores
    - A cold shower every day has a huge discipline factor. There are physical benefits and mental benefits, and one of them is that it builds willpower: you choose your path intentionally even though it's uncomfortable in the moment, because you know it leads to a good place. Every day you take one, you feel stronger against urges toward that poor five-second decision, and against **resistance**, the resistance within us that wants us lazy, on the porn path, pulled in directions we don't want. A cold shower helps you be stronger against resistance, "period"
    - Daily physical training. Where you're at in strength or condition doesn't matter: what matters is instituting the habit. Not exercising at all? Maybe commit to two sets of push-ups every day, more on days you feel stronger, or running every day if you really like it. You choose to do it though it's a little uncomfortable in the moment, because it'll make you stronger: pretty much porn, where resisting is a little uncomfortable when you're used to it but worth it in the long run. The more such things we do, the more we build that muscle
    - This comes back to doing something else instead. Say you have a 90-day period in mind to hit the reset button: instead of porn, a cold shower every day, physical training every day, a gallon of water every day. Or meditate every day for 90 days: there's no instant dopamine fix from sitting down to meditate; you get the long-term benefit, so it builds willpower too. Maybe uncomfortable or not that desirable short term, it's strategically powerful long term
    - Then you'll have more willpower when that five-second urge comes up on a Saturday afternoon, alone, scrolling your phone
- There's more to this, of course. From so many guys talked to, and from personal experience years ago: this is not something we talk about, which sucks. You've very likely done some internet searching, but probably haven't talked to friends or family about it. For a lot of guys it lives in their head, and that makes it even harder
- Breaking powerful old patterns that are really stuck, it's really helpful to have somebody on board who understands, to keep you accountable and motivated. If you've tried to stop before without really succeeding, that's a great sign that someone to guide you through the process is probably a good idea
- The aim is to get as many guys off porn as possible: seemingly, as you know too, we're better and stronger without it, and we need all the men we can get right now to be strong and create the lives they want
- Have faith. Guys sometimes panic and think they'll never be able to do this. Guys talked to, say 20 years old, all of a sudden can't get it up with their girlfriend, know it's the porn, and think "I'm 20. This is gonna be this way for the rest of my life. I'm screwed". No: we are a product of our habits, and when you change your habits, it takes a little bit of time, but things will and can change. With porn or any other aspect of life, go one day at a time: when you start stacking days, you can make things change in a dramatic way
