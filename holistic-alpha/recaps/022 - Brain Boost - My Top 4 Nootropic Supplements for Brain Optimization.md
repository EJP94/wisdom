> **TL;DR** — Natural nootropics are highly recommended over synthetic racetams; personally, the top four: Huperzine A, 50–150 micrograms; Alpha GPC, 600 milligrams once or twice a day; acetyl-L-carnitine for brain mitochondria; ginkgo biloba for memory, around 120 milligrams one to three times a day.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 22, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- A **nootropic** is basically anything that's really a cognitive enhancer, enhancing brain power. Technically there are a couple of qualifications, but any cognitive enhancer is generally called that. Four, grown to love over the years, made a meaningful difference in the brain personally, and "I'll bet" they can in yours too
- All four are essentially natural. Some drugs enhance the brain too, like a class called racetams, quite common in various forms, especially as you dig online into the world of nootropics, but sticking with natural solutions is highly recommended. **Story:** racetams and other synthetic nootropics were tried; they seem to suffer from what synthetic forms usually do: natural forms are simply much better for us and tend to work better with our bodies
- The four:
  - **Huperzine A**, extracted from moss. It basically boosts acetylcholine in the brain, a very key neurotransmitter that directly impacts brain capacity: learning capacity, memory, processing speed, reaction time. Instead of being a source of choline, it stops choline's breakdown, keeping it active, so it effectively raises our levels
    - Personally, incredible benefits taken first thing in the morning. Really cheap, works really well: highly recommended
    - Very tiny doses, measured in micrograms, not milligrams: the recommended dose is anywhere between 50 and 150 micrograms a day; try that and see how you feel
    - It's often in a lot of brain-boosting blends, and often way overdosed: four or five hundred micrograms stuck in one is just too much. Various dosages were tried, and as with a lot of things in life, more is not necessarily better, definitely with huperzine. Really not a fan of supplement blends in general, for a lot of reasons, and this highlights it: get it directly. Easy to get, all over Amazon, quality brands in local health food stores; very cheap and very effective
  - **Alpha GPC**, alpha glycerophosphocholine, kind of the sister supplement to Huperzine A. Huperzine A improves choline levels in the brain, and actually the body, by inhibiting its breakdown; Alpha GPC is a direct source of choline, which turns into acetylcholine, kind of the downstream form, the actual neurotransmitter. The combination is really powerful for brain and body
    - Like lots of neurotransmitters, choline isn't just used in the brain but throughout the body, with a lot of benefits for both
    - A lot of studies show improved power output and physical performance in athletes. Exercise drops choline levels, and Alpha GPC prevents those exercise-induced reductions. Taking choline prior to a workout, the power output difference personally felt is amazing; you'll feel it in your brain the rest of the day, and certainly in your workout
    - Quite a few good brands; a favorite is the NOW brand, available all over and taken on a regular basis, along with others
    - It's isolated from a food, from a couple of sources including soy. Not a big fan of soy, but it's such an isolated substance, without soy proteins, that it seems not a big deal
    - The preferred dosage of most of the studies is 600 milligrams. Personally, 600 milligrams usually twice a day; maybe once a day might be good for you, depending on your volume of physical activity. When that's pretty high, twice a day before your workouts seems really the ideal time
    - It takes a little longer to take effect as a pre-workout: take it a good hour and a half or two hours before the workout, and have your coffee or whatever, say, 15 minutes before, since those work a lot more quickly
  - **Acetyl-L-carnitine**, covered in an episode on energy: it absolutely improves energy, but most definitely the brain too. Basically a mitochondrial-boosting supplement: mitochondria, essentially the powerhouse of our cells, are in almost every cell and especially dense in brain cells, so boosting them boosts energy overall and in the brain
    - A plug for fasting: one of its amazing benefits is the mitochondria boost, which happens especially after we break the fast, one of its fundamental powerful effects
    - Carnitine is in all our cells, and you can buy just L-carnitine: look for the acetyl-L-carnitine form, which crosses the blood-brain barrier to act on the brain's mitochondria, improving cognition, learning, memory and mood
    - Acting on mitochondria everywhere, it also reduces fatigue, shows notable improvements in sperm quality in the studies (obviously your balls have mitochondria too) and improves muscular endurance: really wide-ranging benefits. A favorite, taken on a regular basis
  - **Ginkgo biloba**: really all four benefit the body in so many ways besides the brain. Ginkgo works by enhancing circulation, especially to the brain, but also the rest of the body; enhancing it so significantly to certain parts of the brain gives massive brain-boosting effects, specifically on memory
    - Personally, acetyl-L-carnitine, Alpha GPC and even huperzine definitely bring a lot of processing speed, reaction time and learning capacity; ginkgo, a significant difference in memory, and the studies are really strong around memory too
    - Hormone-boosting supplements need cycling; others like ginkgo don't necessarily: you could pretty much take it regularly forever and be fine, at a moderate, appropriate dose. **Story:** cycling off things occasionally anyway, partially for the benefit and the sort of fun of feeling them fresh and new again. Back on ginkgo after a break, more really vivid memories from the past would pop into the brain: the past kind of lit up
    - Beyond memory, the enhanced circulation helps lots of aspects of the brain, and the body: it enhances nitric oxide, really important for blood flow, including for erections and exercise performance, and oxygen uptake. Some studies even show significantly improved skin moisture, "I would imagine" because of circulation to the skin
    - Absolutely worth taking, especially as we get a little older: a memory all of a sudden better than it has been recently is awesome
    - Dose: around 120 milligrams one to three times a day. It typically comes in 60 or 120 milligram capsules, the dosage a lot of the studies and brands use
- Take any or all: they're all safe in appropriate doses, even at pretty high crazy doses, which you don't need, since more is not necessarily better
- Timing:
  - Ginkgo is the only one of the four really recommended at night: before bed is another awesome time. It can be taken pretty much whenever; personally, three times a day, usually with each of the two cups of coffee mixed with supplements, and once again before bed
  - Some people take Huperzine A before bed, which will definitely bring more vivid dreams and potentially lucid dreaming; personally, it kind of works a little better earlier in the day
  - Alpha GPC and acetyl-L-carnitine: definitely recommended earlier in the day, first thing, then maybe midday or afternoon, whenever you have that second cup of coffee
- Personally, these are the top four brain-boosting supplements ever found, after trying lots of others, the favorites: highly recommended to give them a shot
