> **TL;DR** — Andy Frisella's 75 Hard sets five basic rules for 75 days; the suggested Holistic Alpha way adds zero ejaculations, daily meditation and a daily cold shower, restarting at day one on any failure, and a ramped-up personal version is tracked in tallies.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 195, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- 75 Hard was created by Andy Frisella: basically a challenge, if you will, described by him as "a tactical guide to win the war with yourself". In a lot of ways, that's what this show is all about
- **Story:** not having done his specific challenge before, and already doing a lot of it daily, the decision was to do it and kind of amp it up. There seem to be some important ways to do it in a Holistic Alpha style
- The basic rules of 75 Hard, as Andy Frisella created them:
  - Follow a diet for the entire 75 days, with zero alcohol and zero cheat meals. He doesn't get specific about which diet: the idea is that you must follow a specific eating plan
  - Two 45-minute minimum workouts each day, at least three hours apart, one of which must be done outside regardless of the weather
  - Drink a gallon of water a day
  - Read at least 10 pages a day of a nonfiction self-improvement, self-development, growth-related type of book
  - Take a progress picture each day
- You can read a lot more about it and sign up for free: his website sends the details, but it's pretty simple. He also has a podcast episode that goes into more detail, and sells a book that goes into a little more
- **Story:** really most of it was already daily. Rarely a cheat meal anymore, or anything you'd call one, from no motivation for it: the preference is to eat what feels good. Typically two training sessions a day, though not necessarily every single day before, and certainly during the challenge. Usually a gallon of water, and usually a progress pic, now made sure of every day for the 75. The reading is a little new: a lot of audiobooks, but to follow the spirit of the challenge, a paper book, at least ten pages
- **The Holistic Alpha way**, suggested if you want to try this: the five basic rules plus three additions:
  - Zero ejaculations for the 75 days
  - At least one meditation every day
  - A cold shower every day
- If you fail on any one of those items, you start over at day one, the way he lays it out and the way suggested here too: no cheat days, 75 straight days
- You can adapt it to you personally. If there's more you want to add on top of the minimums you can, but you don't have to. Do at least the minimums he lays out: there's a reason they're there
- **Story:** a personal version, ramped up to make the idea he lays out applicable personally, to where things stand: all his rules plus the three additions, tracked with tallies (a tally app already used daily for the important self-care items and some other things), with a few added. Most were already tracked daily. Every day for at least the next 75 days:
  - Two cold showers a day: usually one morning and one evening already, now a daily thing
  - A gallon of water a day
  - Meditating twice a day
  - At least five rounds of Wim Hof breathing, made part of the challenge after trying to implement it on a more regular basis (if you haven't heard of it, look up Wim Hof and the Wim Hof breathing method)
  - On top of the two 45-minute workouts, daily minimums on specific exercises: 350 burpees, 350 push-ups, 350 squats, 150 arm haulers or Supermans (basically bodyweight back work), 35 pull-ups, and at least eight sprints
  - At least two Kegel training sessions a day
  - Edging at least once a day: not necessarily every day before, but the benefits are felt when doing it
  - At least 10 pages of a self-development book, and the progress picture
  - A podcast episode every day through the 75 days, not all about this, each with a minute of update on staying on track and the previous day's numbers, to stay accountable
  - Lumosity, a brain-training app, personally found to make a difference in the brain
  - A target for how much content gets produced online
  - Time to roll out the body and do some fascia release, definitely needed with those fitness numbers
- Some of you are probably already doing a number of these things: some of you already get a couple of training sessions a day, maybe you already drink that amount of water. Take wherever you are and go for this, give it a shot. If you haven't really been working out or following a specific eating plan, maybe do the basics of 75 Hard. It's called 75 Hard for a reason: it's not easy, and for a lot of people it is a major, major challenge
- **Story:** those basics had been part of daily life for a long time, so things had kind of moved a little beyond them already, hence the ramp-up. You don't need to ramp it up as much, but the suggestion is to do the basics of the Holistic Alpha way, which is really three key additions to the normal program
- This is not easy, but guaranteed, if you put this into action you will see some changes. **Story:** excited to put it into action, and it will definitely be a challenge to keep up some of the specific workout numbers and accomplish everything, having ramped it up quite a bit from the basics
- **Story:** the numbers on day three of the challenge: two cold showers, a gallon of water, two meditations, five Wim Hof breathing rounds (the minimum), 400 burpees (minimum 350), 430 push-ups (minimum 350), 150 arm haulers, 400 squats (minimum 350), 40 pull-ups (a little over the minimum), eight sprints, two 45-minute-plus workouts, both actually outside, two Kegel sessions, an edging session, the 10 pages read, the body rolled out, the podcast happening right then, Lumosity, a progress picture, and the content goals met. "Forward, we shall move"
