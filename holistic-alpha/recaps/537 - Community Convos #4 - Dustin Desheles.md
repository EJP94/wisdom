> **TL;DR** — The guest went from porn at 11 or 12, through religious shame, escalation and a failed erection, to edging, a support community and a relapse he came back from; meditation and mindful sexuality are the two most powerful tools to explore the inner world.

*From Holistic Alpha episode 537, a conversation between host Steven Mathis and Dustin Desheles. Lines marked **Guest** are Dustin's, unmarked lines are the host's. Not fact-checked.*

- **Guest story:** 34, born and raised in Nashville. Introduced to porn at 11 or 12 by a cousin, back in the CD-burning era, through a website they used where changing one letter led to an adult site. He told his uncle "this wasn't me". It opened "the can of worms", and as puberty woke his body up it quickly became an addiction. Finding this podcast was "truly life changing": it pulled him out of addictions he thought he couldn't get out of, and he is now much stronger, not just sexually but physically and mentally
- **Guest story:** raised in a very conservative Christian home, and still Christian, which really set him up for failure around sexuality. He was taught sex is only good inside marriage, so anything felt outside it is bad: "a war inside your head", with fear of hell. As a boy it was "don't touch yourself, don't do this", and parents are scared to talk about it. His dad's talk: you'll see people, get hard, something will come out
  - He asked why a loving God would give such strong desires with no outlet, and what happens if he never marries
  - He searched the Bible and found nothing forbidding masturbation or edging, but found evidence of energy and life force. Christian theologians and doctors he read encouraged healthy masturbation
  - His conclusion: "this is completely normal", "religion got this one wrong". It was "a control method", not help
- **Guest:** why would we be designed to feel things we aren't allowed to express in a healthy way?
- Probably the vast majority of religions, all of them as far as personally known, kind of get this wrong. Keeping sex sacred seems a good idea. Not teaching men how to access their own sexuality is not
- **Guest:** a lot of religions get it wrong in that they set you up for failure. He firmly believes marriage is very sacred, something to be respected and vowed. However, we weren't given these desires and needs [?]
- **Guest story:** from his late teens through his 20s, more independence made it "extremely unhealthy", for over 10 years
  - Thinking about porn almost all the time, masturbating and ejaculating several times a day, at work too
  - Regular porn stopped being enough. He moved to darker, more extreme content and sites that pull you into that lifestyle, not from desire but to feel the original pleasure again, like a drug addict raising the dose. Three or four ejaculations a day, chasing the reward
  - He felt depleted, depressed and anxious, lived in secrecy and lied to girlfriends about it. The secret becomes harder and harder to control the further you get into it
- **Guest story:** with a girlfriend several years ago, after days of heavy porn use, he couldn't get more than a half erection and had to stop. "What the hell is wrong with me?" threw him into a panic attack. The shame was too much and they broke up, and he went straight back to porn, because porn could still get him hard. Porn ended relationships and stunted his sex life
- **Guest story:** he always knew it wasn't good for him, but the pleasure outweighed the risk. In his late 20s, a four-man Bible study and accountability group did NoFap and no-porn runs together. Once, about two months in, he found that getting close without releasing helped him keep going, without knowing the word "edging". Every time a group fell apart, so did his streak: without accountability, the old habits came back
- **Guest story:** in his early 30s, every program he found wanted hundreds or thousands of dollars. One guy on Instagram asked his income level "to make sure you're actually committed". He gave up and fell back
- **Guest story:** about five months before the conversation, he found the podcast by searching porn addiction recovery on Apple Podcasts. He listened to judge whether the host sounded legit, messaged on Instagram and got an answer that asked for no money. He still relapsed, until he realized he would never have a fruitful marriage or become who he wants to be this way. He joined Inner Circle, the podcast's private membership community. September 26 was probably the last day he looked at porn
- Finding edging on intuition alone, with no name or guidance for it, is remarkable
- **Guest story:** he went in "balls to the wall" and asked the most random questions, and got answers without judgment. The biggest thing from day one: a community of men who want you to succeed for your own sake. He went about 25–26 days before releasing
- **Guest story:** his earlier, accidental edging wasn't daily and was mixed with the NoFap mentality of "don't even touch yourself". **Guest:** there was no benefit really to that, beyond trying to get you to break an addiction
- **Guest story:** his first edging session, the night of the 26th, was a five-minute timer, and he was nervous, because with porn he raced to finish in 30–45 seconds. His whole nervous system ramped up, happy and excited out of nowhere: "when a car runs out of gas, you put just a little bit in there and it's all of a sudden it's fired up and ready to go", "somebody just lit a torch under me"
- **Guest:** his biggest takeaway is that sexual energy is a real life force that fuels and drives the body, not something occult or magic
- Set a timer when starting an edging practice. It gives you a target and a clear point where you're done
- **Guest story:** he edged once a day, every day, and leveled out around 15–20 minutes of full focus, pushing distractions away: "a full body, mind, soul and spirit moment". He felt stronger, with the endurance to go 10–15 minutes when he never came close before, growing mentally and physically. **Guest advice:** get coconut oil, his one sales pitch [?]
- **Guest story:** a relapse. After a podcast episode on shame and a call where one of the guys shared his own shame, a lifetime of being told this was shameful, that he was weird and different, and that "straight guys shouldn't be doing that" flooded back. He ran: cancelled his membership and deleted everything off his Instagram
  - He didn't go back to porn. But for about three weeks he masturbated and ejaculated several times a day, even in work bathrooms
  - Like an addict who relapses, as doctors talk about it, he went straight back to full intensity instead of back to the beginning. **Guest:** for addicts, that is usually what ends up killing them. Then he felt shame about that too
- **Guest:** American men are taught to bottle their emotions, shame and guilt and not deal with them "until you're old and you're dying", because they never learn how to handle them [?]
- **Guest story:** after days of inner war ("he probably wants nothing to do with me"), he messaged the host and got a fast, loving, non-judgmental reply. He rejoined, and not one of them asked why he had left: they welcomed and affirmed him. "Men need that." It was his most life-giving week
- Old patterns, thoughts and emotional responses will pop back up along this journey. **Host story:** across coaching, Inner Circle and a past bootcamp, every withdrawal that comes to mind seems to have invariably been a slip and a shame spiral. Support plus accountability changes things
- **Guest story:** now he edges once a day in the evenings, about 10–15 minutes, plus other exercises. His new focus is meditation, with the Headspace app and meditations sent to him. It helps him see who he is and why he does what he does. He lives with anxiety and depression and takes medication for both. **Guest:** from what he is finding, through meditation a lot of people are getting the ability to rewire their brain's network around that. His goal is to use meditation and edging to "build a new map" in his brain, change how he processes emotions, and one day not need the medication
- The vast majority of people can probably relate to some level of anxiety or depression at times in life. For a large number of people it is probably part of the human experience
- Going from porn, or even mindless masturbation, to intentional exploration of the inner world "changes everything"
- Meditation and sexuality, approached mindfully and as sacred, are the two most powerful tools to explore the inner world. They heal you from the inside out: energetic, emotional and body. Outside habits matter too, but it all starts inside. **Host story:** those two healed and empowered him in fundamental ways
- **Guest question:** in edging he goes close to the edge, calms down, and goes close again. Where does the energy lie: in the semen, or in some chemical reaction in the body? And what should he focus on?
- The energy comes from the energetic field. We and everything around us are almost entirely space filled with energy, "99.99999%". The view: arousal, like working out, doesn't create energy or pull it from somewhere else. It basically liberates stored energy already within and around us. The more you tap in, the more you liberate, and it is limitless: you can go as deep as you are willing and able
- What works for connecting with the energy probably varies a little for different guys. Some tools:
  - An anchor like the breath can be a good tool, since the breath is itself energy and life force. When the mind drifts to a bill, a project or porn, bringing it back to the breath is a good way to go
  - The first few minutes of most Headspace meditations walk a path from outer to inner focus (senses, air, body scan, breath) that gets quicker with practice. Use it as an anchor in edging too
  - Some men feel it as warmth or tingling and follow that as a map to the energy. **Host story:** he often sees the energy as a light and puts his attention on it
  - **Humming:** sounds we make are "extremely powerful". Humming's vibration becomes an anchor for attention and awareness, like making a target light up in the dark. A few men who tried it say it helps them connect. It doesn't need to last the whole session. More on it in an upcoming episode
- **Guest:** controlling his breath has been the most helpful part
- Things deepen with reps. Perspective can shift fast, as in his first weeks, but the inner world takes time to unfold. There is not really a shortcut: stick with the habits
- **Guest story:** his vision for five years:
  - Become a champion and spokesperson for this practice, starting with the students and college-age men he works with at church, changing how we talk to young men: "are we life giving or are we shame giving?"
  - Finish his master's and move into public relations and marketing, driven by the energy this builds
  - Challenge wrong narratives and speak up
  - Find a woman with the same energy. **Guest:** this process builds an energy that attracts women
- Bring sexuality out of the shadows: it doesn't need to be taboo, secret or degenerate. People want to get good at other parts of life but rarely apply that to sexuality
- **Guest story:** in his first weeks, he felt way more confident in who he is. People kept telling him he had "a vibe" and seemed happy and energetic. People came up just to chat, and friends he hadn't heard from in a while called to talk things through. **Guest:** people are starving for that energy in themselves without knowing it
- "Change your energy, you change your life", a phrasing probably from Joe Dispenza. Inner work alone (energy work, edging, manifesting things and creating visions, meditation, diving into your inner world) can change how people treat you, who you run into and what opportunities show up
- [speaker unclear] Energy affects every part of life. To be stronger, smarter or more driven, change your energy: "it works"
- **Guest advice:** jump in with your whole self and don't fear feeling awkward. Most times in life, the awkward questions and seasons produce the most growth. Don't let groups or ideologies tell you something is wrong or that their way is the only way. Speaking as a Christian, this practice doesn't invalidate the faith. It adds to it and will help you grow in all areas, including faith and spiritual life. Challenge what you've been told and you'll find the two complement each other
- It is a spiritual process, when approached the right way
