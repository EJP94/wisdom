> **TL;DR** — Total daily volume builds strength fast: 20–50 sets that push you a little, spread through the day, seem to signal growth without really breaking the muscles down, and a week aiming for 1,000 push-ups a day showed how small sets add up.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 5, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- Being strong makes every day better and brings a different energy to a man's life
- **Total daily volume** is a favorite way to build strength quickly, and it works surprisingly well: many small sets of a simple exercise or two, spread through the day
  - Pick a set size that pushes you a little but that you can hit every time, not your max. For example, if 10 push-ups are steady for you and you max out around 15–25, use sets of 10
  - Do 20, 30, 40 or 50 sets a day, one or two sets every 15 or 30 minutes. The longer the spread the better, if you can, from waking up to bedtime. It doesn't have to be every 15 minutes: vary the interval and the number of sets to fit your hours
  - Another example: if you can do 5 push-ups, aim for at least 20 sets, or 100 push-ups a day. Over five hours that is one set every 15 minutes: set a timer. You will probably surprise yourself with how much volume you can do and how quickly it builds strength. After work, 5 to 10 p.m. works
  - There are 96 fifteen-minute blocks in a day. Using even a quarter or half of them adds up fast
  - Make it a big target. The sets obviously get harder by the 40th or 50th
- Why it seems to work: each mini set activates the muscles without really breaking them down, and the body takes that as a signal to heal a little and grow a little. A full workout of half an hour to two hours needs at least until the next day to recover
- Exercises that work:
  - Push-ups, the recommended one. A basic functional strength that everybody can probably benefit from, whatever their sport or goal. Ten push-ups take about 20 seconds, so you can do them at work, even in the lunchroom
  - Bodyweight squats or Hindu squats for the lower body
  - Pull-ups, if you can do at least one or two
  - You can mix them
- **Story:** a first try at spreading sets out: aiming for 1,000 push-ups a day for about a week. Not every day reached 1,000. The target mattered because it seemed huge and unreachable in one workout, yet it added up in small chunks
- A big target like that seems a great way to set a new record for yourself and shift your perspective. The exact number doesn't matter
- Try it for a week, three days, two days or even one. Start today, even if it's late: one day of effort and nothing to lose
