> **TL;DR** — Hitting an emotional wall is very common as retention takes you deeper into yourself; suggested approach: introspection; an intentional release can be powerful with clear awareness of what to let go of, otherwise, most of the time, keep retaining, without beating yourself up.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 675, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Listener question:** A young man asks whether others, in their second to third week of retaining, hit a phase of feeling bad about themselves and their progress and just feel like releasing. He learned somewhere that this is when unhealed traumas and inner child problems arise: is it true, and how to deal with it, since it can be hard and overwhelming, especially alone?
- It takes so much courage to work on this stuff, and to reach out for support and align with other guys working on it.
- Don't get too hung up on the time frame: this may happen at any point, probably around a week for a lot of guys starting out, or a month or two in for those practicing that long; maybe why this listener hits it at two to three weeks.
- Probably the easy way to call it is "a bit of an emotional wall", kind of hit in some way after maybe feeling great, better and better. "It is very, very common" at various stages of retention. For a lot of guys in the beginning, it's an overwhelm of sexual energy, of uncontrolled lust; this listener's wall is a little bit different and very, very important.
- Is it true? "I would say absolutely one of the things that will happen as you practice retention": you dive deeper into yourself, your power, life force and emotions, so you'll hit points where you get a little uncomfortable and emotions you haven't really tapped into before. That's to be expected: experienced personally, and seen "over and over and over again with guys", this listener being an example, along with many clients and others heard from.
- For a lot of guys, retention is practiced essentially alone, so this can feel very alone and unsure.
- Suggested approach: base it on a lot of **introspection**. Ask what you feel uncomfortable about, what emotions come up, in what area of life. That is the foundation of understanding and awareness, "And it's the awareness that's key."
- "Releasing" is a key word: ejaculation is in fact a huge energetic release, often thought of, maybe not negatively (though sometimes it's easy to fall into that trap), but as draining. It is also a powerful potential release of things we want to let go of.
- Through introspection (meditating, thinking, journaling, talking to a close friend), once clearly aware of something you really need to let go of, an intentional release aimed at letting it go "can be incredibly powerful": with that clear awareness and a clear intention, you'll very likely experience a lot of positive benefits.
- Our emotions, all our trauma and inner child stuff, live in our body: one reason we hit this wall in different ways, times and layers, tapping more strongly into the life force there. So a physical release can serve a hugely positive role; the difference is awareness.
- Retention is not about never releasing, nor about seeing a release as a bad thing, "because it's not". Too many releases will drag you down; unconscious ones, without intention, discipline or awareness, probably will. An aware, intentional release at the right time for the right reasons can be incredibly powerful and beneficial.
- Releasing won't always necessarily be the best call, and there's not a right or wrong: "it's all testing and learning". Sometimes the work is to keep pushing against retention while working through emotions in other ways, probably especially without clarity about what to let go of, or when it isn't deep and longstanding, not stress from work yesterday. With awareness of deeper things, releasing is probably a good call, if that's what you feel.
- Hitting the wall doesn't necessarily mean releasing is right: sometimes the best call is to keep retaining and lean into those emotions, feel them, sit with them, meditate, go for a walk; don't resist them, be with them.
- There is "not a one size fits all approach": every individual and every moment is unique. With intention, patience, grace for yourself, discipline and commitment, ask whether something clearly has been eating at you for a while and this is a positive opportunity to let it go. If so, go for it: set the intention. If not, most of the time, keep retaining: especially earlier in the journey, a big part of it is pushing up against that edge, a little farther and farther.
- Even so, don't beat yourself up if or when you release: it's never a bad thing, always part of the journey and a learning experience, even if you might learn, having felt a little sort of on that edge and gone for it anyway, to be a little more disciplined next time.
- You will face these walls. **Story:** a promise: 10-plus years into this journey, all of this still comes up, in different ways, layers, depths and time frames, because this is how we function. "And it ought to still come up."
- That's a good thing: you're tuning into deeper levels of yourself. It may be uncomfortable, new or unwanted, but go with the roller coaster, the climbs and drops, as long as you keep stepping with awareness, intention and heart.
- It seems a lot of guys start, probably personally too, from a little more of a superficial place, so to speak: maybe improving erection quality, or maybe shame. For most guys in the beginning, there's not really a sense that retention is "actually a journey of self-exploration on very deep levels". But that's what it is, and it's all beautiful.
