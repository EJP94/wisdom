> **TL;DR** — All growth lies on the other side of hard things, so practice intentional discomfort in physical training, which transfers to the rest of your life, without ignoring your body's signs of injury; the big version updates of you only come through difficulty.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 10, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- There is no easy button for life: so much of it boils down to our willingness and ability to do hard shit, the only way to grow and progress meaningfully. "Hard is just another word for new", and all growth lies on the other side of hard things, some more challenging than others
- One of the best things we can do is intentionally practice hard things, not just where we kind of have to: a business, job or managing-people challenge probably takes hard stuff to grow through, and one of the best ways through is hard situations in physical training
- **Physical training transfers** to the rest of your life: train to discomfort, readjust and keep going until it sinks in that you can exist there; practiced regularly, it translates. It's facing resistance "in an arena of your choosing at a time of your choosing", to show him who's the boss, so tomorrow, at your job or business, resistance is still there, but you're a little more powerful against him, and he comes in feeling a little worse about himself because you kicked his ass in the physical arena
- For men, physical training is about so much more than looks: mind, emotions, success, drive, hormones, relationships, everything is affected, negatively by inactivity or extremely positively. It helps the best man we all want to be bring what lives inside into the physical world, body change or not: the harder the thing, the more you can benefit and the stronger you can be when you face it
- Fun in your workout may mean you're not on the right path, until, the awesome thing, you have fun being uncomfortable, once you really do that a lot and get into that mindset. **Story:** some climbing workouts were just climbing to tunes. That, or a run where you feel awesome, differs a lot from pushing until really uncomfortable, then deciding to keep going
- Listen to your body and know pushing really hard from injury: you can push way harder than you think without injury, or too far, into injury. The suggestion is not to ignore your body's signs but to get uncomfortable, decide it's okay and keep going, in push-ups, pull-ups, climbing, running, lifting, whatever, chosen as your intention beforehand
- You can't necessarily go this hard every day forever: you need active recovery days, and our bodies kind of go in cycles a little bit. But as often as you can, go in with the one goal to "push until I get as uncomfortable as I can get"
- Try to break yourself: if an extreme enough physical effort has never kind of broken you down mentally and emotionally, you haven't pushed yourself. Push hard enough and emotional stuff will all of a sudden come up, a part of you that you're not facing
- Strong advice: decide before some workouts that your only goal is to push until discomfort. That's when the workout starts, everything before is warming up, and every minute there pays huge dividends, in body and life. A promise: spend time there and you'll feel the benefits, if you're willing to put in the work
- Life comes down to: are you willing? **Story:** plenty of life was spent unwilling, and the question applies to sharing this message too. Are you willing to put in the work, physical too, for the change and growth you want? We either are or aren't
- If we're willing, the most power can be found through choosing **intentional discomfort**: decide it's okay, your friend, your power, because it is. All our power lies in the hard shit in our life. It's cliche, but a lot of cliches are true: all our strength lies in our hardest times
- **Story:** one of the greatest periods of growth came when his son, about a week and a half old, had open heart surgery, then three months in the ICU and a year or so of recovery. Watching him fight and almost lose, as a kid really, was by a wide margin the hardest experience to that point, still top two or three, and the time of the very most growth
- **Life as software versions**: like iOS or Android, the dot versions, one point two, one point six, 13.2, 13.4, are usually minor security fixes; the big changes come with the next whole version, six to seven. Habits and intention make incremental gains, but a major update to the you that you are only comes through difficulty, "the reality of life". Recognizing that difficulty is our friend, you can make those things happen and push the minor updates along faster. Do hard stuff, your own way
- Just for today, forget the future: you can only live today. Find discomfort in the physical arena today, maybe bodyweight squats, a plank as long as you can and then a whole lot longer, push-ups, climbing or running: it will pay off
