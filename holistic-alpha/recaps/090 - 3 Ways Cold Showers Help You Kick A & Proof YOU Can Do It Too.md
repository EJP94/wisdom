> **TL;DR** — Cold showers raise your metabolism and release norepinephrine, kick resistance in the nuts at the start of the day and help you tap into the quiet place inside, and you will adapt by building brown fat, your cold muscle, if you start easy.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 90, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- This weekend, beyond rest and fun, plan to dig into a part of your life that could use some growth: maybe a workout, some meditation, maybe meal prep, whatever helps you have an awesome week next week. We gotta keep moving forward
- The cold truly is our warm friend. Cold showers make really powerful shifts that lead to a lot of awesome things, and you can adapt to them more than you think, with less discomfort than you think
- **Cold showers** help in three ways:
  - Number one, physical. Cold exposure is "absolutely proven now" to up your metabolism. The body basically produces heat through thermogenesis, shivering and non-shivering. Cold kicks both into gear, shivering only initially: once you adapt a little, it goes away and non-shivering thermogenesis takes over. That is basically the process of brown adipose tissue, brown fat, a special tissue really, really good at turning food into energy, specifically heat. The result is a big metabolic boost that lasts a significant period of time, so you burn more fat throughout the day: if you're looking to drop weight or get leaner, cold showers will lead to an increase in metabolism for sure. It would probably be worth it just for that
    - One study used longer exposure at not as cold temperatures, an hour (you don't need to do an hour). 68 degree Fahrenheit water, just barely cool really, gave a 93% increase in metabolic rate; 57 degree water, a 350% increase
    - Cold also brings a big release of norepinephrine, both a hormone and a neurotransmitter that regulates, among other things, mood, attention, focus and arousal: basically one of your feel-good hormones. Its mood-boosting, antidepressive effect is unreal. **Story:** on mornings of dragging and feeling down, nothing else ever found changed that: back in the day every energy drink known to man, coffee, all kinds of supplements. Nothing will change your state more than a cold shower
    - It's really proven to reduce your stress: you feel better and more exhilarated, and more and more science now testing this on people finds a level of alertness that lasts throughout your day. There's more, but metabolism and norepinephrine are the two big ones
  - Number two, **resistance**: what holds us back from doing what we want, pops up fears, doubts and insecurities, brings up past pain and leaves us feeling stuck. We battle it on an ongoing basis, and some things shift the battle in our favor. A recent episode covered semen retention, absolutely a powerful and fundamental one
    - On a day-to-day basis, "I truly believe there is nothing more that you can do, nothing more powerful that you can do to start your day" and your battle against resistance from a fundamentally more powerful standpoint than a cold shower
    - No matter how adapted we are, it's never about comfort. It's a choice, made knowing the benefits and how much better you'll feel after, despite the discomfort: like kicking resistance in the balls right at the start of your day, saying "I am here to fight"
    - That has a ripple effect: making calls at work, deciding what to eat, getting to your workout, wherever resistance potentially pops up, you're in a more powerful position
  - Number three, related but more quiet: the meditative place. If you don't meditate, please start: it only takes 10 minutes and truly is one of the things that will change your life, helping you tap into a quiet, calm place away from anxiety and stress, and even more, the real, truest, most powerful version of you
    - We have a sort of quiet, still place inside, always undisturbed, full of pure light and energy and calm peace, always there no matter the storms in our head, emotions or outer world. It just gets obscured when we get lost in emotions, thoughts and worries. The inward journey is to find it and come back to it, again and again, since we keep getting pulled out. We need tools for that, and meditation is the place to start
    - Cold brings one of two responses. You freak out: you want to escape as fast as you can, breathe all fast, and "I'm so cold" consumes your thoughts. Or you go deeper. Just as mental and emotional storms and external events don't touch the quiet place, neither does your body being hot or cold, comfortable or uncomfortable. It's always there, warm, peaceful: not always full of laughter, there are hard times in life, but always full of peace
    - When you get really intentional and decide to use the cold shower to find that place instead of wanting to escape, it will take you there, "I promise"
- Cold showers can't be recommended highly enough. **Story:** used to love a long hot shower, or at least a warm one. About five or six years ago came a first exposure to cold through hardcore training methods: a lot of guys training really hard use ice baths or ice plunges, as a lot of higher-level athletes have for quite a while. During a stretch of trail running, ice baths on the legs followed really long runs, 15, 20 mile trail runs, but never really adapted to it as a lifestyle or a more frequent thing
- Then came Wim Hof, "you've probably heard of him": amazing, and no better source to learn about cold, how to get so much out of it, and the breathing techniques he teaches. Look him up if you haven't already
- **Story:** about three years ago, halfway through a snowy Salt Lake City winter, all bundled up, came the decision "I'm just gonna adapt". Since then, literally not more than a t-shirt, mostly a tank top. It is incredible the way we can adapt
- If you tried this before, you probably think there's no way you can, or want to, do it regularly; maybe you've never tried it, or once when forced to. Either way, a scientific process helps you deal with the cold better and better, "absolute proof" you'll adapt more than you think:
  - The norepinephrine cold triggers also triggers an increase in brown fat, the tissue behind non-shivering thermogenesis that keeps us warmer. Brown fat is really closely linked with better health: a study of obese men showed that they had less of it. It's like the one kind of fat we do want, a special tissue we don't have a lot of and want as much of as we can, for a number of reasons, energetically too
  - Every time you take a cold shower or expose yourself to cold for any decent period, you build brown fat. In one study, people exposed to cold air at 60 degrees, not that cold, six hours a day for 10 days, had on average a 37% increase in brown fat
  - It's like training a muscle: you have a **cold muscle**. If you get cold really easily, exercise it and it will grow and adapt like any other muscle. Brown fat is your body's engine to make heat without shivering: the more you have, the more heat you make, so along with a number of health benefits you get continually more comfortable with the cold
- **Story:** shoveling snow in a tank top, out of being weird like that, a guy of habit who trains and is in gym attire a lot. The adaptation came from intending and deciding to, not from being special: the body adapted, just like yours can. Winters in tank tops or shirtless hikes are awesome too if you can get that exposure, but you don't need them: with a cold shower, you will adapt
- **Story:** people ask on snowy days "Aren't you cold?", and the answer is "No, not really". First, because the quiet place inside is always warm: those who have gone on some kind of inward journey know what that means, and it means so much more than the words. Beyond that, we adapt and grow brown fat, gaining an ability to produce more heat
- Like anything, start easy. You wouldn't walk into the gym after two years off and try to squat 300 pounds, and having never been in the cold is no reason for a 30 minute ice bath. If you're taking warm showers now:
  - Switch to cold at the end: first say 20 or 30 seconds, then a minute, over the course of say a week or two weeks
  - Then gradually up it a little more
  - After maybe three, four weeks, you'll be ready to give straight cold showers a shot. You will adapt, "I promise", feel amazing benefits, and not regret it
- If you're sick, recovering or feel like you're getting sick, maybe you shouldn't take a cold shower? The opposite is actually true: it's one of the most beneficial things you can do to boost your immune system. Absolute science over the last several years has shown an increase in white blood cell count. It might not feel comfortable or exciting, but being sick is still absolutely a great time for a cold shower
- You've got to put things into action in your life, but find the one, two or three most impactful things for you, not some 18 module course of 150 different ways to be a powerful man. Daily action on those makes a huge difference
