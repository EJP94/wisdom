> **TL;DR** — Funky, off-path periods hit a lot of guys; be real about them and rebuild momentum with a 21-day challenge of one to about four tracked habits, starting now, with retention, for most guys, the most powerful choice.

*From Holistic Alpha episode 549, a conversation between host Steven Mathis and Charles Martin. Lines marked **Guest** are Charles's, unmarked lines are the host's. Not fact-checked.*

- The guest is a friend, a former coaching client and a member of the Inner Circle, the private membership community; what he had been going through had been seen in a lot of guys recently
- A certain positivity that carries through even when you're feeling shitty is something personally practiced, or tried; the guest has it: surely there are times he's not really feeling it, since we all have shitty times, but he has "this deep foundational kind of positivity", and it will serve him well
- **Host story:** host and guest had both been in "a little bit of funky energetic periods", a little off the path, and the same came up with a lot of guys in the Inner Circle and from other people in life. If that many feel it, a lot of people do. So acknowledge it and be real about it, and do something to move through it as effectively as possible
- **Guest story:** a weird season of bad days; a lot of good habits took a hit, a lot of momentum was lost, and it's tough to get back on, especially after a streak of L's ([speaker unclear]: stacking L's builds the habit of taking an L, a less than ideal habit). He had to sit down and reason with himself and see it for what it is: you ride high stacking wins, and "every champion gets knocked out". He'd distanced himself, wanting to "embrace the suck" on his own, and retreated
- **Guest story:** an emergency in his family, a lot of extra hours at work and a crazy holiday season in sales burnt him out to the point of not catching his emotions as he normally does; when triggers showed up, he wasn't mentally strong enough to call on his good habits, and bad habits crept back in
- **Guest:** we need to know, and the other guys need to know, that it's okay to fail; the point is solutions and how to get back on
- Solutions don't mean instant change or being perfect. They mean being real, acknowledging when we're not on a path that will lead where we want to go, and moving through this part as effectively as we can, gradually getting back on a better path
- For the guest, past relationship stuff, dating, texting and the early stages of relationships have brought up emotional triggers; naming that isn't digging at a wound but recognizing a big trigger that has led to a lot of the other stuff happening or not happening
- **Guest story:** working on building the better version of himself, in this weird offseason he saw everybody on social media "all booed up" for the holidays, happy, in love. Every guy has his off season. After grinding in silence and stacking habits, he didn't want to be the guy sitting back asking "Where's mine?"
- **Guest story:** thoughts crept in about old relationships and situationships where he dropped the ball, and he knows why: he was in the grips of all kinds of bad habits, porn included. "Everything happens for a reason." Then came another failure with a girl he was talking to; it kind of all happened at the same time, and he fell low
- **Guest story:** negative thoughts came: this ain't working; maybe I'm not cut out for this fight, for the greater version I envision; I used to be comfortable; it doesn't make sense to take an ice-cold shower in the middle of December, to meditate when stressed, to journal when tired, to work out when your body aches. He listened to "the inner bitch", let him take the steering wheel and retreated, unable to address his emotions or use the good habits he knows work
- **Guest:** we know these habits work, or we wouldn't have tried them and been consistent with them. **Guest story:** not perfect, he took his L's, accepted them, even welcomed them, "This fucking sucks", rather be comfortable, though not comfortable admitting that, and stopped tracking his habits like he normally does. Glad not to be the only one, he left the conversation fired up, and knows it will fire up a lot of the guys too, or anybody in these lows or who kind of took a hit, maybe from something they didn't see coming
- Over and over, a lot of guys have said that, in their own unique way, it's been a little funky. It plays out differently for everybody: certain habits stick, others fall off; some have a child or a medical situation going on
- **Host story:** one big thing was the change in seasons, despite being a big believer in the cold: a cold shower every single day for probably at least three or four years, one of the most foundational things, which has been huge. But also a big fan of the sun and warmth, so the change was a little rough, probably part of it. Add big visions for this whole community and reconnecting with someone from quite a while ago, very beautiful and very intense: an inner world with a lot going on
- It looks a little different for everybody, but it seems everybody's inner world has a little more going on recently. It's okay to have these times. Don't wallow: recognize it, and do something to move through it as effectively as possible, "because there will be another side of it. Things always flow and change." Neither of them will feel like this forever, and the same is true for listeners: we'll get back to feeling more powerful, to the highest vibration we have. Move through it so it's as short as possible and you come out with as much momentum as possible
- **Guest:** the word momentum explains everything he's been dealing with: sometimes, as we gain momentum in certain areas, we might lose it in others. **Guest story:** he gained so much momentum with work and personal life, and lost it in the habits that protect him from going backwards
- **Momentum** really matters in everything, a new appreciation of recent months: workouts, cold showers, eating, your mental approach, the thoughts you let repeat in your head, relationships with your child, niece, nephew, spouse or girlfriend, and totally in habits. Both had felt a little bit of its negative aspect; get back to the positive one
- **21-day habit challenge**: host and guest do it together and invite listeners. Registrants get a quick video by email every day for 21 days, very short for busy lives, to keep us all on the path, plus a daily podcast check-in of somewhere between two and ten minutes, for accountability on the habits each committed to
- **Host story:** physical training hasn't been entirely dropped, still moving every day, but without the same intensity. At first that was probably good, letting the body do some long-term healing; then probably not. The body seems done healing, and a return to a certain level of intensity seems needed, which has been kind of hard: momentum again
- A suggestion: pick between one and a maximum of about three to four habits: the three or four foundation habits you do when things go well and have fallen off of, for a few days, a week, a month; or one habit; something you've slipped on, or something new. If you're kicking ass, as a lot of listeners are in a lot of ways, and may not even really be feeling funky energy the last few weeks, that's okay: you can still benefit. Pick habits that make your path better and more powerful
- **Guest story:** his four: physical training, not taken seriously for about two weeks, and it's showing; retention, for the energy he gets retaining over a long period; meditation, since his busy work schedule tells him he probably needs to meditate more, to avoid getting too overwhelmed or stressed; and cold showers: after one warm shower, it took about five minutes the next day to get under the cold water, having reintroduced that warm comfort
- **Guest:** personally, a cold shower corrects the day: whatever else, at least he got it, and it feels good to get done something he didn't want to do; a lot of guys benefit from it
- Four may seem like a lot to some guys, but, as the guest has experienced, everything kind of feeds the other: in some ways the right three or four that support each other can actually be easier, it seems, and only one or two may potentially be a little harder. What matters is dialing in what really matters to you right now, encouraged for all of you guys out there; how many doesn't really matter. A suggestion: don't go beyond four, which probably gets a little excessive; it's only 21 days, and you've got years and decades ahead
- **Guest story:** with his responsibilities increasing, he narrowed down his stack of habits: back to the basics, archiving some, building on the ones he's really lacking that need more focus and willpower; condensing, simplifying, trying to win again
- If you're not tracking at all, "you absolutely have to track your habits". **Guest:** tracking has made an "Exponential difference" over the last year plus: looking back to his first month, he sees his strong and weak months and why, a month without retention, meditation or workouts explaining a dip in performance. It also helps you stay accountable
- As seen personally and in the guest's life over the last year, year and a half, you get back on the path faster when tracking, because you notice two, five or ten days have gone by. It seems to happen to us all that even in a pretty good place, with four, five or six self-care habits, a walk, meditation, edging, it's easy to fall off one or two, especially without tracking; tracking shows you skipped a particular thing for two days. **Host story:** both use a habit tracking app and share it with each other
- Decide, starting today, right now, not next week: this conversation went from a phone call to a published episode very quickly, "because action matters and speed matters". Both said they were starting that night
- The holidays are the perfect time: it's not about massive changes but a few things that support you, and "if there's ever a time when we need the support of self-care habits to move through life effectively, it's the holidays". It seems true for literally everybody, whatever the family situation: probably everybody known finds the holiday time of year a little funky, because it's stressful
- From their discussions, the guest's habits, cold shower, meditation, workout, retaining, make him feel better and let him show up more effectively elsewhere. **Guest:** 100%; when he skips them, he sees and feels the consequences
- The holidays are an emotionally rough time for a lot of people, bringing up relationship stuff and people who have passed on. Supportive habits literally support us in the hard parts of life; it doesn't have to be all about growth, it can just be moving through as peacefully as possible. The challenge helps carry you through the holidays and come out the other side as effectively as possible: while others try to shed the extra 15 or 10 pounds from Christmas and decide on January 1st on a big change, participants will be something like 18 days in by the 13th, kicking ass not from doing a billion things or being perfect but from dialing in one or a few things that support them
- **Retention** is on the guest's list ([speaker unclear]: going back on it makes all the difference). "For so many of you guys listening, this will be the most important habit". Not for everybody: someone in a relationship comfortable releasing like once a week may not want to retain for 21 days, and that's fine. For most, retaining during these 21 days is the most powerful thing you can do. For a lot of guys, it seems, the top two will be no porn and retaining, especially retaining, for its huge energetic impact; emotionally, too, as both know, retention makes a huge difference in their emotions (the guest agrees). Highly encouraged, not absolutely required: very much consider it

The plan: record all 500 and post sped-up videos over the next few weeks, for accountability

- **Host story:** second, meditation. 10- or 15-minute meditations are an automatic part of every day. What's missing is a longer one: after some very good recent experiences with about 50 minutes to an hour, one such meditation every day for 21 days. Both take a decent amount of time and energy, so two instead of a bunch
- [speaker unclear] Everybody else is waiting for January 1st to get their shit together, but most of us have been putting the work in all year round. There's no waiting: tomorrow's not guaranteed. Be grateful for today
