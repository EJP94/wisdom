> **TL;DR** — Staying on course is stacking small, achievable wins: track your key habits every day, be consistent enough that they shift your identity, and start with one or two small things, such as a cold shower and a short workout, before adding more.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 458, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Listener question:** a guy asks on social media how to stay on course. He always has a good start and good intention but keeps falling back into old habits (watching pornography, eating trash food, stopping exercise). He wants to be the best version of himself, but seems to always fall back to things that drag him down further, and his mental health doesn't fare well then. A lot of guys possibly struggle with this now, have before or will: it is a very human thing
- **Small things stack.** The first thing to understand, it seems: most areas of life, including self-care and self-empowerment, are not big events but the stacking of a lot of small things. We don't reach a high vibration (fit, mentally and sexually well) or sink into a low one and old unproductive habits through one or two big events
- That's actually good. From a low place, where we want to be often seems so far away, a big task, but it's not really as big as it seems: the task is a little win, then another, then another. Little wins are achievable and also feel within reach. Example: a hundred pounds overweight, losing a hundred pounds seems really big, and in some ways it is, but making this next meal healthier is much more doable, and seems it, so you're more likely to follow through. Off course, first and foremost get small wins
- **Tracking.** For good starts followed by failing, the big thing found personally to make a world of difference:
  - It keeps you accountable and lets you know sooner when you fall off. After working out every day or six days a week for a couple of weeks, if habits just live in your head, one missed day becomes two, four, six, two weeks: "man, I haven't worked out in two weeks". Tracking, you notice immediately
  - Do something three or four days in a row and that's a new habit, so three or four days without training is a new habit of not training, a new path. The act of tracking itself keeps you on the path
  - Checking off the day's task is an important psychological feedback loop, a satisfaction that makes doing it tomorrow that much more likely
  - It shows how consistent you are, and consistency is what matters. Off and on gets us nowhere, back in the same place
- **Identity.** Be consistent enough that it shifts your identity. For working out to stay with you for years, for decades, it must become part of who you are, with no background identity saying "I'm working out right now, but it's kind of not really who I am". Done consistently enough, it becomes just who you are, more ingrained the longer it lasts
- **Story:** once overweight, out of shape, struggling with porn, excessive ejaculation and so many things discussed here, and an alcoholic ("lots there to unpack another day"). So many of those things completely changed, to the point where they will never go back: years and years of training essentially every day, minus occasional rest days, because the identity shifted
- The only way to get consistent is tracking. Personally, no one worked with or heard from in the last few years has yet made significant changes in these areas without some kind of tracking of key habits. Over and over, when guys start one-on-one and implement tracking, they improve: not all the way at once, not perfectly, with steps back, but "what gets tracked gets improved"
- **Start small.** Changing porn, exercise and eating trash all at once is not going to go well, or maybe goes well for a period and then you fall off. Pick a smaller number of things and be "ruthlessly consistent". Once one, two or three small things are ingrained, part of your identity, add another, then another, slowly over time
- Thinking so many things need to change overwhelms you, a lot of times sending you further down the low-vibration path. Dial back that mental approach: one or two things, ruthlessly consistent, tracked every day. Suggested for the listener, every single day: a cold shower and some kind of physical training, even five minutes to start, without worrying about changing everything else. For others it may not be those two: look at your situation and pick one or two small empowering habits
- Find one of the many habit-tracking apps that works for you (one is used personally with clients and really liked), or use sticky notes or a dry-erase board. How doesn't really matter; tracking does
- Not exercising now, don't make your change an hour a day: from nothing to an hour every single day, consistently, isn't realistic, "It's not gonna happen". Dial it back to something small and achievable
- **Cold shower every morning**, kind of another discussion: so many reasons it is such a powerful habit, the biggest being mental strength and discipline. Men who start their day with one make different, more powerful choices the rest of the day, "You just will". Physiological benefits and other reasons aside, the number one reason it is recommended is that you will be more disciplined
- After a couple of weeks, a few weeks, of the cold shower and the five or maybe 10-minute workout every day, increase or add: maybe the workout from 10 to 20 minutes, then nutrition, one small food choice at a time, then different choices about porn and your sexual health
- "I promise": with small building blocks, one small win at a time, and your tracking system as a daily habit, you will improve and lock some things into your identity, never going back, a beautiful place, experienced personally and seen in so many guys. Even if it's been up and down and never stuck, whether you think you can or not, "you absolutely can do it"
- Track every day, whether it gets done or not: when your tracking system shows the next morning that something didn't get done, you immediately get back on the path, toward some permanent changes that become part of who you are
