> **TL;DR** — Question the stories you tell yourself about your limits, and build testosterone, and how it feels, from habits and fuel, not supplements: water, sleep, super clean eating without alcohol, daily strength work with sprints and legs, managed sex energy, low stress and a purpose.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 127, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Question your reality**, the challenge for the week. Question your beliefs about yourself, where you are and what you can do. The only way to create a new reality is to question the current one. "You are not stuck"
  - Look at where you feel stuck, not good enough, or where you think "I wish"
  - Test: would somebody looking from the outside, who sees only your current skills, knowledge and experience, without your usual story, say it is impossible for you? Or that you could do it as much as anyone else?
  - Just as we need to question the "bullshit propaganda" fed through the media, question the propaganda we feed ourselves: "I'm stuck in this job, I'm stuck in this relationship", stuck in a physical habit or a weight. Ask whether there is any evidence in the physical world for the limit
  - The real truth is "so much more awesome" than the story sold from the outside or the one sold from the inside
- **Story:** a Reddit post asked for testosterone supplements without side effects. Lots of men have searched for the best testosterone supplements, personally included
- Ask the right question. Most men probably care less about a lab number than about how they feel: erections, energy, strength, being lean. Raising testosterone matters, but the end goal is the feeling
- **Testosterone is built from habits and fuel, not supplements.** Some supplements are useful, but without the foundation no supplement can save you. Only with every foundation item done, and still wanting a boost, might supplements have a place. If you feel your testosterone is low, or have been told so, start with the foundation, not supplements, "and it's certainly not with testosterone therapy"
- Do the foundation consistently and it would be shocking to still have testosterone issues. Don't dismiss these items as too simple: simple things are powerful, positively when done and as a drag when not. In no particular order, all key:
  - **Water.** Its effect on how you feel, erections, energy and mind "cannot be understated". Many people may never have felt truly hydrated
    - Formula given to clients and people: body weight in pounds ÷ 2 + 20 = ounces a day. At 200 pounds that is 120 ounces, close to a gallon (128 ounces)
    - A gallon is a good target for most men. Very small men might get away with less. Men of 250–300 pounds or more may need more
    - Only after two to four weeks in a row at that amount do you know what being truly hydrated feels like
    - Beyond its effect on testosterone, water helps erections, strength, energy and staying lean
  - **Sleep.** Hormones are made primarily during sleep, so messing up sleep messes up hormones directly, even if every other item is right
    - Get enough: for most people something like 7–8 hours, around 7.5
    - Don't eat too close to bed. Digestion pulls blood and energy away from other areas, so the body works on food instead of hormones. "It'll crush your hormones, it'll crush your erections". Even the same junk, eaten three hours before bed, would be better. **Story:** years of eating junk right up to bedtime without noticing what it did
    - Avoid screens late at night: blue light hurts sleep quality, and better sleep quality means better hormone production
  - **Super clean eating.** Eat what grows: the produce section, healthy fats (macadamia nuts, coconut oil, red palm oil, avocado oil, olive oil), pasture-raised meat and wild cold-water fish. Anything beyond that, including fast food and takeout, is not what counts as super clean eating here, though takeout might not be the worst (personally, much worse has been eaten). Not necessarily for life, but it is how to know you support your body. You don't need a PhD to understand any of this, but you do need the commitment, and putting it into action is not necessarily easy. "We are our habits and our fuel"
    - Enough healthy fats, especially monounsaturated: avocado oil, olive oil and macadamia nuts. They are key building blocks for hormones. If you're really deficient in them, producing enough testosterone is hard
    - Enough carbs, as covered in earlier episodes. Low carb is a bigger discussion, but in short, going low carb is "a very dangerous thing" for testosterone, and "plenty of studies" show low carb and testosterone don't go well together. Get lean by doing everything else and grinding hard, not by cutting carbs. Healthy carbs means produce like potatoes, not crackers and bread
    - Avoid sugar and alcohol. Wanting better testosterone while still drinking is "not gonna happen". Stop drinking and testosterone will improve: "I guarantee it". Personally, there is possibly no worse habit for cancelling out the good ones, so cut back dramatically or stop
    - After drinking, the liver takes four to five days to fully detox the alcohol and basically stops detoxing anything else meanwhile. So beyond alcohol's depressive effect, toxins build up, even from regular moderate drinking. Drinking more is your choice, but recognize it will have an impact on your hormones and how you feel
    - **Story:** years of drinking, a lot in some of them, then a little, then a bit here and there. On any regular basis, it drags you down
  - **Exercise**
    - Every day
    - Strength, not cardio or long and slow. To boost testosterone, boost your strength: push hard, pull hard, squat hard
    - Sprints two to three times a week. "So many studies" show they boost testosterone, and the payoff is huge
    - Work the big muscles of the legs with squats and sprints. The more muscle fibers you activate, the bigger the hormonal response, and the big muscles hold the most fibers. Sprinting works everything "full bore". Raise the intensity
  - **Sex energy.** With everything else right but sex energy drained, testosterone may even test fine while you "feel like shit", a shadow of who you could be
    - Keys: stop jerking off to porn, ejaculate less often since each time "hits the reset button" on sexual energy, practice semen retention, edge, and move the energy through your body. For most men, that is a big ask: a change
    - Studies over one or two weeks of semen retention show testosterone boosts, though they don't fully cover the practice. The number goes up, but how you feel changes even more
  - **Mind and stress.** Stress hormones like cortisol, essentially negative hormones for the most part, serve a role, but we generally have too many of them. They have sort of an inverse relationship with testosterone and other feel-good hormones: the higher the cortisol, the lower the testosterone. Constant stress crushes hormone production and also affects goals like losing weight or better erections
    - Any kind of activity can help release stress, but the two big ways to change how stress works in you are meditation and being in the present
    - Being in the now sounds like just words until you feel it. Most people only get brief glimpses: flying downhill on a mountain bike, or moments in sex when everything else fades. Learning to get back there transforms your life. It is a constant journey of getting pulled out and coming back
    - Book: *The Power of Now* by Eckhart Tolle, audiobook or print. Not necessarily the only way to understand being in the now, but for a lot of people it helps: "I cannot recommend that book highly enough". **Story:** life before that book and life after it, with a completely shifted inner state
    - Don't write being in the now off as hollow. It is a huge thing, maybe one of the biggest, and the only way to see how big is to feel it
  - **Purpose.** The body responds to the needs you place on it: strength training signals a need for strength, and the body answers with more testosterone. A life purpose places the same kind of need
    - You do have a purpose, even if you think you don't. Without being in touch with it, or at least on the journey to find it, that need is not really placed
    - Being stuck in a job you hate and dread feels like the opposite of high testosterone
    - Live in an aggressive way: go after what you want, have a purpose, commit to it, and the body responds with a hormone boost
- Do the foundation and testosterone will be good and you will feel great. If you still don't, a couple of supplements can help. **Story:** a few supplements in personal use, for the brain and for erections. Without the fundamentals, though, there's no business expecting supplements to do the work
