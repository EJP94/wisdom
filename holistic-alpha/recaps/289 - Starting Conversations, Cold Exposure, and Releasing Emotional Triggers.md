> **TL;DR** — Talk to people around you, authentically and without expectations, even without confidence; get cold exposure, a mental-emotional reset and willpower trainer whose every little dose trains the brown-fat cold engine; and try releasing emotional trigger points by finding them as physical sensations.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 289, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- People don't often talk to those around them in public spaces like they used to, which some older listeners might see a little better. It depends to some degree on where you live, but it seems that in general people have kind of moved away from it a little bit, and it was made a lot worse "with all the scamdemic isolating people bullshit"
- Random conversations make us feel connected day to day, and bring friends, sometimes lifelong ones, and romantic partners. Humans never had Tinder or Bumble: they lived their life and talked to the people around them. We were not meant to swipe through people, friends or dates, and pick and choose, but to go out and interact
- **Talk to people.** An encouragement to talk to the people around you, maybe a little out of your comfort zone. Not feeling confident is okay: do it anyway. That moment of sort of courage will actually help you feel more confident. Semen retention, physical training and cold showers also build confidence from the inside out, but what it really comes down to is just talk to people. You will form friendships and, if dating, real connections. Life gets better when we talk to people
- Maybe find some opportunities this coming week to strike up conversations, with two suggestions:
  - Be authentically you
  - Have no expectations. If part of you thinks a date with the woman you're talking to would be awesome, don't think about that. Be in the moment with another human being, say something authentic, be present
- Example: in line at the grocery store, the person ahead buys the really good local pasture-raised eggs. Just comment, "oh, those are the best eggs, aren't they?" They might say "Yep" and go on, or it starts a conversation
- When not feeling confident or feeling down, it seems easy to fall into the trap of thinking we're the only one and everybody else has it figured out. Everybody's going through the human experience. Talk to somebody anyway
- It's a perfect time of year for **cold exposure**: for a lot of listeners, though not everywhere, it's getting into the coldest part of the year. Even a little has powerful physical, mental health and "drive to get shit done" benefits
- There are a lot of studies, and you can look up the science, on boosting the immune system and so on. Personally, the two biggest benefits are:
  - A huge mental reset and mental-emotional lift. Nothing ever found is really as powerful at shifting your state, other than maybe breath work, really amazing too but at times kind of more work to get started. Cold snaps us out of repetitive down kind of states
  - It gets us in touch with willpower: stepping into a little discomfort because it leads where I want to go. That is the same fundamental choice we need to make throughout the day to create the life we want, and cold showers are so effective for getting into that frame of mind
- Ways to get it: start your day with a cold shower; work out outside, or go to work and the store, in less clothes than you otherwise would. You don't need to always have a jacket on if it's cold: wear a t-shirt
- **The cold engine.** We have a cold engine, brown fat, trained basically the way a few push-ups dropped into the day train muscles. Every little bit trains it: a 30-second or 10-minute cold shower, an ice bath, shoveling your driveway in a t-shirt, walking from the car to work in a short-sleeve shirt. It helps keep you warm and adapt to cold
- Cold showers are one of the fundamental habits heard over and over, from men worked with in coaching, talked to or listening to the show, as one that sticks. Get over the hump: do it every day for a couple of weeks, three weeks, notice how much different you feel, and it sticks. Personally, so many men come to mind who can't imagine going back, because it can shift your mind, emotions and body. "It's so worth it, I promise"
- **Fascia** is basically a wrapper, almost a fabric, around our entire body. Tightness around a muscle after a workout is, in general, fascia tightness, and sometimes becomes a trigger point, a stuck spot you've felt if you train regularly: a bunching up where energy can't really flow and gets sort of stuck and stagnant
- **Emotional trigger points.** The same happens with emotions, because emotions are a physical thing that lives in our body
- Unlike our mind, the body never ever forgets. A two-year-old's trauma might still impact them 20, 40 or 60 years later, not because the mind remembers but because the body does
- From childhood on, experiences often create some kind of trauma, which is really just emotional energy, especially energy we can't process at the time, and it becomes stuck as a physical thing
- "Triggered" is kind of a common word today. When a message or conversation triggers you and you suddenly feel completely different, mentally and a lot of times physically, what really happens is that it triggers the physical stuckness of that emotion
- When we really heal something and completely move past it, we keep the memory but it loses its energetic charge, so it no longer brings up the emotions. That charge is a physical thing
- That stuck, stagnant stuff can be released. One method, encouraged as something to try: enter your body and find the emotional trigger as a physical thing. Say a text from your wife snaps you into anger, frustration, sadness, guilt or shame, and you feel it in your body. Suggested: search for it as a physical sensation and identify with it as one
- What seems to happen with a lot of this stuff when it gets stuck is that we lose the physical connection to it. A breakup, say, triggers you, fades, then comes back a day, a week or a month later, again and again, because we lose the physical connection with that stuck energy, and so it kind of hides in the body. However much we think or talk it through, if we don't physically reconnect with that emotional energy in the body, it stays stuck
- The idea is used in a type of therapy called **somatic experiencing**, which basically takes people through identifying emotions and experiences with their physical sensations. Identifying with them is what lets the charge be released. A really simple but really powerful method. The steps:
  - Recognize when you're being triggered. Without meditating, without the awareness to step back and observe your thoughts, you won't really recognize it, just be swept up. With a regular meditation practice you may notice it in the moment
  - Enter the body: bring your awareness into its physical sensations, a phrase that means more if you've meditated. Start by closing your eyes and feeling the weight of your body, your feet on the ground, your legs on the chair. The breath is a great gateway into the body's sensations
  - Identify the trigger as a physical thing. Sometimes it's in a particular part of the body, because certain trauma gets stored in a particular part. Or it may be generalized, almost cellular: then identify the nature of the feeling. Is it warm? What does it feel like? Let go of the thought side and connect to the physical thing
- An introduction, maybe for more depth at a future time. When something triggers you, sit, close your eyes, enter your body and really dive in. In the 1987 movie Inner Space, which some listeners weren't alive for, Dennis Quaid was shrunk into basically a little spaceship and injected into Martin Short. Suggested: almost imagine you're in that microscopic ship, venturing around your body. Can you find those emotions? Where are they? What do they feel like? Connecting to them physically can really release that charge. Experiment with it
- We all have stuff that triggers us emotionally, because we're all human beings
