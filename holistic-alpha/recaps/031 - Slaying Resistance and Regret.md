> **TL;DR** — Unfought resistance ends in regret, which is poison: let go of the past, ask what you'd regret at life's end, and take one tiny step toward it, a one-minute workout, five minutes of work, writing it down, to instantly shift the energy.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 31, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- Resistance and regret are the two dreaded R words. **Resistance** is part of our life and always will be; when we don't fight it, we end up with the pain of regret, which is absolute poison
- Number one with regret: let go of the past. Too many of us hang on to past failures and screw ups until they become sort of part of our identity. We can't do anything about the past, good or bad, not even relive the good stuff: work on living life today
- The key thing: what will you regret in the future? We're all going to die. Imagine repeating the major parts of today, or of the average day last week, for the next 70 years: would you be happy, or have regrets? Those are the things to change, the ones we have control over
- We often have goals, getting in shape, a better job, a business, a relationship, and sometimes they seem too big because we mentally try to do the whole thing at once, overnight. It doesn't happen that way: stuff takes time, a reality we can accept or not
- Accepting it, we benefit from a major fact: as soon as you take one step toward what you want to change, improve or add, even the tiniest, you've instantly punched resistance in the face, destroyed that regret and shifted your energy
- Imagine dying a week from now after an accident, on a hospital bed, as your life kind of flashes before you: maybe some awesome memories, and regret for what you didn't do. If you talk to older people around you, and as we all experience once we get some age behind us, we realize the things we don't do are the ones we'll really regret. So just do it: after one step, at least you tried
- The empowering thing: we don't have to accomplish huge changes before we start avoiding regret and getting sort of some energy transfer toward the goal. It starts the moment you take some kind of action. Doing it all at once is impossible, so the task seems impossible. Dial it back: one little step to shift the energy, then one more. That's the way changes happen
  - Haven't worked out in three weeks? Set a timer for 1 minute and do something for 1 minute: you got a workout in today that you didn't get in yesterday
  - A business: what activity do you need to do every day to build it? If days have gone by with zero minutes of it, do 5 minutes
  - A relationship to repair, with your spouse or whoever: talk for a couple minutes, say something a little uncomfortable, open up a little more
- Where we feel stuck, that's resistance kicking our ass. The best way to gain ground back is a little action: standing back up after he's been pounding on you and giving him a little jab to the face. That shifts things entirely
- **Story:** so far from perfect: so many years letting resistance win in so many ways, ground gained in some, still working on it. Life is a journey for all of us, and we can all start with one little bit of extra action
- If something you'd regret not doing has been eating at you, start by writing it down: get it into the physical world, even if you throw the paper away. Even better, make it a goal and read it every day: that kind of repetition is really powerful to dial things into our mind. Even if you do nothing with it, ever, writing down the change shifts the energy a little. Then, if it's a workout thing, do that one-minute workout tomorrow, or tonight before bed
- Probably too many of us live with too much regret all the time for what we're not doing, already feeling some of what will be really painful at the end of life. Weeks go by while something eats at you and you take no action: it feels shitty, we feel stuck and regret day to day. That's a fraction of the regret at the end of life if we didn't at least try
- Whatever change you need: a better job, switching industries, a business. If your relationship sucks, commit to fixing it, or if it can't be fixed, move on. If you've neglected being as much of a father as you could be because you've been working, take one little step, and you can instantly shift away from that regret
- This weekend, with the little extra time we probably mostly have, take a little action on something you've been stuck on: one minute, 30 seconds, five minutes. Right after this episode, write down the change, do push-ups for a minute, or write out how you'll change your diet
- You're stronger than you think you are
