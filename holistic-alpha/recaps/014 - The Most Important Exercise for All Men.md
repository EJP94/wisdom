*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 14, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Kegels** are the single most important exercise every man should be doing every day, without a doubt in this view
- You may have heard of Kegels more often in relation to women, or to preventing urinary leakage, but Kegels and reverse Kegels exercise the pelvic muscles, really our sex and life force muscles. The pelvic floor is in so many ways one of the sort of life force centers of our body and spirit, where one of the chakras essentially sits, and its state and condition is a fundamental driving power. Basically our sex energy core, and in a lot of ways our life force core, so exercising it is incredibly important
- **What they are**: imagine stopping your pee midstream and holding the muscles you'd engage: that's a Kegel. Imagine peeing harder midstream: a reverse Kegel. Two sides of the same coin, flexing those muscles in different ways. Of the couple of different muscles there, it takes a little time to dial in which one you're using, especially if you've never practiced. In edging or sex, which muscle you use and how, in either direction, has an incredible impact on your arousal level, your ability to control it, and whether it rises or drops
- Done on a regular basis, Kegels most definitely bring stronger erections, no matter how strong yours already are, much more control over your arousal, especially with reverse Kegels, which you absolutely should include, lasting longer and stronger orgasms
- A warning: don't do just Kegels without reverse Kegels. Balance both directions, seemingly equally: you get a lot of the sort of life force increase benefits from Kegels, but for controlling your arousal and lasting longer, in sex or edging alone, reverse Kegels are really, really powerful
- Even more, Kegels have a way of really firing up the drive and energy within us. Personally, precisely why is unknown: maybe a blood flow thing, maybe an energetic center, an energy exchange around our body. Bottom line: you feel it walking around in the world and with other people and women, in more overall confidence, drive and power
- Long term, as we get older, and maybe more depending on your current age, Kegels help ensure proper function of that whole area, including urinating without leakage. Most of us at 25, 30, 45 or 50 are probably not concerned about that now, but it's a nice long-term benefit
- **The prostate**: there's a lot of science around men who ejaculate at least at a certain frequency having a healthier prostate and less prostate enlargement, prostate cancer. The strong belief here: it's not actually the ejaculation causing that benefit, but the contractions of those muscles keeping the area strong and healthy. Like a bicep never used, your arm just hanging there, used maybe once every month or two, unused pelvic floor and sexual core muscles waste away: you might get urinary leakage, and it also affects the prostate, right in that area. So, a strong belief, Kegels lead to long-term prostate health
- In the short term, the major benefits, and seemingly the reason every man should do Kegels: the boost in life force energy, drive and confidence from truly strengthening that area, the way it feeds and channels into your body, and the incredible benefits to your sex life, whatever your relationship status, really overall a better experience. A better sex life, however it looks for you, feeds into every aspect of who you are, your life and your energy levels
- **How to practice**: a daily routine of a few different timings:
  - Moderate holds: hold maybe 5–10 seconds, release a couple seconds, maybe 5, and repeat, say, 5–10 times
  - Quick squeezes: squeeze for one second, release for one second, 10 to 30 of those
  - Longer holds: hold 20–30 seconds or longer, release for say 5 seconds, and repeat
  - Repeat those three timing groups on both the Kegel and the reverse Kegel side
- One of the beautiful things about Kegels: you can do them anywhere, while doing pretty much anything else, and nobody's going to have the slightest idea. Try it now. There's not really a lot to it: no extra time, just a willingness, a decision to do it
- **Story:** a tally app on the phone tracks certain key daily activities with tally marks, and Kegels are one of them. Pen and paper, an app or however you track stuff: track it and do it daily. With a little bit of effort, the initial benefits probably come within a day or two, and really significant ones within a week or two of practice
- One final recommendation: Stamena, an awesome app personally liked and used, on iOS and pretty sure on Android, though not 100% certain. It has you do both Kegels and reverse Kegels in those timings, progressively more challenging in length and repetitions
