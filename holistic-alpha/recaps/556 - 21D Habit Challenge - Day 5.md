> **TL;DR** — Scaling a habit back, even a lot, still keeps wiring the pattern, like Michael Jordan's unremarkable games that didn't stop him; show up, give what you have, count it as a win, and rethink the habit when you scale it back over and over.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 556, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Story:** in the 21-day habit challenge, the habit was cut the day before to 250 burpees from the target of 500, without feeling bad
- As came up in conversation, being okay with scaling back habits takes a mental and emotional adjustment. You can scale habits back, even a lot, and still get a lot of the benefit, because you keep wiring them as the pattern: maybe you normally meditate for 20 minutes and cut it to three
- **Story:** growing up a big Michael Jordan fan, grateful to have watched "an incredible competitor, certainly an imperfect human as we all are". He had plenty of games that were not noteworthy, like 16 points and five rebounds. Does anybody remember them? No. Did they stop him from becoming who he was meant to be? No. He showed up and, even when his output wasn't the same as the night before, kept demonstrating a pattern of showing up and giving what he had that day
- Same with your habits: scaling back won't stop your journey or your growth. It's okay; it keeps you marching forward. Show up and give what you have: if it's 500 burpees and you've got a hundred today, do a hundred, "Infinitely better than doing zero"
- **Story:** day 5, among lots of other self-care habits tracked: 500 burpees done, with burpee strength coming back (their own animal, a cardio-strength combination), meditation, and some amazing human connection
- There's zero need to feel bad about scaling back; recognize it as the win it is, doing something instead of notching a zero
- If you have to scale a habit back over and over again, something needs to change: maybe when you do it, where it fits in your day, what the habit is, or the time in your life. On occasional days, don't sweat it or beat yourself up
