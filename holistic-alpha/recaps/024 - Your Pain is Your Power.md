> **TL;DR** — Your pain is your power: a son's open heart surgery turned a miserable, out-of-shape life around, since hard things galvanize you and shift your perspective, so look at whatever pain you've had as there for a reason and transform it today.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 24, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- **Your pain is your power**: a fundamental truth that personally took a long time to learn, with a dramatic impact on our life. Wherever your greatest pain points are now, or have been in the past, that's the source of your strength: your challenges and pain make you stronger and make you who you are. In the midst of the pain it's not always easy to see: it's real easy to get overwhelmed and bummed out, and not see it for what it truly is, your strength
- **Story:** why helping men feel great and kick ass is such a passion: a pretty typical childhood; never an athlete, just kind of a skinny little kid. After high school, a job, marrying pretty young, and filling what seems to have been a lack of true purpose with negative things on the outside. The diet got worse and worse, with no idea what was being done, and no care either: more and more weight, weaker and weaker, more and more hurting, more miserable mentally and emotionally
- **Story:** there was a wife at the time, loved, and a daughter had pretty young (three amazing kids now), and parts of life were awesome, but being in such a shitty place internally and physically made it miserable. You've probably been there, "we've all been there", in some way, with sort of health, wellness and habits, not feeling good because of them
- **Story:** by 25 it felt like twice that age: the heaviest and most out of shape ever, unable to run a quarter mile to save one's life, literally, falling apart, mind and body a mess. Some half-hearted attempts at change, but nothing ever really stuck
- **Story:** then came one of the major events of a lifetime, which shifted things. Of the three kids, two girls and a boy, the son, Andrew, is in the middle. At nine days old he wasn't really seeming to do that great, wasn't eating a lot, and by the end of the day his lips were a shade blue, terrifying. At the Instacare his pulse oxygen was low; then the ER, and a couple of hours later the news: a couple of holes in his heart and what's called a narrowed aortic arch. He needed open heart surgery, and had it two days later
- **Story:** the job then was stock trader at a financial services company, trading for clients, a typical corporate gig: pretty good money, but absolutely miserable, probably part of what fed a negative life experience in a lot of ways. The wife and kids were awesome, but the job was hated, the body in terrible shape, life physically miserable
- **Story:** about the next three months were spent by his side in the ICU at the children's hospital, on a leave of absence from the stock trading job. So thankful, and so lucky to be near such amazing care: Andrew's surgeon, one of the top surgeons in the country, absolutely saved his life, and without him Andrew probably wouldn't be alive today
- **Story:** without question the hardest time of life to that point, and it changed something
- **Galvanization**: going through hard things makes us stronger, applying a force to produce a stronger outcome. It doesn't always feel like that, but it's true
- **Story:** it unleashed a new determination to live the life wanted. Until then there was seemingly the idea a lot of us have kind of as kids: not really seeing the finite nature of life, seeming to feel we'll live forever. Now came the realization: you're going to die. Do you want to die like this, in this condition, living life this way? The answer was no. What had been stop-and-start, half-hearted efforts completely transformed, during that experience and the months after
- **Story:** all that pain, so hard to deal with and like nothing dealt with before, needed somewhere to go: all the emotion, energy, sadness and heartache were poured into workouts, hill sprints at the time, literally crying while running them. It took years to really recognize what had been happening: all that pain and difficulty was coming on board as strength, a transformation into a stronger, better, more capable person who could attack and deal with life in a new way
- Hard, difficult things not only give us the experience of having gone through them: they shift our perspective. Getting through them, you realize you're stronger than you think you are, and sometimes it takes those kinds of events to make you really realize it. **Story:** a divorce not that long ago, for a long time a worst-case scenario in the mind that couldn't even be imagined; it was hard, but going through the time with Andrew, and years and years later the divorce, brought that realization
- Whatever pain you're experiencing, now or maybe in the past, serves a purpose: it builds you into the person you are
- **Story:** probably one of the reasons for the passion for helping men be manly, physical, strong and healthy is having been the opposite and felt the pain of that place: an alcoholic, fat and out of shape, completely emotionally and mentally lost. Now there's amazing gratitude for a completely different place: almost 40, 40 this year, for sure stronger and healthier than ever, and feeling younger really than ever as an adult
- It's not something special about one man: we can all do that. You can absolutely be the strongest, best version of you at 30 or 40 or 50 or 60. From personal experience of that pain: you can overcome and benefit from whatever pain you're going through, the same way
- **Story:** a lot of years went into living for nothing other than money in a job, miserable; now it's the opposite, nothing other than helping people. If only one person is helped to avoid or get out of that pain, that's enough. For men feeling the pain of being out of shape, hormones out of whack, without the life force and energy they want, the aim is to help them get out of that, to physically and energetically kicking ass: that's why the podcast and the work with clients
- The challenge: look at whatever challenges and pain you've gone through, or are going through now, the same way as the pain of Andrew's surgery. They're there for a reason, to create you into the person you are
- It seems everything is meant to be for a reason in life for us. With some things, like a family member getting cancer, it's real hard to see the reason why, and sometimes we won't ever really see it, but it seems everything does happen for a reason. Whatever challenges you've had in your life, body, health, success or living the life you want, that pain was there for a reason: shift your perspective, look at it as the power it is, and everything changes
- Today is the only day you have: you can't live tomorrow and you can't relive yesterday. Whatever pain is eating at you, transform it into a power by shifting your perspective and your energy, today. That pain is your power, even if you don't feel it yet
