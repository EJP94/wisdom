> **TL;DR** — Stress and anxiety dissolve when you find the quiet place inside, first by getting separation from your thoughts, then through four tools: 10 minutes a day of meditation with Headspace, cold showers, exercising with your eyes closed, and presence, helped by The Power of Now.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 92, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- It's good to train hard, to grind: there's a lot of comfort actually to be found in grinding hard. Get after it this weekend
- Stress, anxiety, negative or just overwhelming thoughts and emotions: it feels like we all struggle with this at one time or another. **Story:** the emergency room for a panic attack, and a long struggle with anxiety: days of drinking wine secretly at 9 a.m. for not being able to deal with it, of addiction to painkillers for a while, to masturbation and other things
- We seek escapes from the stress to cope with the hard stuff in our mind: drink, drugs, video games, golf, porn, masturbation. We don't have to, because there are tools that work. Four of them, which feel like the four most powerful known personally, end that cycle and bring peace, calm, presence and power
- Getting rid of stress and anxiety is really about finding the quiet place inside us: not so much getting rid of the things as seeking the light inside. The light washes away the dark: worries and anxieties dissolve when we're fully in that place
- You can be in that place in a conversation, and be the most connected with that person: in many ways it's about connecting, not isolating. A mind caught in anxiety or worry doesn't connect with a coworker or spouse the way a present one does, having quieted the mental storms a little
- Reducing stress is not just about feeling better or being more productive, although it does those too: you get in a flow state when you're not anxious. Our natural state is to feel good, to be ourselves, sincere and full of joy; sometimes things get in the way and obscure that. At a fundamental level, life is about connection, about the people we love: in a better place we connect more, our light shines brighter, and it affects everybody we touch
- Some of these techniques you may already do without realizing how beneficial they are. Practiced, they give such power: knowing that our emotions and thoughts don't have control over us
- In negative states, so stressed we can't eat, caught in anxious thoughts, depressed, stuck in regret, worried about a meeting or project, we sort of become the thought. There's no separation: we are depressed, angry, stressed
- Probably one of the biggest parts of reducing that cycle's impact is **getting some separation**. We cannot control all the thoughts that come into our mind; we can choose which to engage with more and which to just sort of release. But the first step to any influence over our thoughts is a little distance: being a sort of watching presence that sees thoughts and emotions come and go instead of being them. That alone makes such a difference. Eventually the thoughts get less and less and you spend more time fully present, but the first, so beneficial step is just to see it
- Technique number one, **meditation**. If you never try another thing from this podcast, maybe a couple, one for sure would be meditation: "If I could not get you to ever do anything else, start meditating"
  - Not Zen-level two hour long meditations: 10 minutes a day will change your life, "It honestly will"
  - Like being in love to somebody who's never been, it's impossible to describe: no words have the depth to fully explain how meditation will make you feel and interact with your mind, yourself and your world in fundamentally different ways. It just will
  - By far the favorite way is an app called Headspace. It teaches a really good, progressively growing, really consistent set of fundamentals, then you kind of choose your own path: an initial ten days if you've never tried it, then packs, like sadness, anxiety, loneliness or sports performance, twenty or thirty packs of ten to thirty days each, to dive into a particular aspect of your life. It's not the only way to meditate, but by far the best way ever found to start building the practice: it walks you down the path to the quiet place
  - **Story:** 17,000 minutes meditated in Headspace, "I think", and plenty outside it. You may use it for a really long time too, or for a while before starting your own practice
  - If you're not already meditating, download Headspace and commit 10 minutes a day for the next two or three weeks. By then you won't need to try to make it a habit: you'll look forward to it and feel the benefits. Start there, unless you already have some other method you're involved in
- Technique number two, **cold showers and cold exposure**. They are so powerful at resetting, including your mind. Personally, nothing shifts mental and physical state more than a cold shower: it gets you out of thoughts and mind loops and fully into the moment
  - It's good for your balls: cold showers boost your testosterone, "that's been shown". You also get a massive release of norepinephrine, a hormone and neurotransmitter, basically one of our feel-good hormones, from a cold shower, a cold plunge or even cold air exposure. An episode all on cold showers has more
  - Feeling stressed, depressed, flat or down? Take a cold shower. It's not easy or comfortable, but you will feel better after, "I guarantee it", and the impact is really powerful when it's a regular part of your life
  - A daily cold shower also builds your willingness to get uncomfortable, to face resistance head on. Cold will take you into that quiet place
- Technique number three, **exercising with your eyes closed**, which a lot of people have never even tried. Literally engaging your body in active ways with your eyes closed: a body weight flow of push-ups, yoga or squats, a kettlebell flow, a rowing machine, pull-ups. Grind physically, push your body, with your eyes closed
  - You've probably heard that when people lose a sense, the others sort of amp up: the brain has sort of the same amount of power to devote to the senses, so an unused sense's share gets kind of redirected
  - You get better results from your workout, through the mind-body connection. "There's so many studies around this": so much of our muscular strength is based on what's happening in our brain, not just the size of our muscles
  - You're forced to engage your balance and coordination in a different way
  - The focus here is the spiritual, mind-quieting power. With your eyes closed to the outer world, your body is a gateway: your mind starts to focus on sensation and comes into the feelings of your body. The quiet place is below thought, deeper than thought, and finding it and coming back to it regularly is how we dissolve stress. Moving with your eyes closed will help you find it, "I know it"
  - So many guys who were encouraged to try this say they're loving it and getting really amazing results
- Like meditation, these tools are not instant: two minutes of working out with your eyes closed won't end stress for life. They are tools to come back to regularly, again and again, to return to that calm, quiet, powerful place
- Even when you don't feel awesome, you are awesome. That place of power and joy is always there: we just gotta come back to it
- Technique number four, **presence**: being now. It can't be explained fully with words, it's about feeling into it. You have probably touched this place, probably just briefly: it's amazing how little time we actually spend present. **Story:** no idea how little clock time in life was spent really present, until a realization, then discovering some tools and making it a part of life
  - Right now, wherever you're listening, driving, at home, in bed, look around and feel into your physical surroundings. Can you see or touch the problems, worries, stresses, regrets, anxiety? You can't. They only exist when we are not in the now. Fully present, in a really powerful way, they dissolve
  - Worry and anxiety usually come from being in thoughts of the future: an overwhelming circumstance, a negative outcome, some failure we imagine. There we lose the connection with what's real, caught up in something we cannot cope with. We can only cope with right now
  - Being fully in the moment doesn't make next week's project or tomorrow's meeting go away, but it gives us the power to deal with them when they're there. How often have we been out to dinner with our family while mentally on that project, or on tomorrow's meeting with the boss?
  - Hours of talk won't get you closer: you've got to experience it. You might think you've felt it; it's very likely you haven't, or only for very, very brief times. Once you know how full presence and nowness feel, worries dissolve: that darkness cannot stand up to the light of presence
  - The one resource recommended above all others to feel that place: the book The Power of Now, by Eckhart Tolle, read or as an audiobook. It will impact your life and truly can be life shifting if you open your heart to it. Open your heart and your mind to the idea that presence can be powerful, and recognize that it has to be felt: before you ever had sex, could a description have made you understand it? No way
  - Meditation clearly brings presence, but we've got to carry it into the rest of the day, into moment-to-moment nowness
- Words on a podcast cannot do any of the four justice: they must be put into practice, and then, "I know you will feel the benefits. I know it in the bottom of my heart". These four ways will relieve some of the stress, anxiety and depression and help you tap into that quiet place inside you
