> **TL;DR** — Daily actions become a lifestyle and who you are; tallies in an app keep you on that path with daily to-dos and little wins, fit any area, and stay until a habit is second nature: consistency is the key to any real change.

*Ideas and stories from Holistic Alpha episode 191, as told by host Steven Mathis. Sources he credits are named where they appear. Not fact-checked.*

- What we do on a daily basis is one of the most important things, and seems absolutely critical, for us to grow and improve as men: the things we do daily are what we become. There's a huge difference between doing something a couple of times or a few times a week and doing it every single day. Done every day, it starts to really truly change who you are: it becomes more than a habit, a lifestyle, and that's when things really change
  - When physical training moves from a habit to a lifestyle, that's when your body and your health truly change. When a cold shower becomes every day, a lifestyle, it truly starts to change your mind, your willpower, your inner strength and your physical body as well
- **Tallies**: one of the best ways to not only track what you do every day but actually stay on the path of doing it every day. In Bootcamp (30-day group training) they are set up right at the very beginning, in an app, to track specific daily action items, because there is so much power in doing this
- An example with a couple of daily habits: maybe daily meditation (highly recommended if you're not already doing it) and a certain amount of water, maybe a gallon a day, which is a fantastic idea. Download a tally app and add a tally for meditation and one for water. Mark meditation off as you do it; tally water as you drink it through the day, with a target of maybe eight or twelve glasses, or by ounces
- It seems like such a simple thing, but it's so powerful and effective for keeping you on the path and consistent. Something heard from a lot of guys: trouble staying consistent with certain habits (working out, eating well, meditating), going kind of off and on, a few days in a row then off the path for a few days, or every day for a week or two then off for a month. If consistency is a challenge for you, the strong suggestion is to put tallies in an app to use for your key daily items, because a couple of things come into play:
  - Daily to-dos: you wake up in the morning and your tallies are there, the items you want to get done that day. Getting it out of your head and into a system is really powerful and beneficial
  - A little win every time you check one off. A goal like a gallon of water a day that just lives in your head is hard: sometimes you forget how much you've actually had, sometimes you forget to do it, and you don't necessarily get a lot of feedback through the day. Tallying, say, 12 glasses a day, each mark is a little psychological win, a little feedback, a little feeling of accomplishment, "boom, I got it done", and that little feeling is really impactful for keeping you doing what you've set the intention to do
- Tallies can't be recommended highly enough: they will very likely make a big impact in your life. Heard consistently, over and over, from the guys who have gone through Bootcamp: that part alone, setting up the tallies and tracking them every day, has been a big change for a lot of them and really impactful, because it keeps you on the path, gives you that feedback and keeps you doing the work every day
- Two apps to check out, with more out there but these seem kind of the best: on iPhone, Tally the Anything Tracker; on Android, Tally Counter. Download one today and add a couple of tallies
- It can really be adapted to anything you're working on:
  - A client has been working toward muscle-ups, so he works on pull-ups, L-sits and some other things: add a tally for each of those little individual items
  - Wim Hof breathing, really powerful (breathwork is so impactful in our body and mind; not talked about a lot on the show yet, more to come): a tally for Wim Hof breathing rounds, marked off after every round
  - Cold showers, push-ups, water, how many Kegel training sessions a day, sprints
  - Business: if you're building one, the items you need or want done every day to move it forward, like a certain number of sales calls, or, if you publish content, how many pieces you'll produce. It adapts to literally any area of your life
- If it's important to you and it needs to get done every day, add a tally for it, until it becomes completely part of your lifestyle. Cold showers, when you first start: highly suggested to add a tally, so they're part of that system. Eventually they might become second nature
  - **Story:** a cold shower every single day without fail, not a day in the last five-plus years without one, other than one or two times really, really sick for a day or two (rarely sick, but it happens to everybody occasionally) and a couple of times with a fever and no big desire for one. It's just so ingrained, part of identity, that it doesn't really need a tally
  - **Story:** tallies every single day, not a day going by without them, for the things that do need a tally to stay on track. Wim Hof breathing had been a little off and on, so it was recently added to the tallies, tracked every day, and it has already been really impactful in staying more on the path. Not perfect, just like anybody else: systems are needed to keep growing and progressing, and things like this get shared after being figured out in one's own life
- Determine the important things to focus on every single day, for your health, your wellness, your business, whatever it is: if it's important and needs to get done every day, make a tally for it. Guaranteed, it will help keep you on the path and keep you consistent, and consistency is the key to any real change
